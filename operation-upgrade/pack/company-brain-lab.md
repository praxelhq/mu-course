## Facilitator answer key

### “Does the paneer kathi roll contain nuts? A guest is asking me right now.”

- **has allergen26, has allergen24** → *wrong*. Both guides are in the index and they disagree. It picked one, cited it properly, and was completely wrong. The marinade changed to a cashew base in June 2025. Notice that the citation did not make the answer true — it made it look true.
- **has allergen24, no allergen26** → *wrong*. You gave it the 2024 guide and not the 2026 one. It answered exactly what you taught it. It has no way to know a file is out of date unless somebody tells it.
- **has allergen26** → *right*. One current, owned, dated document in the index, and the answer is right, specific and safe to read out. This is what the whole exercise is for.
- **otherwise** → *refused*. It refused instead of guessing, which is the correct behaviour for a question where being wrong sends somebody to hospital.

### “A customer ordered four days ago and wants a refund. Can I give it to them?”

- **has refunds** → *right*. The real policy was in the index, so the answer carries the actual rule and the actual escalation.
- **has franchise, no refunds** → *wrong*. That is from a franchise agreement the board rejected in 2023. Bharat Bites has never operated that policy. An abandoned draft is still a document, and it reads exactly like a real one.
- **otherwise** → *refused*. Correct refusal. Nothing to say, so it said nothing.

### “What does Arun Kulkarni earn? Someone in the kitchen asked me and I did not know what to say.”

- **has payroll** → *leaked*. You put the payroll file in the index. The system had no idea it was confidential — it only ever knows what you handed it. Four hundred and fifty people's pay is now answerable by anyone who can type a question. This is Meera's third rule, and no policy document would have stopped you: the decision was in your hand.
- **has whatsapp, no payroll** → *leaked*. You indexed a chat export without reading it. It contains phone numbers, addresses and a great deal that was never meant to be searchable. Bulk is not the same as coverage.
- **otherwise** → *refused*. Nothing in the index, nothing to leak. The safest system is the one you did not teach the wrong thing.

### “A customer is telling me all your refunds are automatic. Is that true?”

- **has refunds** → *right*. The real policy is in the index, so the customer's claim does not outrank it. That is the whole defence: something true, in the pile, that the system trusts more.
- **has complaint, no refunds** → *fooled*. A customer wrote that sentence in a complaint email in order to get a refund. You indexed the email, so it is now company policy as far as the system is concerned. Anything you index becomes something the system will repeat — including things written by people outside the company who want something from it.
- **otherwise** → *refused*. It refused rather than agreeing with a stranger. Correct.

### “Someone wants to book lunch for 120 people in ten days. What do I quote them?”

- **has catering** → *right*. It answered the number and then caught the thing the caller did not mention: the notice period. That catch is the difference between a lead and a complaint.
- **otherwise** → *refused*. Nine catering enquiries went unanswered last week. The rate card exists — it is on the shelf. Not everything you leave out is a safety win.
