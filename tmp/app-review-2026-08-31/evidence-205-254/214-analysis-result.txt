  - heading "Customer Need" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Does the idea solve a clear and meaningful customer problem?
  - paragraph: College students regularly face high textbook costs for materials they only use for four to five months. Sourcing reliable used copies from seniors is currently unorganized and time-consuming. However, many students already rely on free digital PDFs or cheap photocopied notes instead of physical originals.
  - heading "Market Opportunity" [level=2]
  - paragraph: How attractive does the opportunity appear based on the information provided?
  - paragraph: Gurugram hosts several large private universities and colleges with thousands of enrolled students. While the local addressable market is concentrated, scaling beyond a few campuses requires separate catalog management for different university curricula. The localized student density makes early on-ground marketing relatively straightforward.
  - heading "Competition" [level=2]
  - paragraph: What alternatives already exist and how difficult could it be to compete?
  - paragraph: Even without direct startup competitors listed, informal alternatives in Indian colleges are strong. Students frequently share pirated PDFs on WhatsApp, buy cheap pirated prints near campus, or borrow from seniors for free. Competing with free or extremely low-cost local workarounds will be a major hurdle.
  - heading "Business Model" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Does the basic business model make sense?
  - paragraph: Buying inventory upfront creates heavy cash-flow pressure before knowing exact course demand. You also face inventory obsolescence if professors change recommended editions or switch reading lists. Managing book condition, deposits, and semester-end returns will require heavy operational effort.
  - heading "Pricing" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Does the proposed price appear reasonable based on the customer and value described?
  - paragraph: Charging Rs 500 per book per semester may work for expensive management or foreign-edition technical textbooks. However, many standard Indian textbook editions retail between Rs 300 and Rs 600 new, making a flat Rs 500 rental fee unappealing. You will likely need tiered pricing based on the actual retail value of each book.
  - heading "Differentiation" [level=2]
  - paragraph: What could make the idea stand out?
  - paragraph: Textbook rental by itself is easily copied by campus bookstores and seniors. Speed of campus delivery and offering full semester book bundles could help set the service apart. Without formal campus partnerships or bundled study resources, student loyalty will remain low.
