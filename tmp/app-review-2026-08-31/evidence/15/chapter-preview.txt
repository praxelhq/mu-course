    - heading "One Rupee on a Saturday" [level=1]
    - paragraph: Delhi, 1963
    - paragraph: Saturdays belonged to Connaught Place. My father would press a single rupee coin into my palm on Friday night, and I would sleep with it under my pillow like it was a secret. In the morning my cousin Vinod and I took the bus from Karol Bagh, holding on to the pole with both hands because the driver believed corners were a personal challenge.
    - paragraph: The white colonnades felt enormous then. There was a man near the inner circle who sold roasted peanuts in newspaper cones, and a bookshop where the owner allowed us to read a comic if our hands were clean. One rupee bought peanuts, a bus ticket home, and once — memorably — a small plastic aeroplane that broke before we reached the gate.
    - paragraph: What I remember most is not what we bought. It is the walking. The smell of rain on old stone, the tailors calling out, my cousin's laugh bouncing off the arches. Delhi was smaller then, and so was I, and both of us were growing at the same time.
    - paragraph: My mother kept the broken aeroplane in a tin with the buttons. I found it again in 1998, after she was gone, and sat on the floor of her room for an hour holding a piece of plastic worth eight annas.
