2. Customer Size Matters


Who actually pays the bills?
Most of the startups in this list cater to small
businesses and everyday consumers. In fact, 70%
of the customer accounts in this dataset belong to
small users.
But here is the catch: those small users only
generate 15% of the total revenue.

The real money comes from "Enterprise" clients
(large corporations). Even though large corporations
make up just 8% of the customer base, they are
responsible for bringing in more than half of all the
money.

4. The Rush to Sell

Founders want to cash out.
Building and maintaining a software company is
exhausting work, especially for the hundreds of
smaller companies fighting for survival.
Because of this fatigue, a surprisingly high number
of startups are actively looking for someone to buy
them. Right now, over a quarter 26% of all the
businesses in this dataset are up for sale.

This creates a huge opportunity for investors to
swoop in, buy struggling software tools at a
discount, and improve them.
In Conclusion: The Playbook

    1. Look Past the Average
    Don't be fooled by high average revenue numbers. The reality is that the vast majority of startups are barely scraping by,
    while a select few make millions.




   2. Build for Big Business
   If you want a stable, profitable company, build software for medium and large corporations. They pay more, and they rarely
   cancel their subscriptions.




    3. Buy, Don't Build
    With 26% of these startups looking for a way out, the smartest move for an entrepreneur right now might be to buy an
    existing business rather than starting from scratch.
