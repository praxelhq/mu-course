Dataset Overview
What TrustMRR tracks, and the shape of the sample

TrustMRR lists real, revenue-verified indie and small SaaS startups. Each record captures:
Revenue & MRR — 30-day, 3-month, 12-month, all-time, plus growth %
Traction — active subscriptions, X followers, domain rating
Positioning — category, audience type, pricing model, persona
Marketplace status — whether for sale, asking price, revenue multiple

A Few Giants, a Long Tail
MRR is heavily right-skewed — mean is not a fair summary

Why it matters

   Mean MRR ($13,472) is ~48x the median ($282) — a handful of outliers (Stan, Brand On Demand) pull the average far
   above what a typical startup earns.
   19.7% of startups report exactly $0 MRR.
   21.6% report $0 in 30-day revenue.
   Median active subscriptions is just 16, vs. a mean of 501.
   Takeaway: report the median as the 'typical' startup, and use the mean only to describe total market value.

Key Takeaways
01
Skew, not scale

02
Volume ≠ value

Median MRR ($282) tells the real story of a typical startup — the mean is dominated by a handful of outliers.

AI has 3x more startups than any category, but E-comm and Analytics earn more per company on average.

03
Geography compounds

04
B2B pays more

The US leads on both count and average MRR — success isn't evenly spread globally.

Median B2B MRR ($1,036) is nearly 6x median B2C MRR ($181).

05
Followers ≠ revenue

06
Sellers are mid-market

X follower count barely correlates with MRR (r   ≈ 0.02) — audience size isn't a reliable revenue signal.
Startups for sale skew toward established-but-not-massive MRR — the true top performers aren't listed.
