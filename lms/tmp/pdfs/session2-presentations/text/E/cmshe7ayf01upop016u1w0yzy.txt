Quiet Money
Startup culture rewards noise. The revenue doesn't.

Across 1,000 startups on TrustMRR, the signals founders are told to chase — follower counts, the
hottest category, a public brand, a headline growth rate — show almost no relationship with what a
business actually earns. The companies quietly making real money often look nothing like what
“building in public” tells you to build.




Source: TrustMRR public listings dataset, 1,000 startups, frozen 30 Jul 2026
CLAIM 1 · AUDIENCE



A bigger following buys you almost nothing
Across all 1,000 startups, X follower count and monthly revenue are essentially uncorrelated (r = 0.02). Founders are told an audience is the moat. The data says: it
stops mattering almost immediately. Median revenue barely moves from 0 followers to 1,000 followers — and going from 1K to 10K+ followers doesn't buy you
more, it buys you slightly less.

 CLAIM 3 · GROWTH


 The fastest “growing” startups are usually the smallest

 One listing in this dataset reports 12,983% month-over-month MRR growth. It sounds like a rocket ship. It's a company that went from a few hundred dollars to
 $135K — a base-effect illusion, not momentum. Take the top 5% of startups by reported growth rate: their median MRR is $145. The rest of the dataset's median is
 $514 — over 3x higher. The steepest growth-rate headlines are coming from the smallest, least meaningful bases.

C L A I M 6 · VA L U AT I O N



Buyers don't believe the hype either
262 of these startups are listed for sale. The median price is $35,000, at a median multiple of just 2.3x monthly revenue. Venture investors pay 5–10x annual
revenue for durable SaaS. Buyers here are paying a fraction of that — because they already know what this whole deck has been showing: a follower count, a hot
category label, and a scary growth-rate screenshot don't make revenue durable. Buyers price the discount in. You should too.

THE VERDICT



The market doesn't reward noise. It rewards revenue.

1        Stop optimizing for followers.

         Audience size stops predicting revenue almost immediately — build the product, not the feed.


2        Don't chase the hottest label.

         AI has the most founders and a below-average payout. Crowded isn't the same as profitable.


3        Ignore your growth-rate screenshot.

         The fastest “growing” startups have the smallest bases. Check absolute revenue, not the percentage.


4        You don't need a public brand to win.

         The highest-earning listings in this dataset don't even have a name attached.


5        Sell to businesses, not crowds.

         B2B earns 6x more per company than B2C, with far less competition for attention.
