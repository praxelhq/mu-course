The story in brief: Stripe dominates the payment layer (60% of listings, 89% of tracked revenue). Revenue is extremely concentrated — a small number
of breakout products (Stan, Gojiberry, Rezi) carry the top line while the typical startup earns under $100/year. Social proof doesn't predict monetization,
tiered subscriptions are the default pricing motion, and e-commerce tools generate the highest average MRR of any category. Roughly 40% of context
fields (category, pricing, audience) are missing, so this dataset is best used as a sourcing funnel — not a precise valuation tool.

Revenue Distribution: Mean vs. Median




   Interpretation: Mean and median diverge enormously (12-month mean $28,124 vs. median $91) — a classic power-law distribution where a small number of
   breakout startups pull the average far above what a typical listing earns. Use the median to describe a "typical" startup on this marketplace; use the mean only
   when sizing total addressable revenue.                                                                                                                             4

Does Audience Size Predict Revenue?




Interpretation: Neither audience size nor site traffic reliably predicts revenue. X followers vs. MRR is essentially flat (log -log r = 0.12, explaining under 2% of variance).
And 80 startups combine top-quartile traffic with bottom-quartile revenue-per-visitor — real audiences that are heavily under-monetized, a easier fix than acquiring
new traffic from zero.
