01 · DATA PREPARATION


The file is usable — but 4 of 29 fields are too sparse to carry a thesis

■       1,000 unique record_ids, zero duplicates removed; 129 shared names are genuine anonymised listings ("Stealth Company", "Hidden Business"), kept but excluded
        from named call-outs.
■       Currency: every monetary field is already USD-denominated (revenue, MRR, asking price); no FX normalisation required. No negative money values found.
■       Sentinel strings (PRICING_NOT_FOUND, "[Private metric ... after login]") converted to missing — otherwise they contaminate every text-based segmentation.
■       revenue_multiple stored as text ("2.3x") → parsed to numeric; we recompute an independent multiple = asking price ÷ trailing-12M revenue and use that for
        valuation work.
■       Growth outliers: 7 records above +1,000% (max +7,900%). Winsorised at [-100%, +1,000%] for averages; medians used throughout as the headline statistic.
■       Payment provider is multi-valued for 13 startups; we analyse the primary (first-listed) provider and flag multi-provider stacks separately.
■       Free-text pricing_model (615 unique strings) and target_persona (603) normalised into 8 pricing archetypes and 13 persona clusters via keyword mapping.




     SO WHAT Treat revenue, MRR and subscriptions as decision-grade; treat traffic, revenue-per-visitor and stated multiples as directional only — they exist for a self-selected
     minority.

02 · EXECUTIVE SUMMARY


A barbell market: ten companies own the economics, 900 are experiments

     Biggest finding
                                                      ▪   Top 10% of startups hold 91% of all-time revenue and 82% of MRR; the bottom half holds 0.1%.
                                                      ▪   Median startup: $3,620 lifetime revenue, $282 MRR — this is a hobby-project distribution with a venture tail.



     Key patterns
                                                      ▪   Distribution beats product: revenue tracks subscriptions (ρ 0.90) and traffic (ρ 0.60) — not social following (ρ 0.23).
                                                      ▪   B2B monetises 5.7x better than B2C on median MRR ($1,036 vs $181) and 5x on ARPU ($49 vs $9).



     Surprises
                                                      ▪   Growth is uncorrelated with size (ρ 0.07) AND with asking price (r -0.01) — the market prices revenue, not momentum.
                                                      ▪   34% of startups are shrinking and 31% are exactly flat; only 66 (6.6%) grew >100% in 30 days.



     Largest opportunity
                                                      ▪   110 startups run >$3k MRR with almost no audience or SEO — acquirable cash flow invisible to the market.
                                                      ▪   Underpriced listings exist at <1x TTM revenue while the median ask is 4.8x.



     Key risks                                        ▪   Revenue quality: 12% of trading firms are one-time-revenue heavy and 34% are in decline; MRR growth is the least reliable field (11%
                                                          missing).
                                                      ▪   Concentration: a single company (Stan) is 23% of all-time revenue — remove it and the market halves.


        Investor takeaway: this is an acquisition market, not a venture market — buy proven small cash flows at 1–3x revenue and
                                                         consolidate distribution.

03 · DISTRIBUTIONS


Log-normal revenue, spiked-at-zero growth: two different businesses in one file




■       Revenue and MRR are log-normal across five orders of magnitude — the modal live startup earns hundreds of dollars a month, not thousands.
■       Growth is a spike at exactly 0% (305 firms) with fat tails both ways: 337 shrinking, 66 above +100%. Flat is the single most common outcome.

     SO WHAT Benchmark candidates against the P75 ($5.5k 30-day revenue) not the mean ($15.6k): the mean is one company away from being wrong.

07 · THE TEN MOST IMPORTANT INSIGHTS


If I were investing in this dataset, these are the patterns I would bet on

  1           Revenue is a top-decile phenomenon: 100 firms hold 91% of it — source narrow, not wide.


  2           Growth is free at the point of sale: multiples ignore momentum (r = -0.01).


  3           B2B out-earns B2C by 5.7x on MRR and 5.2x on ARPU, at the same build cost.


  4           Pricing architecture is worth 8x: paid subscription $1,024 median MRR vs freemium $126.


  5           Customers — not audience — drive revenue: subscriptions ρ 0.90, followers ρ 0.23.


  6           The median listed asset is a 33-month payback; only disciplined sub-2x buying beats a 24-month hurdle.


  7           62% of trading revenue is genuinely recurring — the asset class is real, but 12% is one-time and must be discounted.


  8           110 profitable-but-invisible firms have zero SEO or social footprint: acquirable upside sits in the unexploited channel.


  9           Category crowding (AI: 20% of firms) is inversely related to monetisation; the money is in Education, Marketing, Analytics.


 10           One company is 23% of the market — every dataset-level average is unusable; medians are the only honest benchmark.


          The bet: buy flat-to-growing B2B micro-SaaS at 1–3x trailing revenue, remove the free tier, and consolidate distribution.

APPENDIX


Methodology, assumptions and limitations

■       Source: single frozen extract of the TrustMRR universe, 1,000 rows × 29 fields, dated 30 July 2026. Pivot sheets in the workbook were ignored; all analysis re-derived from the
        raw 'Data' sheet.
■       All monetary values are treated as USD as labelled; no FX conversion or inflation adjustment applied.
■       Medians are the headline statistic throughout. Means are reported only where explicitly labelled and are winsorised for growth at [-100%, +1,000%].
■       Implied multiple = asking price ÷ trailing-12-month revenue, recomputed independently of the marketplace's stated 'x'. Listings with zero revenue are excluded from multiple
        statistics (n=243 of 262 usable).
■       Correlations are Spearman rank correlations, chosen because every monetary variable is heavily skewed; they measure monotonic association, not causation.
■       Category, persona, pricing and audience segmentation rely on free-text fields with 27–36% missingness; segment-level conclusions are directional and stated with n.
■       Traffic and revenue-per-visitor are self-reported by 17.8% of firms and are almost certainly upward-biased; no imputation was performed.
■       Company-level call-outs are screening candidates, not investment recommendations, and are subject to code, churn, contract and ownership diligence.
