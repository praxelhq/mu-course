THE THESIS




 Don’t build in AI.
 Build for a narrow B2B buyer — with AI as the tool, not
 the pitch.

  The category everyone rushes into is the category with the worst odds. The winners sell a specific workflow to a specific
  buyer — AI just happens to be how they built it.




                                                                                                                              17
T H E P L AY B O O K




Three Filters Before You Write Code

                    Pick a narrow B2B buyer
   1
                    Agencies, sales teams, ops — not “everyone.” Narrow buyers pay 5x more.




                    Treat AI as infrastructure
   2
                    It’s how you build, not what you sell. “AI-powered” is not a business model.




                    Check underbuilt categories first
   3
                    Education, Analytics & Marketing pay more per builder — with far less competition.

READ BEFORE YOU AC T




Risks & Caveats

     Small samples in niche categories

     Customer Support (n=5) and similar categories are directional signals, not statistical proof.




     Optional, self-reported fields

     Pricing, persona, and market tags are missing for many rows — category and revenue fields are the most complete.




     A snapshot, not a trend line

     Data is frozen at 30 Jul 2026. It shows what’s working now — not causation, not the future.

APPENDIX C



Methodology Notes

      Median, not mean

      Median MRR is used throughout — a handful of outlier winners (e.g. $494K MRR) would otherwise distort category comparisons.



      MRR per builder

      = total category MRR ÷ number of companies in that category. A market-size-normalized view of category productivity.



      Zero values included

      Companies with $0 MRR are counted, not excluded — this is what drives the “failure rate” comparisons.



      “B2B / B2C” field

      “Both” audience-type rows are excluded from the B2B-vs-B2C split slides to keep the comparison clean.
