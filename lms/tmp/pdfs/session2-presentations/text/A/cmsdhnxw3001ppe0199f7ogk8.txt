      What the data actually shows, in five findings



                  Revenue is a power-law market, not a bell curve.
       1          Median MRR is $282 vs a mean of $13,472 — a 48x gap. One listing (Stan, $3.6M MRR) alone exceeds the combined MRR of roughly the bottom          48x
                  950 startups.


                  Category hype and category economics have diverged.
       2          AI absorbs ~1 in 5 startups by volume but ranks 6th of 13 categories on average revenue per business. E-comm, Analytics & Education monetize   #6 of 13
                  2x+ better.


                  Business model beats product category.
       3          B2B businesses are outnumbered nearly 2:1 by B2C, yet earn 5.7x the median MRR — pricing power matters more than which vertical a founder       5.7x

                  "Growth" is not the default state of these businesses.
       4          Only ~34% of listings grew revenue in the trailing 30 days; almost identical shares shrank or sat flat. Most micro-SaaS plateaus rather than    34%

                  A real secondary market exists, priced well below traditional SaaS.

        How to read this analysis
        – This is a directory of mostly self-listed "indie hacker" micro-SaaS products — not a random sample of all startups, so it over-represents solo/small teams
          that publish revenue publicly.

        – Revenue figures are self-reported by founders via connected payment APIs — treat this as directional market structure, not audited financials.
        – The goal: understand how revenue is actually distributed, which categories monetize best, and what "for sale" pricing implies about typical multiples.
