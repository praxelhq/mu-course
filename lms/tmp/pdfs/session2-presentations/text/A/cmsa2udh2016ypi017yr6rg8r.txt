The Shape of the Market
A Pareto breakdown of revenue share by percentile reveals just how extreme the concentration is. The top 1% alone commands 61% of total revenue.
The top 5% control 80%. The bottom 80% of startups split the remaining sliver. This isn't gradual decay — it's a near-vertical drop from the elite tier
to everyone else.

   "If it's not on Stripe, it's probably not making real money yet."


The gap between Stripe's 64% startup share and 96% revenue share is stark: non-Stripe startups represent over a third of listings but account for
only 4% of total revenue. Stripe isn't just the dominant payment rail — it's a near-requirement for generating meaningful revenue in this ecosystem.

Key Takeaways
1   Revenue is winner-take-most
    Not evenly spread — the top 1% captures 61% of all revenue, and the top 10 out-earn the other 990 combined.



2   US + Stripe + B2B is the highest-probability combo
    This combination dominates the revenue charts across geography, payment rails, and business model.



3   Audience size is a vanity metric
    Traction metrics — MRR and subscriptions — are the real signal. Followers show almost zero correlation with revenue.



4   A fifth of the ecosystem is stalled
    20% earned $0 in the last 90 days; 10% have never earned a dollar. Worth investigating why.



5   Missing data is a signal, not noise
    Some of the best performers are the ones hiding — 131 stealth-listed startups, one of which made $11.3M alone.
