# Course 1 First-Draft Extract

Source: `Praxel_MU_Applied_AI_for_Business_First_Draft.docx`, first draft for discussion, June 2026.

This file preserves the Course 1 proposal as received. It is source material, not the approved course contract.

## Programme-level premise inherited by Course 1

- Students learn AI by operating one "Living Business" across the programme.
- Every session ends with something built.
- The durable method for Course 1 is the Operator's Loop: frame the problem and define success; decompose it; match parts to capabilities; choose and defend tools; execute and iterate; verify.
- The portfolio goes live in Course 1 and accumulates evidence throughout the programme.
- Assessment emphasizes artifacts, live demonstrations, and defence of decisions.

## Proposed Course 1

**Title:** AI for Business: The Operating Stack  
**Duration:** 20 hours, 10 sessions of 2 hours  
**Headline output:** Operating Snapshot and portfolio v1

The draft describes Course 1 as a broad, hands-on pass across research, AI fundamentals, documents and analysis, presentations, images, video and voice, connectors, and a first built page. Each use case is meant to land on the student's Living Business.

### Proposed assessment

| Component | Evidence | Weight |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Build Log | Artifacts shipped into the Living Business across all sessions | 30% |
| Capability Sprint | An unseen business problem solved live with the Operator's Loop | 25% |
| Operating Snapshot | Capstone deck and artifacts with a three-minute tool-choice defence | 35% |
| Participation and peer | Engagement and peer feedback | 10% |

## Proposed session sequence

### Session 1: Orientation, the stack, and setup

- Tooling: accounts and workspace setup across the core stack.
- Capability: map what AI can do for a business; introduce the Operator's Loop.
- Use case: assign and frame the Living Business.
- Build: business name and first logo concept in 20 minutes.
- Note: intentionally light because setup consumes time.

### Session 2: Prompting and research as an operating skill

- Tooling: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and deep research modes.
- Capabilities: structured prompting, deep research, source checking.
- Use case: sourced market brief for the Living Business.
- Build: research duel for the fastest credible answer to a live question.

### Session 3: Fundamentals as decisions

- Tooling: compare chat and reasoning tools side by side.
- Capabilities: models, tokens, context windows, RAG, and web app versus terminal versus IDE as choices rather than mechanics.
- Use case: choose the right setup for a business task and defend the choice.
- Build: run one prompt across three models and explain the differences.
- Note: Sessions 2 and 3 may form a four-hour evening block.

### Session 4: Documents, data, and analysis

- Tooling: Claude, ChatGPT, and spreadsheet AI.
- Capabilities: drafting, structured analysis, working with data.
- Use case: turn a messy dataset into a one-page decision memo.
- Build: rescue a poor spreadsheet in 15 minutes.

### Session 5: Presentations

- Tooling: NotebookLM, Gamma, Claude, Canva.
- Capability: build brand-aware decks quickly.
- Use case: investor-style one-page deck for the Living Business.
- Build: 30-minute deck with peer voting.

### Session 6: Images and design

- Tooling: current image models.
- Capabilities: image generation and business design fundamentals.
- Use case: brand visuals and hero image.
- Build: magazine cover or billboard concept.

### Session 7: Video, voice, and the jingle

- Tooling: current video models, ElevenLabs, Suno.
- Capabilities: short-form video, voiceover, music.
- Use case: 15-second product teaser.
- Build: ad jingle and spokesperson voice clip.

### Session 8: Connectors and workflow, orientation level

- Tooling: Make, Zapier, n8n, and connectors.
- Capability: understand connectors and what they enable.
- Use case: one guided automation for the Living Business.
- Build: a first automation that visibly performs work.
- Note: depth is reserved for Course 3.

### Session 9: Projects, working surfaces, and portfolio v1

- Tooling: Claude Projects, Custom GPTs, and a simple site builder or AI build partner.
- Capabilities: persistent context and a first built page.
- Use case: publish a personal portfolio with Course 1 artifacts.
- Build: the student's first live software surface.

### Session 10: Capstone showcase

- Format: present an Operating Snapshot of the Living Business with the accumulated work.
- Defence: three-minute justification of tool choices, scored on the rubric.
- Close: awards for most shipped, best jingle, and sharpest brief.
- Bridge: introduce Course 2.

## Questions intentionally left open

- The final Course 1 story and job-market promise.
- Whether every student uses an assigned Living Business, their own context, or a shared client/problem bank.
- Which artifacts deserve portfolio prominence versus remaining practice evidence.
- How Praxy hosts, verifies, and presents the student portfolio.
- The LMS interaction model, simulator catalogue, diagnostics, mastery checks, and telemetry.
- Delivery staffing, section consistency, tool provisioning, accessibility, and failure recovery.
