# Course 1 LMS Concept

**Course:** Masters' Union PGP — Applied AI for Business, Course 1  
**Scale:** 20 live hours, 480 students, eight sections  
**Product posture:** Platform-agnostic learning and evidence architecture  
**Status:** Concept contract for curriculum, simulation, assessment, and LMS design  
**Reference pattern reviewed:** Praxel Market Research Simulation (`mr.praxel.in`), July 2026

## Executive decision

The LMS should be an **AI Transformation Mission Room**, not a content library and not a gallery of polished AI outputs.

Every student defines a defensible **Target role × Representative work context × Consequential decision × Inspectable evidence** and accumulates one recruiter-readable evidence trail. Operator mode supports internal/customer decisions; advisor/investor mode supports client, deal, and investment decisions. The platform guides the learner through staged business missions, captures decisions before generation, injects realistic failures, provides rapid bounded feedback, and separates four different claims that are too often conflated:

1. **Completion:** the student submitted something.
2. **Artifact success:** the submitted system or deliverable works against stated criteria.
3. **Student ownership:** the student can inspect, diagnose, modify, and defend the AI-assisted work.
4. **Transfer:** the student can apply the capability to an unseen but equivalent business situation.

Only the last three should contribute materially to mastery. XP, badges, streaks, and leaderboards may motivate practice, but must never stand in for learning evidence.

The system must work for nine career streams without becoming nine separate courses:

1. Consulting
2. FOCOS — Founders' Office, General Management, Chief of Staff, Strategy
3. Product Management
4. Finance — Investment Banking, Venture Capital, Corporate Finance
5. Human Resources
6. Supply Chain and Operations
7. Sales
8. Marketing
9. Data

The scalable answer is one common capability spine, a common mission grammar, and stream-specific scenario overlays, datasets, stakeholders, quality bars, and portfolio framing.

## What to preserve and improve from the Market Research Simulator

The reviewed simulator establishes a strong pattern: an end-to-end consulting narrative, eight locked stages, persistent student context, AI stakeholder guidance, structured submissions, AI-simulated interviews and focus groups, immediate feedback, XP, badges, a mandatory bias checkpoint, an analysis lab, reporting, and an executive presentation. It makes invisible professional work visible and turns a method into a sequence of consequential decisions.

Course 1 should preserve:

- a coherent business story rather than disconnected exercises;
- staged progression with explicit prerequisites and unlocks;
- AI personas who have goals, constraints, and memory;
- structured workspaces instead of generic file upload;
- immediate, actionable feedback and redo opportunities;
- checkpoints that interrupt confirmation bias;
- an analysis workbench that exposes evidence, not just conclusions;
- a final executive defence;
- persistent context and a visible record of growth.

Course 1 should improve the pattern in six ways:

1. **Unlock on evidence, not a model's holistic “high quality” label.** Mechanical requirements and critical safety gates are deterministic; judgment scores are rubric-bound and auditable.
2. **Make predictions precede assistance.** Students state the expected outcome, choice, or risk before AI generates or acts.
3. **Separate coaching from grading.** The coach can be generous and iterative; the assessor sees a frozen submission and cannot reveal hidden tests.
4. **Prove ownership.** Clean-room reruns, unseen modifications, failure diagnosis, and sampled oral defence complement the polished artifact.
5. **Support true case variability.** Equivalent case variants, seeded defects, and stream overlays reduce copying while preserving fairness.
6. **Design graceful degradation.** Every core mission has a printable/classroom version, cached case materials, deterministic feedback cues, and a facilitator override.

## Product principles

### 1. Evidence before elegance

The public portfolio can be polished. The assessment record must preserve the original brief, predictions, sources, versions, execution traces, test results, failures, revisions, and defence. A screenshot is not proof that a workflow ran, an app was used, or a source supports a claim.

### 2. One mechanism per mission

Each lesson should make one controllable mechanism visible: source quality, retrieval, structured output, trigger/branch logic, human approval, observability, user behavior, or multimodal consistency. Tool menus are secondary.

### 3. Assistance fades; constraints increase

Early missions provide examples, starter assets, schemas, and hints. Later missions remove scaffolds, change the surface context, and inject failures. The final transfer mission is unseen.

### 4. Common spine, relevant skins

All students learn the same durable capabilities and face equivalent evidence requirements. Stream and company relevance enters through business context, stakeholder roles, data, constraints, and the interpretation of value.

### 5. AI judgment is contestable

Any AI-generated critique cites the exact student evidence and rubric criterion. Students may challenge a judgment. High-stakes scores require deterministic evidence, calibrated human review, or both.

### 6. Publish only what is safe and defensible

Private assessment evidence and public portfolio evidence are separate views. Publication gates scan for secrets and personal data, verify disclosure and consent, test links, and require an independent-project disclaimer.

## The experience architecture

The course operates as three studios supported by two persistent rails:

| Layer | Student question | LMS responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intelligence Studio | Can I make AI know and reason from the right evidence? | Research workspace, extraction/data lab, source ledger, RAG tests, consent-safe persona or clone sandbox |
| Systems Studio | Can I make AI repeat a method and act reliably? | Skill builder, clean-session tests, workflow sandbox, event simulator, logs, failure injection, approval gates |
| Operating Prototype Studio | Can I choose and make the role-authentic system people can test and understand? | App/workflow/assistant/dashboard/model/data-surface shells, replay/deployment checks, task-attempt exchange, feedback, creative lab |
| Evidence Rail | Can another person inspect and reproduce my work? | Git/repository checkpoints, provenance, version history, validators, test evidence, portfolio proof view |
| Growth Rail | Am I more capable on an unseen task? | Pre/post maturity missions, retrieval checks, confidence calibration, mastery map, transfer assessment |

The LMS does not have to host every external creation tool. It must own the **learning contract** around those tools: brief, prediction, structured inputs, evidence capture, validation, feedback, retries, and publication.

## Student flow

### Before the course

1. **Identity and access check**
   - verify roster, section, accommodations, required accounts, repository access, and supported browser/device;
   - run a no-grade technology check with a downloadable fallback pack;
   - teach safe credential handling before any connector is used.

2. **AI maturity baseline**
   - objective literacy items;
   - a 25–35 minute messy business performance mission;
   - confidence recorded after consequential decisions;
   - a short self-report used for support, never certification.

3. **Role, work-context, and stream selection**
   - choose one of the nine streams;
   - nominate a target employer/sector, role, operator or advisor/investor mode, decision owner, consequential decision, and role-authentic output;
   - complete a feasibility screen: public, licensed, course-supplied synthetic, or explicitly consented evidence as labelled; ethical boundaries; testability; measurable outcome; and supported prototype fit;
   - receive approval, revision, or a curated fallback brief.

4. **Portfolio workspace creation**
   - create the persistent transformation brief, evidence ledger, decision log, repo link, disclosure status, and private/public views;
   - establish the independent speculative project disclaimer.

### The recurring lesson loop

Every live lesson uses the same interaction grammar:

1. **Brief:** receive a business tension, stakeholder request, and quality bar.
2. **Commit:** predict the result or record a decision before tools unlock.
3. **See:** observe a short instructor demonstration of the mechanism.
4. **Build:** work in a scaffolded environment or supported external tool.
5. **Submit evidence:** provide a structured artifact, link, log, or execution trace.
6. **Validate:** run deterministic checks and receive rubric-bound coaching.
7. **Break:** face a seeded failure, changed brief, hostile input, or conflicting source.
8. **Repair:** diagnose the failing layer, revise, and rerun.
9. **Defend:** answer one short reasoning prompt or a sampled oral question.
10. **Ship:** add the evidence increment to the private portfolio; publish only after safety gates.
11. **Retrieve:** complete a two- to four-item lesson retrieval check after a delay.

### Between lessons

- short retrieval quiz and one misconception repair;
- checkpoint completion or redo, not open-ended “homework polish”;
- peer adversarial test assigned by the LMS;
- asynchronous facilitator clinic triggered by telemetry, not self-selection alone;
- optional advanced challenge that does not change core grades.

### End of course

1. Freeze the decision evidence brief, AI operating prototype, and recruiter case page/proof pack.
2. Complete a parallel unseen maturity mission.
3. Perform a clean-room modification with reduced scaffolding.
4. Defend one randomly selected claim, component, failure, or design trade-off.
5. Pass the privacy, consent, secret, provenance, and link-health publication gate.
6. Publish a recruiter view and retain a richer private proof view.
7. Receive a capability transcript showing verified evidence and growth—not only a completion certificate.

## Instructor and facilitator flow

### Before delivery

- choose and freeze the supported tool paths and fallback paths;
- assign section-specific case variants using an equivalence blueprint;
- preview every student-facing asset and run all validators against pass, partial, fail, and unsafe test cases;
- calibrate facilitators on anchor examples at each rubric level;
- confirm accounts, credits, rate limits, datasets, consent-cleared assets, and accessibility alternatives;
- publish only the pre-work needed for the next mission;
- rehearse a fully offline 30-minute continuation plan for each lesson.

### During a section

The facilitator dashboard should prioritize action, not surveillance. It shows:

- readiness: access and pre-work exceptions;
- progress: students stuck before or after each gate;
- misconception heat map by capability and rubric criterion;
- system health: model, validator, deployment, and connector failures separated from learner errors;
- integrity signals requiring review, never automatic accusation;
- students needing accessibility or language support;
- exemplary and interesting failure cases for live debrief;
- primary-scoring queue with frozen bounded packets, SLA and assigned human scorer;
- separate moderation/double-score queue for flags, appeals, drift and the stratified reliability sample;
- section pacing against the common run-of-show.

Facilitators can reveal a hint, extend time for an accommodation, approve an alternative evidence form, reset a mission, override a false-negative gate with a reason, or switch the whole section to fallback mode. Overrides are logged for later calibration.

### After a section

- review unresolved scoring disputes and AI-feedback flags;
- complete trained-human primary scoring for every judgment-bearing frozen packet;
- moderate all flags, borderlines and appeals, and blind-double-score at least 10% of every major component across sections/streams/bands;
- tag confusing prompts, broken data, model drift, and timing bottlenecks;
- record the 24-hour student pulse and compare it with behavioral evidence;
- publish a short facilitator note for the next section without leaking hidden tests;
- rerun cross-section consistency checks before grades are released.

### Program-team flow

The program team receives operational dashboards, not student prompt transcripts by default. It can manage roster, accommodations, deadlines, communications, case versions, manual-review queues, incident status, and feedback exports. Curriculum owners see item quality, simulator failure patterns, learning growth, and version history.

## Evaluation architecture

### Division of labor

| Evidence type | Deterministic system | Rubric-bound AI | Human / calibrated peer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Required fields, file structure, valid links | Primary | None | Exceptions only |
| Citation existence, source liveness, claim-source linkage | Primary for presence/liveness | Support-strength suggestion | Sample consequential claims |
| Dataset schema, calculations, hidden test outputs | Primary | Explain likely error | Ambiguous analytical choices |
| Controlled RAG lab or grounded prototype: answerability, citation match, abstention, retrieval traces | Primary where tests are specified | Failure classification | Safety and contested-source judgment |
| Prototype method layer: triggers, output schema, known/hidden behavior | Primary | Qualitative diagnosis | Novel method quality |
| Prototype execution layer: branches, retries, approvals, logs | Primary | Log summarization | Business boundary and escalation quality |
| Prototype availability/replay, core task, event receipt, assigned task attempts | Primary | Interface/pattern critique | User value and prioritization |
| Role-authentic communication technical requirements and disclosure | Primary | Coherence/persuasion critique | Strategy and sensitive claims |
| Decision evidence brief structure and claim ledger | Primary for evidence presence | Argument/counterargument critique | High-stakes factual and strategic judgment |
| Oral defence | None | Question generation only | Primary |

AI feedback never produces a final criterion grade. It returns criterion-level observations with citations to the student's evidence and a calibrated confidence. A trained human is the primary scorer for every judgment-bearing graded packet; low confidence, safety issues, scoring disputes, boundary cases and sampled reliability checks enter a separate moderation queue.

### Mastery states

- **Not attempted** — no usable evidence.
- **Developing** — scaffolded attempt exists but fails one or more essential criteria.
- **Demonstrated** — artifact passes the known contract and the learner explains the key mechanism.
- **Verified** — passes an unseen variant, failure-repair task, or clean-room test.
- **Transferable** — applies the capability in the parallel final mission with reduced assistance.

Completion badges may be earned at Demonstrated. Public capability verification requires Verified. The final capability transcript distinguishes the two.

### Scoring rules

- publish criterion-level scores and evidence, not one mysterious quality number;
- essential safety or evidence criteria can cap the artifact score;
- deterministic failures distinguish learner error from platform incident;
- model-generated feedback can recommend, not silently change, a score;
- retries replace the prior learning state but preserve attempt history;
- late policy, accommodations, and incident relief are applied by rules, not facilitator whim;
- cross-section moderation uses common anchor submissions and a shared sampling plan.

## Simulation shells

These are reusable interaction engines, not single cases. Each shell accepts a stream/pathway overlay and the canonical Work Sample Brief plus content-pack version.

### Shell A — Evidence Under Pressure

**Target capability:** frame a decision, find and evaluate sources, extract data, distinguish fact from inference, and revise a thesis.  
**Learner role:** analyst advising a named decision-maker.  
**Pressure:** incomplete evidence, one stale source, one fabricated or unsupported statistic, conflicting time periods, and a deadline.  
**Actions:** define question, rank sources, inspect evidence, extract structured facts, calculate or chart, record claims, recommend.  
**Injected failure:** a late primary source contradicts the initial story.  
**Evidence:** pre-data thesis, source plan, raw snapshot, data dictionary, analysis, claim ledger, changed-mind note, decision memo.  
**Deterministic scoring:** source types opened, claim links, arithmetic checks, hidden false-statistic rejection, required counter-hypothesis.  
**Judgment review:** causal humility, decision usefulness, counter-evidence.  
**Low-tech version:** printed source cards, spreadsheet extract, claim-ledger worksheet, timed evidence auction.

### Shell B — Controlled Grounding Lab / Optional Knowledge Interface

**Target capability:** context engineering, RAG, answer boundaries, evaluation, voice/identity safeguards.  
**Learner role:** owner of a disclosed role/evidence interface using a fictional, consenting, historical/public-domain, or non-imitative knowledge context. This shell teaches a common mechanism but is not a universal public portfolio requirement.  
**Pressure:** corpus gaps, conflicting dated sources, prompt injection, unsupported opinion request, latency, and escalation.  
**Actions:** curate corpus, define persona and non-goals, test retrieval, add a contradictory update, configure abstention and disclosure, optionally add voice.  
**Evidence:** corpus manifest, consent status, held-out test set, retrieval trace, failures, revised instruction, disclosure.  
**Deterministic scoring:** answerable/unanswerable cases, required citations, updated-source precedence, forbidden identity actions, disclosure.  
**Judgment review:** trust design, corpus quality, escalation boundary.  
**Low-tech version:** document packets, retrieval cards, facilitator as generator, learner chooses context and answer policy.

### Shell C — Prototype Method Layer

**Target capability:** turn tacit judgment into portable, testable agent behavior.  
**Learner role:** operator packaging a recurring method for a team.  
**Pressure:** vague request, messy input, false-positive trigger, missing information, and a changed requirement.  
**Actions:** specify trigger/non-trigger, input contract, steps, resources, output schema, checks, and test suite.  
**Evidence:** skill package, clean-session peer run, known tests, hidden tests, false-positive result, revision log.  
**Deterministic scoring:** package structure, schema, trigger cases, output properties, prohibited over-trigger.  
**Judgment review:** quality of codified judgment, scope, usefulness.  
**Low-tech version:** instruction cards handed to another student who executes the method literally.

### Shell D — Prototype Execution Layer / Workflow Control Room

**Target capability:** event-driven automation, human/AI allocation, structured data, branching, approval, retry, and observability.  
**Learner role:** process owner on call for an operating workflow.  
**Pressure:** malformed record, duplicate event, service timeout, policy exception, low-confidence model output, and consequential external action.  
**Actions:** map trigger and states, decide deterministic versus model steps, set permissions, run, approve/reject, diagnose logs, repair and replay.  
**Evidence:** workflow map/export, run history, approval record, failure injection, fallback, owner runbook, value metric.  
**Deterministic scoring:** event accepted, branches reached, idempotency, retry/fallback, human gate, no secret exposure.  
**Judgment review:** automation boundary, escalation, value and risk.  
**Low-tech version:** students route event cards through role stations while the facilitator introduces exceptions.

### Shell E — Role-Authentic Prototype and Task-Attempt Lab

**Target capability:** frame a narrow role job, select the appropriate prototype form, direct AI-assisted building, deploy or make replayable, instrument/log, observe, and prioritize.  
**Learner role:** accountable operator, advisor, or investor shipping an unofficial work sample. Valid forms include app, workflow, grounded assistant, dashboard/model, data pipeline, or a supported combination.  
**Pressure:** limited build time, one accessibility constraint, user confusion, invalid input, and sparse behavioral evidence.  
**Actions:** define work job and acceptance test, choose the smallest authentic form, build/deploy or make replayable, instrument/log the core task, complete LMS-assigned role-card attempts, observe, decide a release.  
**Evidence:** brief, acceptance tests, deployment/replay package, event/log schema, three to five qualified task attempts, observed session, issue/release trail.  
**Deterministic scoring:** live route, core task completion, error state, required events, qualified-use criteria, secret/PII scan.  
**Judgment review:** problem selection, evidence-led prioritization, user value.  
**Low-tech version:** clickable/paper prototype, structured usability tasks, facilitator-run event log.

### Shell F — Role-Authentic Multimodal Communication Lab

**Target capability:** turn evidence into a creative brief, direct images/voice/video consistently, disclose synthetic media, and test persuasion.  
**Learner role:** campaign, sales, product, change, executive-communication, training, or analytical-storytelling lead. It is a universal studio experience but a public graded film only where role-authentic.  
**Pressure:** locked brand constraint, data claim requiring support, synthetic-media disclosure, target-audience trade-off, and variant choice.  
**Actions:** define audience and action, storyboard, generate asset system, produce variants, run brand and claim audit, gather target-user response.  
**Evidence:** insight-to-brief trace, storyboard, asset/model ledger, rights/consent status, variants, response evidence, final cut.  
**Deterministic scoring:** duration/format, required disclosure, asset ledger, brand constraints, linked support for claims.  
**Judgment review:** strategic coherence, persuasion, originality of direction.  
**Low-tech version:** storyboard and asset-card pitch with blind audience voting tied to the brief.

### Shell G — Executive Defence Arena

**Target capability:** explain business value, evidence, limitations, and system behavior under challenge.  
**Learner role:** candidate presenting to a hiring panel or executive committee.  
**Pressure:** randomly selected claim/component, changed constraint, skeptical functional stakeholder, and strict time.  
**Actions:** present, inspect live evidence, diagnose, modify or decline, quantify value, state risk and next step.  
**Evidence:** question seed, response, referenced proof, modification result, reviewer notes.  
**Deterministic scoring:** time, required evidence opened, modification/test result.  
**Human scoring:** ownership, judgment, clarity, honesty, transfer.  
**Low-tech version:** calibrated viva cards and a two-person panel.

## Nine stream overlays

The same shells are reskinned with stream-authentic decisions. Samples must cover all nine streams in demos, practice banks, and portfolio exemplars.

| Stream | Anchor mission | Candidate company problem | Stakeholders / AI personas | Seeded tension and ownership test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consulting | Build and defend an AI transformation recommendation | Reduce service cost without degrading customer outcomes | client CEO, operations head, skeptical frontline manager | client data contradicts the fashionable recommendation; adapt the workflow and quantify trade-off |
| FOCOS | Prepare an operating decision for the founder/CEO | Choose which growth or operating bottleneck to address first | founder, finance lead, functional heads | two priorities compete for one budget; defend sequencing and escalation rights |
| Product Management | Ship and instrument a narrow AI feature | Improve discovery, onboarding, support, or retention | user personas, engineering lead, trust/safety lead | impressive prototype fails a core user task; diagnose event evidence and cut scope |
| Finance | Produce an investment, transaction, or capital-allocation case | diligence a target, screen a deal, forecast a decision, or monitor covenants | IC partner/CFO, management, legal/risk | model uses a wrong period or invented comparable; trace, correct, and restate the recommendation |
| HR | Redesign a talent workflow with accountable human decisions | screening, onboarding, learning, engagement, or workforce planning | candidate, employee, recruiter, manager, legal/DEI reviewer | proxy bias and sensitive data appear; detect, remove, and design appeal/human review |
| Supply Chain & Operations | Operate an exception-handling workflow | stockout, delay, quality incident, demand mismatch, or service recovery | planner, supplier, warehouse lead, customer | duplicate event plus supplier timeout; prove idempotency, fallback, and human gate |
| Sales | Build an evidence-grounded revenue workflow | account research, qualification, proposal, follow-up, or pipeline risk | buyer personas, sales manager, legal, customer success | prompt injection or stale CRM data triggers a risky message; stop, verify, and recover |
| Marketing | Create and test an evidence-led campaign system | launch, reposition, personalize, or improve conversion | consumer personas, brand lead, media lead, legal | winning creative violates a claim/brand constraint; repair without losing the strategic insight |
| Data | Turn messy data into a monitored business decision | metric definition, segmentation, anomaly, forecast, or experiment | business owner, analyst, data steward | leakage/outlier/definition shift creates a persuasive false result; find it and update decision |

Every overlay includes a shared core dataset plus optional company-specific public evidence. This preserves comparability while allowing recruiter relevance.

## Quiz and assessment system

### Assessment stack

| Layer | Purpose | Frequency | Stakes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technology check | Prevent access failure from masquerading as low ability | once before course | none |
| Maturity baseline | Diagnose starting capability and confidence calibration | pre-course | low; completion required |
| Entry ticket | Retrieve prerequisite needed for the upcoming mission | before selected lessons | low |
| In-lesson checks | Catch misconceptions before the build compounds them | one to three per lesson | formative |
| Delayed retrieval | Strengthen and measure retention | 24–72 hours after lesson | low |
| Mission evidence | Demonstrate applied capability | every lesson/studio milestone | graded |
| Clean-room / hidden test | Prove ownership and robustness | after major artifacts | graded |
| Peer adversarial review | Expose failure and improve feedback skill | selected missions | graded for review quality, not peer-assigned artifact score |
| Post maturity mission | Measure transfer on parallel unseen work | end of course | summative |
| Executive defence | Verify ownership and business judgment | final plus sampled checkpoints | summative |

### Item design

Retrieval quizzes should use short business scenarios, output critique, tool-choice reasoning, source verification, error diagnosis, and risk boundaries. Tool-interface trivia is prohibited.

Every item stores:

- outcome/capability ID;
- construct and cognitive demand;
- prompt and response type;
- correct answer or scored properties;
- rationale;
- why each distractor is plausible and wrong;
- difficulty and estimated time;
- feedback after response;
- form/equivalence group;
- staleness flag and review date;
- accessibility notes;
- pilot statistics after use.

### Retrieval policy

- two to four items per lesson, normally under five minutes;
- one immediate retry after explanatory feedback using an equivalent item, not the same wording;
- for each three-item lesson bank, 3/3 is ready, 2/3 triggers targeted repair before Form B, and 0–1/3 triggers facilitator support plus repair; 90% across cumulative delayed checks indicates retrieval mastery;
- quiz scores cannot compensate for failed applied evidence;
- repeated low performance triggers a targeted repair card and facilitator signal, not public ranking;
- questions about current vendors or model features are isolated in a volatile bank and date-stamped.

### Pre/post AI maturity assessment

The maturity benchmark measures ten capabilities:

1. frame a business outcome and quality bar;
2. allocate work between human, model, deterministic logic, and accountable approver;
3. create, structure, and manage context;
4. research, extract, and reason with evidence and data;
5. design a reusable skill or workflow;
6. evaluate outputs and diagnose failure by layer;
7. ship, instrument, and iterate from behavior;
8. handle privacy, consent, security, bias, and business risk;
9. communicate value, limitations, and next action;
10. calibrate confidence.

It has three components:

1. **Objective literacy:** 12–16 parallel scenario items, 15–20 minutes.
2. **Performance mission:** two equivalent but non-identical cases, 25–35 minutes, requiring framing, AI direction, evidence checks, and diagnosis of a seeded failure.
3. **Calibration:** confidence captured after key decisions; report overconfidence, underconfidence, and calibration error.

Pre and post forms share constructs, difficulty, rubric, and approximate information volume but never company names, surface wording, source order, or seeded defect. The pre is partly hidden after submission. Growth reporting separates objective knowledge, process performance, risk judgment, and calibration. Self-report is shown as perception, not competence.

### Portfolio and course assessment

The internally frozen evidence mix, pending Masters' Union academic and staffing approval, is:

- **20% decision evidence brief**;
- **30% AI operating prototype**;
- **25% individual unseen transfer sprint**;
- **15% recruiter case page and proof pack**;
- **10% structured participation and peer attack**.

Apply a 70% common capability rubric plus a 30% stream-judgment overlay to the first four components. Participation means observable preparation, assigned review completion, evidence-specific peer testing, and response to feedback—not airtime, charisma, or attendance alone.

## Anti-gaming and ownership proof

The course permits and expects AI assistance. Integrity means honest provenance and personal control, not pretending the work was unaided.

| Gaming path | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Copy a starter or classmate artifact | Starters are allowed and recorded; grade hidden adaptation, failure repair, and defence |
| Delegate the entire task in one prompt | Require pre-tool prediction, intermediate structured decisions, source/claim trail, and diagnosis |
| Import a public skill/workflow template | Record origin; require changed business contract, hidden test, false-positive test, and clean-session transfer |
| Submit a screenshot or prerecorded happy path | Require live endpoint, machine-readable execution history, injected failure, and rerun |
| Fabricate users or exploit social access | Use LMS-assigned role-card task attempts with supplied inputs and observable events; external users are optional enrichment |
| Cosmetic Git history or final-day dump | Create automatic lesson checkpoints; link submissions to immutable commit IDs and execution timestamps |
| Overfit known tests | Maintain hidden equivalent variants and change one requirement at defence |
| Outsource work | Sample oral defence, inspect version trail, and require live modification; never rely on style detection alone |
| Generate reflection after the fact | Reflection prompts reference a timestamped prediction, run, error, source, or commit |
| Invent citations or data | Verify URL/source existence, open consequential sources, link claim to exact evidence, and audit a random claim |
| Hide unsafe content or leaked credentials | Automated secret/PII scan plus publication quarantine and remediation |
| Game XP with repeated low-value actions | Award XP on first meaningful evidence or improvement, cap repeatable events, keep grades separate |
| AI grader flatters AI output | Use deterministic checks, criterion-bound evidence citations, trained-human primary scoring, moderation, and appeal |

Integrity signals should create a review queue, not automatic guilt. Similar submissions may be legitimate because students share templates, cases, and tools. The final determination rests on provenance, test performance, and ownership evidence.

## Feedback and motivation design

### Feedback order

1. Confirm what the system observed.
2. Identify the exact criterion met or missed.
3. Cite the student's evidence or missing evidence.
4. Diagnose the likely failure layer.
5. Recommend the smallest next action.
6. Offer a hint only after the student chooses to use one.
7. Reveal the reference answer only after the attempt closes, when appropriate.

### XP and badges

XP rewards useful practice behaviors: committing a prediction, finding a non-obvious failure, correcting a claim, completing a clean-session peer test, handling an exception, or improving after feedback. It does not reward token volume, time online, number of prompts, or raw submission count.

Badges should name professional evidence, for example:

- Source Skeptic — rejected a plausible unsupported claim;
- Changed My Mind — revised a thesis after counter-evidence;
- Boundary Setter — made an AI system abstain or escalate correctly;
- Failure Hunter — reproduced and classified a hidden failure;
- Reliable Operator — recovered a workflow from an injected fault;
- User Witness — changed a release decision from observed behavior;
- Proof Builder — published a complete, reproducible evidence trail;
- Executive Owner — defended and modified a system under challenge.

Badges are private by default and publishable by the learner. Competitive leaderboards, if used, should be section-local, temporary, and based on challenge performance or improvement—not cumulative advantage. Never rank maturity scores, accessibility accommodations, or speed on tasks where speed is not an outcome.

## Telemetry architecture

### Event taxonomy

Event names should describe learning actions and system states, not vendor UI clicks.

| Family | Example events | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access | `access_check_started`, `account_blocked`, `fallback_pack_opened` | operational readiness |
| Brief | `brief_opened`, `work_sample_brief_submitted`, `brief_revision_requested` | work-sample feasibility |
| Decision | `prediction_committed`, `confidence_recorded`, `human_ai_boundary_set` | ownership and calibration |
| Build | `build_started`, `checkpoint_saved`, `external_artifact_linked` | progress and evidence continuity |
| Evidence | `source_added`, `claim_linked`, `dataset_versioned`, `consent_attached` | provenance |
| Execution | `run_started`, `run_succeeded`, `run_failed`, `approval_requested`, `fallback_used` | system reliability and capability |
| Validation | `validator_started`, `check_failed`, `unsafe_state_detected`, `gate_passed` | mastery and friction |
| Feedback | `feedback_viewed`, `hint_requested`, `feedback_contested`, `revision_submitted` | feedback effectiveness |
| Quiz | `item_answered`, `misconception_tagged`, `retrieval_repair_completed` | knowledge retention |
| Peer / user | `assignment_proposed`, `peer_attack_assigned`, `peer_test_completed`, `qualified_attempt_received`, `attempt_replacement_issued` | assigned task evidence and exchange reliability |
| Defence | `defence_sampled`, `claim_opened`, `live_change_completed` | ownership proof |
| Publish | `publish_scan_failed`, `portfolio_published`, `proof_view_opened` | safe career signal |

### Telemetry rules

- collect the minimum event properties needed for learning and operations;
- do not treat raw prompts or sensitive connector payloads as default analytics;
- classify data as public portfolio, private learning evidence, restricted personal data, or operational telemetry;
- show learners what is captured and allow correction of profile/context fields;
- define retention and deletion rules before launch;
- exclude accessibility and accommodation data from performance comparisons;
- version event schemas and validate them before use in grading;
- never grade time-on-task, keystrokes, or prompt count;
- make qualified-use telemetry explicit and consented.

## Reliability and fallback architecture

### Failure classes

1. learner misunderstanding;
2. invalid or unsafe learner input;
3. case/data defect;
4. LMS or validator defect;
5. external model variance or outage;
6. connector/API credential or rate-limit failure;
7. deployment/hosting failure;
8. section-wide network or power failure.

The platform must classify these before assigning a failed attempt.

### Required controls

- autosave structured work locally and server-side where possible;
- idempotent submission and execution IDs;
- visible system status and retry guidance;
- cached case materials and downloadable data;
- deterministic validator reruns against frozen submissions;
- model timeout, retry, circuit breaker, and bounded fallback response;
- frozen/canned persona turns for core learning if live generation fails;
- precomputed execution logs for diagnosis missions;
- sandbox credentials with least privilege and reset capability;
- facilitator section-wide fallback switch and deadline/attempt relief;
- incident records linked to affected attempts so grades can be corrected;
- no single external vendor required to complete a high-stakes assessment.

Every simulator brief must include a low-fidelity classroom version that can start within five minutes. Fallback mode should preserve the decision, diagnosis, and evidence objective even when it cannot preserve the spectacle.

## Accessibility and inclusion

The target is WCAG 2.2 AA-equivalent behavior across core flows, plus instructional alternatives:

- full keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic landmarks, and accessible error messages;
- captions and transcripts for every video, voice, and AI-persona response;
- text alternative to voice input/output and non-voice path for clone missions;
- alt text or equivalent description for meaningful images and charts;
- no color-only status, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion support, and zoom/reflow;
- screen-reader-readable tables, logs, charts, and validation results;
- instructions in plain English with glossary support; do not grade accent, idiom, or performative fluency;
- adjustable timing and pause/resume for approved accommodations;
- individual reflection before fast group discussion to reduce airtime bias;
- mobile-friendly reading and submission, while clearly identifying tasks that need a laptop;
- downloadable and printable case packs;
- equivalent alternative when voice, video, public posting, or live presentation creates a documented barrier;
- public portfolios use learner-controlled display name and disclosure choices.

Accessibility alternatives must test the same capability and quality bar, not reduce rigor.

## Privacy, safety, and publication governance

- use public, licensed, synthetic, or institution-approved datasets;
- prohibit uploading employer/client confidential information and personal sensitive data;
- provide consent-cleared or fictional identity packs for clone/voice work;
- record synthetic-media disclosure and asset provenance;
- separate private assessment evidence from public portfolio content;
- require an “independent speculative project; not affiliated with or endorsed by the company” label;
- scan repositories and submissions for secrets, credentials, private keys, and likely personal data;
- quarantine unsafe publication without deleting the learner's private evidence;
- give learners a correction, unpublish, export, and deletion route;
- log administrative access to restricted evidence;
- define red-team and incident response for harmful persona outputs, prompt injection, and content misuse.

## Release gates

No lesson, quiz, simulator, or validator is released until it passes:

1. **Alignment:** every activity maps to a capability; every capability has evidence.
2. **Authenticity:** the learner makes a credible business decision under meaningful constraints.
3. **Demonstrability:** evidence can be opened, observed, explained, and defended.
4. **Feasibility:** it fits 20 hours, 480 students, eight sections, staffing, and tool capacity.
5. **Fairness:** prior coding skill, paid tools, charisma, or subjective taste cannot dominate.
6. **Consistency:** prompts, rubric, answer key, validators, facilitator notes, and feedback agree.
7. **Reliability:** model variance, network loss, bad data, and connector failure have recovery paths.
8. **Accessibility:** all core interactions and evidence have equivalent accessible paths.
9. **Portfolio signal:** public evidence is useful to a recruiter and safe to publish.
10. **Maintainability:** volatile tools, cases, sources, and model assumptions are isolated, versioned, and date-stamped.

Each release includes pass, partial, fail, unsafe, accessibility, outage, reset, and adversarial test cases. A failed safety, scoring-validity, or fallback test is a blocker.

## Decisions frozen by this concept

1. The LMS is a mission and evidence system, not the primary creation-tool host.
2. Target role × representative work context × consequential decision × inspectable evidence is persistent student context; operator and advisor/investor modes are explicit.
3. One common capability spine serves all nine streams through equivalent overlays.
4. Artifact success, ownership, and transfer are scored separately.
5. Quizzes support retrieval and diagnosis; applied evidence remains primary.
6. Pre/post maturity uses parallel performance missions plus calibration, not self-report alone.
7. High-stakes scoring cannot depend solely on generative AI judgment.
8. Predictions, hidden tests, failure repair, and defence are core anti-gaming mechanisms.
9. Private proof and public recruiter views are separate.
10. Every core simulator has a low-tech version that preserves the assessed decision and evidence; facilitators also have a five-minute incident bypass.
11. XP and badges motivate evidence-producing behaviors but do not determine grades.
12. Reliability incidents are distinguished from learner failures and can trigger score relief.
