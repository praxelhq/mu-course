# Cold-Student Experience Rubric

**Question:** Would a capable but initially uninterested management student stay engaged for two hours, make meaningful decisions, experience a genuine surprise, and leave able to demonstrate a transferable capability?

Use this after the structural validator and before a session is called internally releasable. Review the student journey in chronological order; do not award points for content that exists only in instructor notes or an unimplemented feature promise.

| Dimension | 0 — fails | 1 — present but weak | 2 — release standard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | abstract framing or lecture opening | relevant example but no urgent question | striking result/business incident creates a consequential question and a learner commitment inside 10 minutes |
| Role clarity | student is a generic “user” | role named but authority/stakes vague | authentic role, decision owner, stakes, boundary, and hiring relevance are immediately legible |
| Agency | follows clicks or copies output | makes low-consequence choices | chooses evidence, method, action, or release path with defensible alternatives |
| Consequence | nothing changes based on choice | cosmetic score/message changes | decision changes a stakeholder, metric, risk, branch, artifact, or release state |
| Surprise | novelty depends on flashy AI output | reveal is interesting but optional | plausible first answer fails against evidence, hidden input, policy, stakeholder, or changed requirement |
| Mechanism visibility | magic demo | concept explained | one controllable mechanism is opened, manipulated, and connected to observed behavior |
| Build time | under 40 minutes of action | 40–59 minutes | at least 60 protected minutes of meaningful learner action with checkpoints |
| Feedback | delayed/generic praise | feedback identifies an issue | mechanical and evidence-citing feedback arrives in time for a visible repair |
| Learning | student can repeat steps | student can explain terminology | student diagnoses why the result occurred and succeeds on an unseen transfer or defence |
| Ownership | polished output is accepted | reflection asks what they did | prediction, distinct assessment input, before/after repair, provenance, and transfer separate ownership from generation |
| Career authenticity | same chatbot/content task for all | nouns change by stream | payload, metric/rule, failure, action, authority, and proof are role-authentic across all eleven pathways |
| Inclusion/reliability | assumes one device, modality, tool, or network | fallback exists but changes the task | supported, accessible, low-bandwidth/offline route preserves the assessed decision and evidence standard |
| Proof/bridge | file upload or “takeaway” | artifact saved | inspectable portfolio receipt plus two-minute recruiter proof action and a motivated bridge to the next mission |

## Required thresholds

- Maximum: 26.
- Internal release: at least 22.
- Hook, Agency, Consequence, Feedback, Learning, and Proof/bridge must each score 2.
- Any safety, privacy, fairness, assessment-validity, or impossible-delivery blocker overrides the numeric score.

## Audit prompts

1. At what exact minute does the learner first commit to a prediction or decision?
2. What would the learner miss emotionally and intellectually if they left after 30 minutes?
3. Which plausible first answer is intentionally broken, and by what evidence?
4. Can a student obtain the same success state by copying the demo or generating a polished explanation?
5. What is the shortest observable sequence proving that the student—not only the model—learned?
6. What role-specific judgment distinguishes the eleven pathways without changing the common standard?
7. What happens in the room if authentication, model access, deployment, audio, or the network fails?
8. What exactly can the student open for a recruiter, and what can they prove in two minutes?

## Audit record

For each session, record:

- score by dimension and total;
- exact asset/path evidence;
- blocker/major/minor findings;
- correction made;
- residual dependency that cannot be solved by source authoring;
- reviewer and date.

Do not use “engaging,” “interactive,” “hands-on,” or “real-world” as evidence. Cite the action, decision, feedback, failure, repair, and proof.
