# Adversarial Review: Company-to-Role AI Transformation Portfolio

**Review date:** 17 July 2026  
**Course:** Masters' Union PGP — AI for Business, Course 1  
**Scale:** 20 live hours, 480 students, eight sections, approximately 60 students per section  
**Career pathways in scope:** Consulting; FOCOS; Product Management; Finance; HR; Supply Chain and Operations; Sales; Marketing; Data  
**Review posture:** Pre-architecture release gate, deliberately adversarial  

## Sources and proposal reviewed

This review tests the following proposed course spine:

> Each student chooses a company they would like to work for, selects a function and business problem, and builds a connected, recruiter-shareable AI transformation portfolio.

It also tests the original universal artifact set: an AI clone, a skill, a deployed workflow, a deployed application with users, a Git repository, an AI-generated ad video, an evidence-led article with data and web extraction, and a pre/post maturity assessment.

The review is grounded in:

- the shared Course Outline v1.2 and its 20-hour, build-heavy delivery format;
- docs/source/course-1-first-draft.md, especially the proposed 10-session sequence and assessment model;
- docs/research/2026-07-15-artifact-backward-curriculum-research.md, especially “What is frozen—and what is not,” “Artifact verdicts,” “How the portfolio can signal post-MBA ability,” “The anti-gaming assessment system,” and “Feasibility at 20 hours and 480 students”; and
- the newly supplied nine career pathways.

The source research already acknowledges the central feasibility problem: seven substantial artifacts plus a diagnostic cannot all become portfolio-grade in 20 hours. The new nine-stream requirement makes that problem more severe because the same artifact has very different career value and data requirements across pathways.

## Release verdict

**FAIL in its current universal-artifact form.**

The company-to-role idea is a strong motivational and portfolio frame, but the current phrase “Company × Function × Business Problem” and the expectation that everyone completes every listed artifact are not releaseable at this scale. They produce scope overload, unequal projects, weak evidence of student ownership, assessment inconsistency, and avoidable legal and reputational risk.

**The spine can become PASS WITH CHANGES** if it is revised to:

> Target role × representative work context × consequential decision × inspectable evidence.

The chosen company remains the motivating context, but it is not the same kind of object in every stream. An operator solves a problem inside a company. A consultant advises a client. An investment banker works on a transaction. A VC evaluates an investment. The portfolio must represent the work of the target role, not force every student to pretend to be an internal product builder or marketer.

The course should have three universal assessed deliverables, role-specific output forms, and a common ownership test. The clone and ad video should not be universal graded outputs.

## What is structurally strong and should be preserved

1. **Aspirational context creates motivation.** A company the student cares about makes research and iteration more meaningful than a generic tutorial.
2. **One connected evidence trail is superior to unrelated tool exercises.** Research, a repeatable system, user or evaluator evidence, and a recruiter-facing explanation can accumulate around the same decision.
3. **The existing Operator's Loop is durable.** Framing, decomposing, matching capabilities, choosing tools, executing, and verifying apply across all nine streams.
4. **Build challenges and hidden failures are valuable.** They create observable learning and reduce the value of cosmetic outputs.
5. **The portfolio can be a real career asset.** It will be valuable if it leads with a role-authentic work sample and relegates implementation evidence to a proof layer.
6. **AI use should be allowed and visible.** The aim is not unaided production. The aim is demonstrated control: specifying, checking, adapting, and defending AI-assisted work.

These strengths do not compensate for the blockers below.

## Blocker findings

### B1. Seven universal portfolio artifacts plus a diagnostic are impossible to make credible in 20 hours

**Location:** Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “What is frozen—and what is not” and “Feasibility at 20 hours and 480 students”; shared COT v1.2, 20-hour allocation.

**Impact:** Students will optimize for visible completion: a shallow prompt packaged as a skill, a copied workflow, a generated app shell, friends clicking a link, a one-click video, and an article drafted by deep research. The resulting portfolio will look busy while providing little trustworthy signal. Novices will spend their time on account setup and debugging; advanced students will spend it polishing. Neither group receives equivalent learning.

At 480 students, seven artifacts create 3,360 artifact submissions before quizzes, diagnostics, peer reviews, and the capstone. Even ten minutes of human attention per artifact would require 560 reviewer hours. That is incompatible with tight, consistent review.

**Smallest correction:** Limit the graded portfolio to three universal objects:

1. an evidence-led decision brief;
2. one tested AI operating prototype appropriate to the stream; and
3. one recruiter case page with an inspectable proof pack.

The pre/post performance mission remains a separate assessment, not a portfolio artifact. Skills, workflows, apps, voice, video, images, and dashboards become possible components of the operating prototype or communication layer, not seven separate graded projects.

**Workload gate:** Before lesson planning, Masters' Union and Praxel must declare whether “20 hours” means live contact only or total student effort. If there are 20 live hours, cap mandatory asynchronous work at 10–12 hours and state it in the prerequisite note. If 20 hours is total effort, reduce the portfolio to the evidence brief and one prototype plus an in-class transfer test.

### B2. “Company × Function × Problem” does not represent work consistently across all nine streams

**Location:** Proposed company portfolio spine; Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “How the portfolio can signal post-MBA ability.”

**Impact:** The current formula fits product, operations, marketing, sales, HR, data, corporate finance, and parts of FOCOS. It breaks for advisory and investing roles:

- a consultant usually solves a client's problem, not an internal problem at the consulting firm;
- an investment banker produces transaction work, not an internal finance application for the bank;
- a VC evaluates and supports portfolio companies, not only the fund's internal processes;
- “Finance” contains at least three substantially different work samples: IB, VC, and corporate finance.

Forcing a universal internal-company interpretation will produce work that is easy to brand but professionally implausible.

**Smallest correction:** Replace the formula with a two-mode portfolio contract:

- **Operator mode:** target employer × target function × internal/customer decision. Used by FOCOS, PM, corporate finance, HR, supply chain and operations, sales, marketing, and data.
- **Advisor/investor mode:** target employer × target role × client/deal/investment decision. Used by consulting, IB, and VC.

Every project must name the decision owner, decision, evidence available, role-authentic deliverable, success criterion, and boundary of student authority.

### B3. Open company choice creates structural inequity in data, access, task difficulty, and portfolio polish

**Location:** Proposed “favorite company” choice; Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “Where durable student excitement will come from,” which recommends bounded choice.

**Impact:** A student choosing a listed consumer company has public filings, customer reviews, campaign assets, product pages, and visible users. A student choosing a private B2B firm, VC fund, HR team, or supply-chain function may have almost no usable data. Students with family or professional access to proprietary information gain an unfair advantage while creating confidentiality risk. Marketing projects will appear more visually polished than finance, HR, operations, or data projects even when the underlying reasoning is weaker.

**Smallest correction:** Introduce a Work Sample Brief approval gate and nine reference pathways. Students may choose a company only if the LMS confirms:

- an approved stream and role;
- a named decision and decision owner;
- sufficient public evidence or an instructor-provided synthetic dataset;
- a role-authentic output;
- a measurable acceptance criterion;
- no dependence on confidential access;
- a supported technical pattern; and
- an independent-project disclosure.

Provide a calibrated fallback data pack for every pathway. A student may add public company evidence, but the assessed hidden test must use standardized or instructor-controlled data. Cap identical company–stream–problem combinations at eight students to reduce portfolio cloning and facilitator overload.

### B4. The proposed evidence does not yet prove individual ownership in an AI-assisted course

**Location:** Universal artifacts; Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “The learning problem hidden by polished artifacts” and “The anti-gaming assessment system.”

**Impact:** Prompt logs, commit histories, screenshots, polished videos, and reflective writing can all be generated or reconstructed after the fact. A friend or agent can build the system. Templates can be relabelled. Students can memorize known demos. The more polished the output, the easier it becomes to mistake model capability for student capability.

**Smallest correction:** Grade stewardship and transfer, not unsupported claims of authorship. Every graded prototype must pass all of these gates:

1. a pre-build decision and acceptance criteria recorded before tool access;
2. one checkpoint created during supervised class time;
3. a recorded failure and diagnosis tied to an execution or test;
4. a held-out input or changed policy issued by the LMS;
5. an individual clean-session modification without access to the original conversation; and
6. a short defence of one evaluator-selected decision.

Git remains useful as an evidence rail, but commit count, prompt volume, and time spent must never be proxies for learning.

### B5. Universal human review and live defence are not feasible for 480 students

**Location:** First-Draft Extract, proposed live capability sprint and three-minute tool-choice defence; Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, LMS and anti-gaming sections.

**Impact:** A three-minute defence for 60 students consumes 180 minutes per section before transitions. Even a 90-second defence consumes 90 minutes and leaves little class time. Sampling only a small fraction of students weakens deterrence and creates perceived unfairness. Pure AI grading is not a valid replacement for ambiguous business judgment and may reward the same model style that generated the work.

**Smallest correction:** Use four assessment layers:

- **100% mechanical validation:** file presence, URL liveness, required fields, test outputs, secrets, citation liveness, telemetry schema, and disclosure labels;
- **100% individual transfer task:** a short parallel case with deterministic and rubric-bounded scoring;
- **structured peer attack:** two assigned peers use a supplied failure prompt and record observable behavior;
- **human audit:** all safety flags, all borderline grades, and a stratified random sample of at least 20%, with cross-section moderation.

Use five anchor submissions per performance band to calibrate facilitators before grading. Double-score at least 10% of human-audited submissions across sections and reconcile material disagreement. Model-based review may draft feedback but must not be the sole source of grades for strategic judgment.

### B6. A public identity clone creates unnecessary safety, legal, and reputational exposure

**Location:** Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “Deep dive 1: the AI clone,” especially “Safety correction.”

**Impact:** Students are incentivized to choose recognizable living leaders because the result appears impressive. This invites unauthorized voice and likeness imitation, invented endorsements, misleading publication, and brand or personality-rights complaints. A clone also rewards surface resemblance and abundant training-set exposure, not necessarily RAG competence. The risk is highest when every portfolio must be public on Praxy.

**Smallest correction:** Drop the famous-person clone as a universal portfolio artifact. Retain the learning mechanism as a controlled simulation using one of:

- a fictional composite;
- a consenting student or instructor;
- a pre-cleared historical/public-domain corpus with non-imitative voice; or
- a role-based evidence assistant that never claims to be a real person.

Public voice cloning must require recorded consent, synthetic-media disclosure, and an approved identity pack. A text-based grounded assistant can be an optional advanced component after passing retrieval, citation, abstention, and safety tests.

## Major findings

### M1. “Favorite company” is motivational but is not itself a recruiter signal

**Location:** Proposed company choice and portfolio promise.

**Impact:** Recruiters do not primarily value brand enthusiasm. They value role judgment, evidence, and transfer. A speculative project can appear presumptuous or superficial when the student lacks internal data. Recruiters are unlikely to inspect seven thumbnails, a full repository, or a long video.

**Correction:** Use “target employer or sector context,” not “favorite company,” in formal course language. Lead the public portfolio with a 60–90 second recruiter scan:

- target role;
- decision and why it matters;
- evidence used and its limits;
- system built;
- one observed result;
- one failure and fix;
- what the student would test with internal access; and
- links to the brief, live prototype, and proof pack.

Every page must state: “Independent educational project based on public or simulated information; not commissioned, endorsed, or affiliated with the named company.”

### M2. A universal deployed app biases the curriculum toward Product Management and coding confidence

**Location:** Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “Deep dive 4: the deployed application” and “Venture Studio.”

**Impact:** A web app is authentic evidence for PM and some founder roles. It is not the most authentic interface for every stream. A finance analyst may need a model and memo, an operations manager an exception workflow, HR a policy and workforce-planning aid, and data a reproducible notebook and dashboard. Requiring an app privileges visual UI and coding-agent fluency over business suitability.

**Correction:** Rename the universal build to **AI operating prototype**. The allowed implementation forms are:

- workflow or agent with human approval;
- lightweight application;
- grounded assistant;
- decision-support dashboard or model;
- data pipeline plus analytical surface; or
- a combination using a provided shell.

All forms share the same common checks: defined user and decision, structured inputs/outputs, live or replayable execution, failure handling, evidence of use, and an unseen modification.

### M3. “Five real users” measures social access as much as product capability

**Location:** Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “Redefine five real users.”

**Impact:** Extroverted students, students with professional networks, and consumer-facing projects have an advantage. Finance, HR, B2B, and internal operations projects cannot easily recruit authentic users. Friends may click through without understanding the role. Evidence collection may expose personal or company data.

**Correction:** Replace external user count with a managed **task-attempt exchange**. The LMS assigns three to five testers who receive a role card, task, input pack, and consent notice. Grade:

- completion of the declared task;
- time or error against the stated baseline where meaningful;
- one observed friction;
- one change tied to evidence; and
- a rerun after the change.

External target users can strengthen the portfolio but cannot be required for the grade. Five attempts are formative evidence, not market validation.

### M4. The evidence-led article is too large and too style-specific

**Location:** Original “Ken-style article” proposal; Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “Deep dive 7.”

**Impact:** A long-form publication consumes disproportionate time, invites one-shot generation, and rewards English fluency and magazine-style prose. “Ken-style” encourages imitation of a publisher's voice rather than an authentic work product. It is less role-authentic for data, HR, sales, operations, and several finance tracks.

**Correction:** Merge research, scraping, data work, and writing into a **decision evidence brief** capped at 1,000–1,500 words or six presentation screens. Require one original calculation, a claim ledger, a counter-hypothesis, data/source provenance, and a decision recommendation. Allow role-authentic formats such as an investment memo, executive brief, operating review, experiment readout, or analytical notebook summary.

### M5. Skill and workflow are being treated as separate trophies when they are one operating system

**Location:** Universal artifact list; Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “Deep dive 2” and “Deep dive 3.”

**Impact:** Students can submit a generic skill and an imported workflow without understanding how judgment moves through the system. Grading them separately duplicates setup and rewards packaging.

**Correction:** Merge them into the AI operating prototype:

- the **skill** specifies when the method applies, required inputs, judgment steps, output contract, and checks;
- the **workflow or application** executes some of that method, captures state, and escalates exceptions.

The student must explain which steps remain deterministic, which use a model, which require a human, and why. A skill without a clean-session test or a workflow without failure recovery remains practice evidence, not a portfolio artifact.

### M6. One common rubric will under-value stream expertise; nine unrelated rubrics will destroy consistency

**Location:** Nine-stream expansion; first-draft common assessment model.

**Impact:** A single generic rubric will reward polish and technical complexity. Nine wholly separate rubrics will make section grades incomparable and be expensive to maintain.

**Correction:** Use a 70/30 rubric architecture:

- **70% common evidence:** problem framing, source/data quality, human–AI allocation, reliability, ownership/transfer, and communication of limits;
- **30% stream judgment:** role-specific quality of the decision and work product.

Publish one common rubric and nine one-page stream overlays. Finance needs three exemplar variants—IB, VC, and corporate finance—even if they share one overlay family.

### M7. Tool access, credits, hardware, and network variance will dominate outcomes unless the golden path is controlled

**Location:** First-Draft Extract, tool-heavy session sequence; Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “Tool-release policy.”

**Impact:** Paid credits, generation queues, account restrictions, API keys, laptops, and network failures create grade inequity. Voice and video tools are especially variable. Supporting several workflow builders and coding stacks across eight sections is operationally unsafe.

**Correction:** Supply one pre-provisioned golden path for each capability slot, one starter repository, managed credentials, pooled credits, saved fallback inputs/outputs, and a no-network recovery exercise. Advanced tools are allowed but confer no rubric advantage. Freeze the delivery stack and instructor runbook before the pilot, and version every case and validator.

### M8. Public portfolios create privacy, IP, brand, and data-publication risks beyond the clone

**Location:** Portfolio publication promise and universal company context.

**Impact:** Students may upload copyrighted brand assets, scraped content, personal data, confidential employer material, synthetic claims presented as facts, API credentials, user recordings, or interfaces that appear to be official company products. HR, finance, sales, and operations artifacts are particularly sensitive.

**Correction:** Make publication a separate pass/fail gate. The LMS must block publication until the artifact passes:

- secret and personal-data scan;
- source and license manifest;
- public versus synthetic data labels;
- consent records for recordings or user evidence;
- company/non-affiliation disclaimer;
- synthetic-media disclosure;
- no automated consequential action in HR or finance;
- no live outbound sales or marketing action without approval; and
- no unapproved identity imitation.

Students should be able to keep the proof layer private while publishing a sanitized recruiter view.

### M9. A pre/post self-rating will overstate learning and cannot compare streams fairly

**Location:** AI maturity assessment proposal; Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research, “Deep dive 8.”

**Impact:** Confidence may rise without transfer. Different company projects make raw before/after performance incomparable. Reusing the same case leaks the assessment.

**Correction:** Use parallel, unseen business cases with the same capability rubric. Keep the pre-test low-stakes. Measure objective literacy, observed performance, and confidence calibration separately. The post-task must use a different company and surface context from the student's portfolio to prove transfer. Report maturity growth separately from the course grade and never publish a single maturity number without dimension scores and confidence intervals or clear limitations.

### M10. Participation and peer evaluation can become popularity and English-fluency grades

**Location:** First-Draft Extract, proposed participation and peer weight; COT challenge and leaderboard design.

**Impact:** Visible contests favor confident presenters, fast typists, prior AI users, and visually attractive streams. Peer voting is not a reliable assessment of business judgment. A leaderboard may suppress experimentation if public failure harms status.

**Correction:** Participation should reward completion of defined review actions, not airtime or popularity. Peer feedback should use observable prompts and checkboxes plus one evidence-based comment. Leaderboards can recognize challenge achievements but must not affect grades. Allow text, audio, or supported presentation modes where the outcome does not specifically assess live speaking.

## Recommended artifact disposition

| Candidate artifact | Decision | Revised role in the course | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence-led long-form article | **Merge and reduce** | Decision evidence brief with data, claim ledger, and counter-hypothesis | Preserves research/data judgment without rewarding length or prose imitation |
| Student-built skill | **Merge** | Method layer inside the AI operating prototype | A skill has value only when another operator or clean session can run it |
| Deployed workflow | **Merge and retain** | Execution layer inside the AI operating prototype | Strong signal for repeatability, integration, approvals, and failure handling |
| Deployed application | **Make one implementation option** | App, workflow, assistant, dashboard, or data pipeline selected by role | Avoids forcing PM-shaped evidence on every stream |
| Five real users | **Replace** | Three to five LMS-assigned qualified task attempts plus one evidence-led revision | Reduces network advantage and fake adoption |
| Git repository | **Retain as evidence rail** | Versioned proof pack with setup, data manifest, tests, decisions, and failure log | Useful for provenance; not a standalone project or commit-count contest |
| AI ad video and image work | **Make role-specific communication option** | Core for Marketing; useful option for Sales, PM, and FOCOS; practice/demo for other streams | Weak universal recruiter signal and high tool variance |
| Famous-person AI clone | **Drop as universal public artifact** | Controlled RAG/voice simulation or optional consent-cleared extension | Teaches useful mechanisms but carries disproportionate identity risk |
| AI maturity assessment | **Retain outside the portfolio** | Parallel pre/post performance missions and calibration measure | Measures transfer; self-report alone is invalid |
| Recruiter case page | **Add as universal core** | One-page public summary linking to sanitized proof | Makes the evidence legible within recruiter attention constraints |

## Recommended three-object portfolio

### 1. Decision evidence brief

An individual role-authentic brief that frames one consequential decision, uses a source hierarchy and supplied/public data, contains at least one original calculation, presents counter-evidence, and names limitations.

**Common proof:** source plan, raw snapshot, data dictionary, calculation, claim ledger, counter-hypothesis, recommendation, and oral or written defence of one selected claim.

### 2. AI operating prototype

One deployed or replayable system that performs a narrow piece of role work. It may be an app, workflow, grounded assistant, dashboard, model, or combined shell.

**Common proof:** user/decision owner, input/output contract, human–AI allocation, live execution, approval or escalation, failure injection, logs, assigned task attempts, evidence-led revision, and unseen modification.

### 3. Recruiter case page and proof pack

A sanitized public page designed for a 60–90 second scan, backed by a private or public repository containing implementation evidence.

**Common proof:** problem, role, business relevance, build, observed outcome, one failure-and-fix story, limits, independent-project disclosure, two-minute walkthrough, and proof links.

The pre/post performance mission is the fourth assessed component but is not presented as student portfolio work.

## Exact course gates

### Gate 0 — Work Sample Brief approval

Before research begins, the student submits:

- target employer or sector;
- target role and stream;
- operator or advisor/investor mode;
- named decision owner and decision;
- authentic work product;
- evidence/data availability tier;
- supported prototype form;
- success criterion and non-goals; and
- confidentiality and affiliation declaration.

The LMS approves, redirects to a reference pathway, or assigns a fallback pack. No unapproved custom stack receives live technical support.

### Gate 1 — Evidence sufficiency

Required before the build unlocks:

- two primary or authoritative sources where the domain permits;
- raw data snapshot and timestamp;
- data dictionary;
- lawful extraction note;
- pre-data thesis;
- one counter-hypothesis; and
- one metric or observable decision-quality criterion.

### Gate 2 — Build ownership checkpoint

Completed in supervised class time:

- student predicts expected output;
- student configures or modifies one substantive mechanism;
- system produces a replayable execution;
- evidence is checkpointed automatically; and
- student labels what was supplied, generated, adapted, and authored.

### Gate 3 — Safety and publication boundary

The system classifies the project for identity, privacy, employment, finance, outbound communication, data licensing, and brand risk. High-risk actions are sandboxed or require human approval. Failure blocks public publication, not necessarily private learning submission.

### Gate 4 — Reliability and failure handling

The LMS injects at least one pathway-specific failure. The student identifies the failing layer, repairs or contains it, and explains the trade-off. Consequential workflows must have an approval, escalation, or safe-stop path.

### Gate 5 — Qualified task attempts

The LMS assigns testers with role cards. Evidence includes task completion, one observed friction, privacy-safe feedback, one release decision, and a rerun. External users are optional enrichment.

### Gate 6 — Individual transfer

Every student receives an unseen company, data fragment, or changed policy and must adapt the method in a clean session. This is the primary ownership evidence and cannot be completed as a group.

### Gate 7 — Recruiter publication

Before the portfolio page goes live, the LMS checks secrets, personal data, citations, licenses, disclosure, company affiliation language, synthetic media, and link liveness. The public artifact must remain understandable without the instructor's explanation.

## Assessment recommendation

Preserve the spirit of the COT's build log, capability sprint, capstone, and participation model, but make the evidence more reliable:

| Component | Suggested weight | Evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Decision evidence brief | 20% | Research/data judgment and role-specific recommendation |
| AI operating prototype | 30% | Working system, reliability, task attempts, and iteration |
| Individual unseen transfer sprint | 25% | Clean-session adaptation and explanation on a parallel case |
| Recruiter case page and proof pack | 15% | Legibility, provenance, failure-and-fix story, and disclosure |
| Structured participation and peer attack | 10% | Completion of assigned reviews and observable feedback, not popularity |

Apply the 70/30 rubric architecture within the first four components. Safety, privacy, secrets, consent, and affiliation are pass/fail publication gates; a polished unsafe artifact cannot compensate with points elsewhere.

## Facilitation and LMS implications

The architecture is only viable if the LMS does more than collect files. It must:

- route students into one of the nine pathway families and three finance variants;
- approve Work Sample Briefs against structured criteria;
- issue calibrated public/synthetic data packs and section-specific hidden tests;
- capture pre-build predictions and supervised checkpoints;
- provision one supported environment and recovery assets;
- validate links, files, schemas, tests, citations, disclosures, and secrets;
- route qualified task attempts across sections;
- store execution evidence and failure diagnoses;
- assign individual transfer cases;
- flag submissions for human review using transparent rules;
- support common-rubric plus stream-overlay grading;
- provide anchor submissions and moderation reports across eight sections; and
- separate the public recruiter page from private proof and evaluator evidence.

Automated checks should verify observable properties. They should not manufacture a score for strategic quality. Human attention belongs at ambiguous judgment, transfer, safety exceptions, and grade boundaries.

## Additional equity controls

1. **Data parity:** grade the assessed mechanism on instructor-provided or standardized data; company-specific public evidence adds context, not hidden advantage.
2. **Tool parity:** no grade advantage for paid models, premium design tools, custom hosting, or advanced code stacks.
3. **Access parity:** no requirement for insider interviews, proprietary systems, or an external professional network.
4. **Language parity:** grade clarity and evidence, not magazine prose or accent. Supply writing and presentation scaffolds.
5. **Disability and format access:** supply captions, transcripts, keyboard-accessible environments, and non-live alternatives where live performance is not the outcome.
6. **Experience parity:** provide a golden path and extension lane; advanced work may deepen failure handling but cannot replace required evidence.
7. **Section parity:** use calibrated variants, anchor work, cross-scoring, and section-level grade distribution review.

## Trade-offs that must be accepted explicitly

1. **Breadth versus credibility:** Students can touch clone, voice, video, skills, workflows, apps, and data, but only three objects should be graded as portfolio evidence.
2. **Choice versus fairness:** Students can choose context and role, but not an arbitrary unsupported data problem or tool stack.
3. **External authenticity versus access equity:** Real external users are valuable enrichment, but assigned qualified task attempts are the fair graded baseline.
4. **Public portfolio versus safe evidence:** The public layer must be sanitized; some high-quality proof will remain evaluator-only.
5. **Automation versus judgment:** LMS automation can validate facts and route work; human moderation is still required for business decisions and safety.
6. **Company specificity versus transfer:** The portfolio can be company-specific, but the final assessment must use a different company or context.
7. **Spectacle versus professional signal:** Clone and video create excitement. They should remain demos or pathway options rather than universal recruiter artifacts.

## Release checklist

The course architecture should not proceed to ten detailed lesson plans until all of the following are frozen:

- [ ] “20 hours” is defined as live hours or total student effort, with an asynchronous workload cap.
- [ ] The spine is revised to target role × work context × decision × evidence.
- [ ] Operator and advisor/investor modes are specified.
