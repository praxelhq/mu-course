# Facilitator Handbook — Course 1

**Audience:** lead instructors, section facilitators, assessment scorers, central exchange/incident desk  
**Status:** operational draft; exact people, tools, rooms and SLAs added after MU approval

## Course promise to protect

Students perform one representative piece of work for a target role: investigate a consequential decision, build a bounded AI operating prototype, break/repair it, and publish inspectable recruiter evidence.

Protect the formal spine:

`target role × representative work context × consequential decision × inspectable evidence`

Do not let the classroom drift into tool tourism, generic AI strategy, app polish, company fandom, prompt competitions or unsupported claims.

## What is common and what changes

All pathways share outcomes, time, supported tools, validators, 70% rubric, ownership tests and evidence burden. Pathway packs change role, mode, records, calculation/rule, decision, failure, authority, 30% anchor and recruiter proof.

Finance must retain distinct IB, VC and Corporate Finance variants. Consulting/IB/VC normally use advisor/investor mode; other pathways normally use operator mode.

## Studio grammar

Every session should visibly include:

1. prediction/retrieval before assistance;
2. concise consequential demo;
3. one opened mechanism;
4. scaffolded then independent build;
5. injected failure/change;
6. diagnosis and repair;
7. transfer/ownership evidence;
8. frozen evidence and async bridge.

Cases create the tension for a build in 10–15 minutes; they are not lectures. Protect at least 60 minutes of student reasoning/build/testing work.

## Before each section

- use only the version-locked lesson/content pack assigned to the section;
- complete the lesson's facilitator preparation checklist;
- run normal, partial, fail, unsafe, accessibility and outage fixtures from a student account;
- verify source/data licenses, snapshots, answer keys and hidden/reserve variants;
- rehearse the demo and failure branch on the classroom network;
- confirm roster, streams/subpaths, compatible access routes and accommodations on a least-privilege view;
- confirm primary scorer, moderation and incident/exchange desk coverage;
- stage printed/cached/replay packs and a five-minute fallback switch;
- do not reveal later-section hidden variants or solutions.

## During the room

Use the dashboard for action, not surveillance:

- access/pre-work blockers;
- mission gate/stuck state;
- misconception by capability;
- platform incidents separated from learner difficulty;
- safety/privacy/accessibility flags;
- useful failure examples for debrief;
- pacing against common run of show.

Do not use prompt volume, keystrokes, general time online, speed, speaking frequency or leaderboard position as learning evidence.

### Intervention ladder

1. Ask for the decision, evidence and current state.
2. Ask the learner to predict which layer failed.
3. Point to the relevant contract/check, not the answer.
4. Reveal the standard hint if the gate permits it.
5. Move to the supported scaffold/golden route without penalty for tool choice.
6. Apply incident/accessibility fallback and log the reason.
7. Escalate safety, privacy, integrity or scoring disputes; do not improvise a high-stakes ruling.

### Common facilitator moves

| Symptom | Move |
| --- | --- |
| “Build an AI strategy for X” | demand role, work context, decision, owner, evidence and thin task |
| beautiful app with no decision | ask what task/result/guardrail can be observed in three minutes |
| confident model output | trace claim/input/calculation and test absent/conflicting evidence |
| workflow with many nodes | reduce to one event, one unit of work, one terminal state and one safe failure |
| “human in loop” label | name role, information, approve/edit/reject choices, rationale and appeal |
| only happy path | inject invalid/duplicate/service/policy condition and require replay |
| peer praise | ask for observable task state, evidence pointer, friction and consequence |
| tiny sample overclaim | require assigned-attempt disclaimer and bounded release decision |
| video/app by default | ask which stakeholder action makes the format role-authentic |
| copied/template concern | inspect declared origin, substantive changed contract, failure repair and clean transfer |

## Feedback contract

Feedback must name:

- rubric criterion/outcome;
- exact submitted evidence;
- diagnosis, not a vague quality label;
- next smallest action;
- confidence/uncertainty where model-assisted;
- whether the issue is learner, platform, content or policy.

Do not give generic praise, rewrite the work for the learner or allow model feedback to become the final grade. Students may contest a criterion using frozen evidence.

## Scoring and moderation

Lesson rubrics are formative unless the Gradebook Map names frozen summative evidence. Never average lesson scores into a course component.

- deterministic validators check facts/properties for 100%; they do not score strategic truth;
- a trained human primary scorer reviews 100% of judgment-bearing frozen packets;
- peers provide observable task evidence but never grade the author;
- AI may index/summarize evidence and coach, not issue a final criterion score;
- all flags, borderlines, safety cases, incidents and appeals enter moderation;
- at least 10% of every major component is blind-double-scored across sections/streams/bands.

Before a component, independently score five anchors. Resolve mastery-band or material point disagreement. Each score cites evidence and retains scorer, packet, rubric, anchor and moderation versions.

No grade is silently changed. Do not force identical section curves.

## Session 7 exchange operations

The central desk and LMS—not the instructor's social matching—route assignments.

- three unique qualified attempts per author cover expected, edge and boundary;
- author v1/acceptance contract freezes before start and evidence remains hidden until prediction/reveal;
- live replacement target is 90 seconds automated/three minutes desk;
- inaccessible, unsafe or privacy-violating endpoints are quarantined without dual penalty;
- fallback is labelled live peer, cached peer replay, calibrated simulation or facilitator case;
- author cannot choose friends or discard adverse evidence;
- tester participation and author artifact evidence remain separate;
- personal login/data/payment/extensions/connectors are prohibited.

Escalate unmatched, expired, unsafe, disputed, consent-withdrawn and incident cases through the Task-Attempt Exchange Contract.

## Safety and integrity

Stop/quarantine and escalate:

- secrets, credentials, confidential company data or unapproved PII;
- real employee/candidate/customer records;
- unauthorized voice/likeness or deceptive affiliation;
- autonomous consequential external action;
- fabricated sources/data/users/logs/consent;
- prohibited personal sign-in/connector/payment in peer testing;
- suspected hidden-key/variant exposure.

An automated flag is not guilt. Preserve evidence, contain harm, inform the authorized reviewer and use MU fair process.

## Accessibility and alternatives

Core capability—not a particular modality—is assessed unless the outcome specifically requires that modality.

- readable/keyboard/screen-reader route;
- text alternative to voice/video and captions/transcript/alt text;
- extended/asynchronous window where approved;
- printable/static/replay alternative for network/tool failure;
- capability compatibility flags without exposing diagnosis/accommodation to peers;
- equivalent evidence standard and documented override.

Never make a learner demonstrate that a peer prototype is inaccessible by being unable to complete an assessed task.

## Incident procedure

1. Identify scope and classify platform/content/learner/privacy/safety.
2. Preserve current attempt/evidence/version.
3. Activate the lesson's five-minute fallback or quarantine.
4. Log affected learners, time, workaround and evidence class without sensitive payload.
5. Grant retry/equivalent route where verified; do not mark learner failure.
6. Notify section/central owners and prevent leakage to later sections.
7. Reconcile scores/evidence before release and record remediation.

## 24-hour section report

Record:

- attendance/access/pre-work completion;
- actual minutes by segment and protected student-work time;
- artifact/gate completion and misconception heat map;
- platform/content/safety/accessibility incidents and fallback use;
- quiz distribution and repair routing;
- stream/pathway equivalence concerns;
- exchange matching/qualification/replacement rates where relevant;
- student pulse: clarity, difficulty, reliability, confidence and open text;
- proposed change, owner, version and whether it can be safely applied before another section.

Do not silently change assessed difficulty between sections. Validate an equivalent variant or defer the change.

## Session release checklist

- [ ] lesson, content-pack, quiz, validator, rubric and outcome IDs join correctly;
- [ ] all represented pathways/subpaths have role-authentic equivalent packs;
- [ ] normal/failure/hidden/reserve fixtures pass;
- [ ] primary/contrast sources and synthetic data are labelled/licensed;
- [ ] demo and student account work on classroom network;
- [ ] accessible and no-network routes preserve the construct;
- [ ] primary scoring/moderation/exchange/incident owners are staffed;
- [ ] student-visible instructions and answer-key boundaries are correct;
- [ ] later-section leakage controls are active;
- [ ] release signatories recorded in the asset inventory.

If any safety, scoring-validity, accessibility, fallback or evidence-equivalence gate fails, do not release the session.
