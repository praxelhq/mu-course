# Draft Response to Masters' Union Program Associate

**Subject:** AI in Business — prerequisites, evaluation and classroom expectations

Dear Madiha,

Thank you for reaching out, and welcome. We are looking forward to working with you on the AI in Business course.

The course is designed as a build-first operator lab. Students will choose a target role and employer context, investigate a consequential business decision, build a role-relevant AI operating prototype, and publish a recruiter-facing work sample backed by inspectable evidence. To protect classroom build time, the following preparation and infrastructure will be important.

## Student prerequisites

There is no coding prerequisite. Before the first session, each student should complete:

1. a technology and account check;
2. a low-stakes AI maturity diagnostic and short performance task;
3. selection of one placement stream and up to three target employers/sectors;
4. a short data/privacy and academic-integrity acknowledgement; and
5. the Session 1 entry preparation.

Expected pre-course onboarding and baseline time is approximately 83 minutes under the standard route. Most later session pre-work will be capped at 30 minutes. We are planning on the understanding that the 20 course hours are live contact hours and that students may complete approximately 10.1–11.9 structured asynchronous hours across onboarding, pre-work, bridges and the separate post-course maturity forms. Please confirm whether that assumption is correct.

The authored session packs budget **8.7–10.5 session-linked asynchronous hours**; required onboarding/baseline brings the complete asynchronous commitment to **10.1–11.9 hours**. The detailed per-session calculation and explicit reduced-scope route if 20 hours means total effort are in the [Learner Workload Budget](learner-workload-budget.md).

## Student hardware and software

Every student will need:

- a laptop capable of running a current Chrome-based browser;
- charger and reliable access to campus Wi-Fi;
- permission to access the course LMS, GitHub, and the centrally supported AI/build tools;
- headphones with a microphone for designated voice/video exercises;
- spreadsheet software or a browser-based equivalent;
- ability to download/upload course starter files and repository exports.

Praxel will provide the final supported-tool list after the delivery stack and vendor/data-processing approvals are frozen. We would prefer centrally provisioned accounts/credits rather than asking students to purchase tools individually. The provisional capability slots are: one frontier AI/research/coding environment, GitHub, one managed workflow environment, managed deployment/analytics, an allow-listed extraction service, and managed image/voice/video credits. No paid tool should affect a student's grade.

## Cases, readings and datasets

No book purchase is required. Praxel will supply:

- concise case provocations on AI disruption, customer-service/BPO redesign, grounded digital identities, AI accountability, and multimodal brand work;
- nine career-stream challenge families, including separate IB, VC, and Corporate Finance variants;
- instructor-controlled synthetic datasets and public-source packs;
- source/data dictionaries, starter repositories, workflow events, test cases, role cards, and fallback files;
- short pre-session readings/videos and retrieval checks;
- rubrics, evidence templates, safety/disclosure guidance, and portfolio instructions.

Materials for each session should be uploaded to the LMS at least seven days before delivery, with any case update or tool change clearly versioned. Student-specific hidden tests and answer keys must remain in the instructor/evaluator view.

For internal coordination, the exact learner/instructor upload split, capability-level procurement, session-specific inputs and sign-off sequence are maintained in the [Course 1 Resource, Procurement, and LMS Upload Matrix](course-resource-procurement-matrix.md). We will send the student-facing prerequisite checklist and final vendor names only after the supported stack has passed institutional and classroom tests.

## Classroom and room requirements

For each section, we request:

- stable high-capacity Wi-Fi tested for simultaneous student use;
- instructor display and audio;
- movable seating or a practical method for paired testing;
- one facilitator/support lane in addition to the lead instructor where possible;
- a printed/offline case pack held by the Program Associate for network incidents;
- a clear escalation contact for roster, SSO, network, room and accommodation issues.

## Evaluation

The proposed evaluation, pending Masters' Union academic-policy and scorer-capacity approval, is:

- 20% — decision evidence brief;
- 30% — AI operating prototype;
- 25% — individual unseen transfer sprint;
- 15% — recruiter case page and proof pack;
- 10% — structured preparation, peer testing and professional operating contribution.

The pre/post maturity diagnostic is reported separately and will not award points simply for a higher self-rating. All students may use AI; they are assessed on framing, evidence, human–AI allocation, reliability, adaptation, provenance and defence.

The LMS will mechanically validate observable requirements, every student will complete an individual unseen transfer task, and peer testing will follow assigned role/task cards. A trained human will be the primary scorer for every judgment-bearing graded packet; AI may organize evidence and provide rubric-bound coaching but will not issue final criterion grades. Safety/borderline/appeal cases receive moderation and at least 10% of every major component is double-scored across sections. We would like to confirm that this model, its staffing and its grade-turnaround expectation are compatible with Masters' Union's academic and integrity policies.

## Participation and decorum

Participation means observable preparation and contribution, not speaking frequency or leaderboard popularity. Students are expected to:

- arrive with pre-work, accounts and files ready;
- respect timed build and review windows;
- give specific, evidence-based peer feedback;
- disclose AI assistance and sources honestly;
- never fabricate users, citations, company data, logs or affiliation;
- use only public, licensed, course-supplied synthetic, or explicitly consented evidence as labelled, and only course-authorized identity assets;
- never upload confidential, personal, credential or proprietary information to course tools;
- extract/scrape only from course allow-listed sources and in compliance with terms, licenses, rate limits and the supplied snapshot/provenance rules; public visibility alone is not permission;
- obtain consent before recording or publishing user evidence;
- challenge systems and ideas respectfully, and maintain a fallback export of important work.
