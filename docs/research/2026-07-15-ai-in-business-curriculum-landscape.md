# AI in Business Curriculum Landscape

**Research date:** 15 July 2026  
**Scope:** Greenfield research for a 20-hour, credit-bearing Masters' Union PGP course delivered to 480 students across eight sections.  
**Directive:** The prior Praxel course draft is not an input to the recommendation below.

> **Brainstorm status:** The `AI Operator Lab` recommendation in this document was rejected as too abstract and process-heavy. The active direction is a Build Fast/Maven-style builder course with short business case provocations. Retain this document for landscape evidence, not as the current course concept. The current structural research is in [Artifact-Backward Curriculum Research](./2026-07-15-artifact-backward-curriculum-research.md).

## Executive conclusion

The market is crowded with AI literacy, prompting, tool tours, and executive AI strategy. The more compelling live cohort programs differentiate through a narrow transformation, repeated building, expert feedback, and proof that can be shown after the course. The strongest opportunity for Masters' Union is to combine business-school judgment with builder-school evidence:

> **Teach students to direct AI through a messy business problem, produce a reliable outcome, and defend the decisions—then certify the evidence, not the attendance.**

The course should not be organized around tools or media formats. It should be organized around the work loop an AI-native manager must repeatedly perform:

1. frame the outcome and quality bar;
2. decide what the human and AI should each do;
3. give the system context, constraints, and tools;
4. produce and iterate;
5. verify evidence and failure modes;
6. operationalize or communicate the result;
7. own and defend the outcome.

## What the market currently teaches

### Elite business schools: strategic judgment and organizational transformation

- [Harvard Business School Online's AI Essentials for Business](https://online.hbs.edu/Documents/ai-essentials-for-business-syllabus.pdf) runs approximately 16–24 hours across four modules: the AI-first firm and AI factory; generative AI and the cost of cognition; strategy and ethics; and transformation and leadership. Its exercises culminate in proposing an AI project and presenting its transformation case.
- [MIT Sloan and CSAIL's Artificial Intelligence: Implications for Business Strategy](https://executive.mit.edu/on/demandware.static/-/Library-Sites-MSharedLibrary/default/dw76723b72/sampleSchedules/GSAI-sampleschedule.pdf) emphasizes managerial implications over technical implementation across AI, machine learning, generative AI, robotics, business and society, and the future of AI.
- [Stanford GSB's AI-Powered Organization](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/exec-ed/programs/ai-powered-organization/curriculum) uses a five-day arc: fundamentals; organizational problems AI creates and solves; functional applications; leadership and management; and external/global implications. A central framework asks which tasks should be outsourced, augmented, or retained by humans.
- [Wharton's AI for Business](https://executiveeducation.wharton.upenn.edu/for-individuals/all-programs/ai-for-business/) covers big data, machine learning, generative AI, strategy, risk, and governance. The related [Coursera specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/ai-for-business-wharton) reports more than 81,000 enrollments and uses module assessments and some peer-reviewed work.
- [Columbia Business School's Generative AI for Business](https://courses.business.columbia.edu/B8631) is a five-day MBA block course aimed at buyers, general managers, consultants, entrepreneurs, and investors. It combines academic and industry research, guided hands-on work, and student presentations around foundations, opportunities, and impact.
- [Kellogg's AI Strategies for Business Transformation](https://online.em.kellogg.northwestern.edu/ai-strategies-for-business-transformation-program) uses cases and named frameworks for readiness, opportunity identification, governance, and a transformation-roadmap capstone.
- [INSEAD's Transforming Your Business with AI](https://www.insead.edu/executive-education/open-online-programmes/transform-business-ai/content) applies weekly concepts to a participant-owned Action Learning Project, then uses peer review to synthesize the recommendation.

**What to borrow:** frameworks for business value, human–AI task allocation, readiness, risk, governance, and transformation; cases; participant-owned problems; boardroom defence.

**What not to copy:** an executive-education emphasis on understanding and roadmap creation without requiring students to produce reliable working evidence.

### Mass-market courses: literacy, productivity, and breadth

- [DeepLearning.AI's AI for Everyone](https://www.deeplearning.ai/alpha/courses/ai-for-everyone) uses four units—what AI is, building AI projects, building AI in a company, and AI and society—with short videos and quizzes.
- Udemy's large courses tend to promise non-technical literacy, productivity, prompting, cross-functional use cases, ROI, ethics, and adoption. Examples include [Generative AI for Leaders & Business Professionals](https://www.udemy.com/course/generative-ai-for-leaders/) and [AI for Business Leaders](https://www.udemy.com/course/ai-for-business-leaders-with-helen-lee-kupp/).
- YouTube learning is dominated by tool-specific demonstrations and quick builds. It is useful as a demo and troubleshooting library, but does not provide a coherent capability sequence, protected assessment, or feedback loop.

**What to borrow:** accessible explanations, fast wins, short demonstrations, role-relevant examples, and low-friction practice.

**What not to copy:** feature tours, lists of tools, generic prompting frameworks, or assessment based mainly on recall and completion.

### Live cohort and builder programs: identity, artifacts, and feedback

- [Maven's AI-Empowered Operator](https://maven.com/myles-sutholt/mastering-ai-become-an-ai-empowered-operator) sells one coherent transformation: build an AI work system on the learner's real work. It includes a starter workspace, stakeholder-modeled advisors, verification, recurring workflows, personal feedback, office hours, and a clean-session handoff test.
- [Maven's Supermanager with AI](https://maven.com/hilary-gridley/ai-powered-people-management) is role-specific and culminates in a Manager OS, evaluators, and coaches rather than a generic tool inventory.
- [Build Fast with AI's Agentic AI Launchpad](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/genai-course) differentiates through public deployment, many working builds, a capstone, mentorship, Demo Day, and a certificate tied to shipped evidence.

**What to borrow:** a named learner identity, one accumulating system, real work, starter templates, repeated shipping, personal feedback, public demonstration, and a credential backed by inspectable work.

**What not to copy:** engineering depth or tool volume that cannot be taught fairly to a mixed-skill PGP cohort in 20 hours; claims that quantity of builds equals mastery.

## What employers and current usage imply

- The [Microsoft 2026 Work Trend Index](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization) defines advanced “Frontier Professionals” by multi-step agent use, routine workflow redesign, and repeatable team practices. Respondents ranked quality control of AI output and critical thinking as the most important human skills as AI takes on more work. The report's central distinction is knowing when to delegate, collaborate, ask, or explore—and retaining responsibility for the outcome.
- The [World Economic Forum Future of Jobs 2025](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/) places analytical thinking first among employer core skills, followed by resilience, leadership, creative thinking, and self-awareness. It argues that GenAI's main skills effect is likely human augmentation rather than simple substitution.
- The [Anthropic Economic Index, January 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report) shows both augmentation and automation in real use, with higher-value enterprise usage concentrated in a relatively small set of tasks. This favors teaching opportunity selection and workflow quality over indiscriminate AI use.

The durable employability signal is therefore not “knows ChatGPT.” It is the ability to set intent, allocate work, create context, evaluate output, redesign a workflow, and communicate accountable business value.

## Learning-design implications

- A major [review of authentic assessment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030) found benefits for engagement, satisfaction, and employability when assessments resemble workplace tasks.
- Business-education work on [facilitator-led simulations](https://doi.org/10.1108/jwam-02-2023-0018) supports combining problem-based, experiential, collaborative, and reflective learning without requiring an expensive third-party simulation.
- A multi-year redesign of an [introductory business course around repeated simulation](https://doi.org/10.62273/EWDY7020) used progressively harder runs and competency-based redo opportunities.

For this course, that means one repeated operating loop, increasingly difficult missions, realistic ambiguity, rapid feedback, and a chance to improve. Quizzes should diagnose misconceptions and reinforce retrieval; they should not be the main proof of applied competence.

## Recommended course concept

# AI Operator Lab

**Subtitle:** From messy brief to reliable business outcome.

**Promise:** In 20 hours, a student learns to take an unfamiliar business problem, decide how humans and AI should divide the work, produce a decision or working process, verify it, and defend the result.

**Graduate identity:** Not an AI expert or prompt engineer. An **AI-native business operator** who can responsibly direct and improve AI-assisted work.

**Portfolio outcome:** A verified **AI Work Sample**, hosted on Praxy, containing:

1. the original messy business brief;
2. the student's outcome specification and human–AI work plan;
3. research and source trail;
4. an analysis or decision artifact;
5. one repeatable AI-assisted workflow or role-specific assistant;
6. a test set and failure analysis;
7. a short business-value, risk, and adoption case;
8. a five-minute recorded demonstration and defence.

This is stronger than a gallery of unrelated outputs because a recruiter can inspect the student's judgment from problem to outcome.

## Proposed 20-hour experience

| Session | Business mission | What students see | What students do | Evidence retained |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The AI Work Benchmark | Rescue a failed AI-assisted decision | Instructor solves the same case badly, then well | Complete a baseline simulation and diagnose failure | Baseline capability profile |
| 2. Directing the Work | Turn ambiguity into an outcome spec | One vague request becomes a rigorous work plan | Set quality bar, constraints, sources, roles, and stop conditions | Outcome spec and delegation map |
| 3. Research Under Pressure | Advise a leader with incomplete evidence | Deep research with live source challenges | Find, triangulate, cite, and separate fact from inference | Sourced decision brief |
| 4. Data to Decision | Make a recommendation from messy operating data | AI analyzes a flawed dataset and misses a trap | Clean, analyze, challenge, and communicate a decision | Analysis workbook and one-page memo |
| 5. Quality Control and Evals | Decide whether an AI output is safe to use | A polished but unreliable output passes superficial review | Build a small golden set, rubric, and failure taxonomy | Evaluation report |
| 6. Executive Communication | Win alignment without AI slop | The same insight shown as weak and strong executive communication | Turn evidence into a decision narrative and visual | Executive brief or board slide |
| 7. Redesign the Workflow | Move from task help to repeatable value | A manual process mapped into human and AI steps | Map trigger, context, work, review, escalation, and logging | Workflow blueprint |
| 8. Build the AI Teammate | Make the workflow run | Instructor builds a constrained assistant or automation | Configure and test a role-specific workflow using a supported tool | Working workflow plus operating instructions |
| 9. Make the Business Case | Get approval for responsible deployment | A flashy pilot fails the ROI, risk, or adoption test | Quantify value, cost, risk, ownership, and rollout | One-page AI initiative case |
| 10. Operator Arena | Solve and defend an unseen case | No long demo; the challenge begins immediately | Complete a timed simulation, publish the work sample, and defend decisions | Verified final portfolio and performance record |

## Classroom and LMS experience

The LMS should feel like a **mission room**, not a content library:

- students receive scenario emails, data, stakeholder messages, and changing constraints;
- each mission begins with an individual decision before tools open;
- submissions preserve process evidence, not just the polished final file;
- automated checks test citations, required fields, consistency, and known edge cases;
- AI feedback is rubric-bound and never the sole judge of high-stakes work;
- facilitators see a section heat map of common failures and intervene live;
- students can replay a mission with a parallel case after feedback;
- passing evidence can be published directly to the Praxy portfolio;
- pre- and post-course diagnostics use parallel unseen cases to show capability growth.

## Assessment model

- **35% Mission evidence:** research, analysis, evaluation, and workflow artifacts.
- **25% Unseen Operator Simulation:** individual performance on framing, delegation, verification, and decision quality.
- **30% AI Work Sample:** integrated working evidence, business case, demo, and defence.
- **10% Review discipline:** useful peer critique, iteration, and documented response to feedback.

Tool fluency is necessary but not graded as an end in itself. Rubrics should score intent, reasoning, evidence, verification, business value, risk control, and communication.

## Three viable positioning routes

1. **AI Operator Lab — recommended:** broad post-MBA relevance; balances personal competence, workflow redesign, and managerial judgment.
2. **AI Chief of Staff Studio:** emotionally compelling and easy to demo; narrower, with the risk of becoming a personal-productivity course.
3. **AI Transformation Sprint:** strongest consulting and leadership signal; risks becoming strategy-heavy unless every student builds and tests the proposed workflow.

The recommended route can borrow the “Chief of Staff” as one mission and the “Transformation Sprint” as the business-case capstone without letting either define the whole course.

## Research limitations

- Course pages are marketing and curriculum descriptions, not independent evidence of learner outcomes.
- Marketplace enrollment and rating counts indicate demand and social proof, not instructional quality.
- Executive-education audiences bring work experience and organizational contexts that PGP students may not yet have.
- Tool-specific curricula will date quickly; the final course requires a scheduled tool and case refresh before each delivery.
