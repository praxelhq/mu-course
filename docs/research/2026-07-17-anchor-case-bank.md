# Anchor Case Bank for Course 1

**Research date:** 17 July 2026  
**Use:** Short build provocations, not substitute lectures  
**Maintenance:** Recheck volatile company facts and links within 30 days of delivery

## Case-selection rule

An anchor case earns class time only when it creates a decision, constraint, or failure that students immediately use in a build. The instructor receives a five-minute fact pattern, a tension question, a primary-source pack, and one deliberately incomplete interpretation. Students do not memorize company trivia.

## 1. Chegg — when the interface to knowledge changes

### Verified source facts

Chegg's 2025 Form 10-K says Google's AI Overviews and increased student adoption of free and paid generative-AI services reduced traffic and subscriber acquisition. It reports May and October 2025 restructuring plans covering approximately 640 employees—about 56% of its then-current workforce—and a 43% year-over-year decline in Academic Services revenue, driven primarily by lower subscription revenue related to reduced traffic. Chegg also describes a strategic move toward a skills-focused B2B organization. Source: [Chegg 2025 Form 10-K, SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364954/000136495426000021/chgg-20251231.htm).

### Productive tension

Chegg is not merely “a company killed by ChatGPT.” Students must distinguish:

- model substitution from search-distribution change;
- product value from traffic acquisition;
- content advantage from answer-interface advantage;
- a technology response from a business-model response.

### Build use

Session 1 students receive four plausible AI-opportunity briefs for a target role. They reject the fashionable brief that lacks a decision owner, evidence path, or value metric, then write a Work Sample Brief. Hidden change: the initial disruption diagnosis is shown to omit distribution or unit economics.

### Ownership question

“Which assumption in your opportunity thesis would still be false even if the AI prototype worked perfectly?”

## 2. Klarna and customer support — automation, augmentation, or segmentation

### Verified source facts

Klarna's 2025 public filing states that its AI assistant handled a majority of service chats in the relevant period, did work equivalent to more than 700 full-time agents, and averaged two-minute resolutions compared with a 12-minute human-agent average in 2024. It also says the company continues to refine processes to capture AI benefits. Source: [Klarna 2025 SEC registration filing](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0002003292/67991e36-4112-4c68-a771-e3feae27b281.pdf). In June 2025, reporting on the CEO's remarks described plans to retain or restore human service for customers who value it, creating a useful quality/segment tension. Source: [TechCrunch interview report](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/04/klarna-ceo-says-company-will-use-humans-to-offer-vip-customer-service/). Gartner reported in June 2025 that 95% of surveyed customer-service leaders planned to retain human agents while defining AI's role. Source: [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-10-gartner-predicts-50-percent-of-organizations-will-abandon-plans-to-reduce-customer-service-workforce-due-to-ai).

### Productive tension

The decision is not “AI or humans.” Students decide:

- which cases are deterministic, model-assisted, or human-owned;
- what quality, cost, latency, trust, and escalation metrics matter;
- which segments receive which service model;
- where a confident answer creates greater loss than a handoff.

### Build use

Session 5 students map a trigger/state/action workflow, add approval and recovery, then receive a policy exception or low-confidence output. The workflow must stop or escalate without losing the case state.

### Ownership question

“Show the event where your automation should stop, who becomes accountable, and what evidence justifies that boundary.”

## 3. Delphi/digital minds — grounded usefulness versus identity imitation

### Verified source facts

Delphi's creator terms explicitly discuss biometric information/identifiers and a biometric consent form when creating a Creator Digital Mind. Its public identity-protection explanation emphasizes disclosure that the experience is artificial. Sources: [Delphi creator terms](https://www.delphi.ai/terms-creator), [Delphi digital identity protection](https://www.delphi.ai/blog/digital-identity-protection), and [Delphi privacy policy](https://www.delphi.ai/privacy).

### Productive tension

Surface resemblance is not the same as a useful, safe knowledge interface. Students separate:

- source grounding from model prior;
- identity/voice consent from availability of public media;
- answer accuracy from style imitation;
- disclosure from permission;
- high-confidence answer from justified abstention.

### Build use

Session 3 uses a fictional, consenting, historical/public-domain, or non-imitative role interface. Students compare base-model and retrieved answers, add a contradictory source, test prompt injection and abstention, and optionally experience a consent-cleared voice layer.

### Ownership question

“Which behavior comes from your corpus and policy rather than the model's prior, and how did you prove it?”

## 4. Air Canada chatbot — the organization owns the answer

### Verified source facts

In *Moffatt v. Air Canada*, the British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal found Air Canada liable after its website chatbot supplied incorrect information about retroactive bereavement fares. The correct policy existed elsewhere on the website; the decision rejected the idea that the chatbot could be separated from the company's responsibility for website information. Source: [BC Civil Resolution Tribunal decision](https://decisions.civilresolutionbc.ca/crt/crtd/en/item/525448/index.do).

### Productive tension

Students decide whether the system should answer, cite, clarify, or escalate when:

- authoritative sources conflict;
- policy changes over time;
- the cost of a wrong answer is asymmetric;
- a link to correct information does not repair a false claim;
- an AI vendor is involved but the organization remains accountable.

### Build use

Session 7 injects stale policy or contradictory evidence into an otherwise convincing prototype. Students diagnose the failure layer, change precedence/abstention/escalation, and rerun an assigned task attempt.

### Ownership question

“Why is your final answer safe enough to act on—or why must it stop?”

## 5. Coca-Cola AI holiday work — production compression versus audience meaning

### Verified source facts

Coca-Cola's account of its 2025 global holiday campaign describes using advanced generative-AI tools and visual annotations to control camera angles and physics-like realism, and explicitly compares 2024 and 2025 AI-powered work. Source: [Coca-Cola company media release](https://www.coca-colacompany.com/media-center/coca-cola-refreshes-givers-of-the-season-embraces-ai-powered-storytelling-in-global-holiday-campaign.html). Public reaction to the 2024 and 2025 work was mixed and often critical, which creates a useful distinction between production control and audience effect. The course should archive representative reactions near delivery rather than teaching a universal verdict.

### Productive tension

Students separate:

- faster/cheaper production from a stronger proposition;
- one-shot spectacle from controlled direction;
- brand consistency from emotional resonance;
- declared AI use from audience trust;
- preference feedback from business outcome.

### Build use

Session 8 requires a locked evidence/claim brief before media tools open. Students create or critique variants, then receive a changed brand, factual, accessibility, or stakeholder constraint. Marketing students may publish a video; other streams choose a role-authentic readout while still experiencing multimodal direction.

### Ownership question

“Which audience insight caused this choice, which claim is evidenced, and what result would falsify the communication strategy?”

## 6. HR, Finance, Sales, and Operations — consequential-action boundary

The course does not need one famous story for every stream. These streams use policy and incident patterns:

- **HR:** a historical proxy creates subgroup harm; the tool must not make an employment decision. Use synthetic aggregate/row-level records and India privacy requirements. Source family: [MeitY Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa).
- **Finance:** a stale filing, wrong comparable, formula defect, or currency/period mismatch changes an investment/transaction/capital recommendation. Use traceable public filings and course-controlled models. Source families: [SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces), [NSE filings directory](https://www.nseindia.com/companies-listing/corporate-filings-directory), and [CFA reasonable-basis standard](https://www.cfainstitute.org/standards/professionals/code-ethics-standards/standards-of-practice-v-a).
- **Sales:** stale CRM data or an unsupported personalization claim tempts an outbound action; seller approval and opt-out boundaries remain explicit.
- **Operations:** duplicate events, late data, and service timeouts test idempotency, fallback, and human override. Use synthetic process/event logs calibrated to public business context.

## Case maintenance checklist

Before each delivery:

- open every primary source and archive a permitted snapshot/extract;
- date all company and regulatory facts;
- separate source fact, instructor inference, and simulated record;
- create at least two equivalent hidden variants;
- confirm no answer depends on a volatile tool UI;
- confirm the case creates a build decision within 15 minutes;
- remove claims that cannot be supported without excessive lecture;
- run legal/privacy/identity/publication checks on supplied inputs.
