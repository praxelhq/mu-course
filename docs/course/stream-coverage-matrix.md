# Course 1 Career-Stream Coverage Matrix

**Course:** Masters' Union PGP — Applied AI for Business, Course 1  
**Draft date:** 17 July 2026  
**Status:** Approved coverage input, revised after adversarial review; not a lesson sequence  
**Purpose:** Ensure that one common, build-first course produces credible evidence for all nine Masters' Union career streams without becoming nine separate courses.

## Executive decision

The course should have **one operating core and nine career skins**.

Every student defines:

> **Target role × Representative work context × Consequential decision × Inspectable evidence**

Operator-mode students work on an internal or customer decision in a target employer. Advisor/investor-mode students work on a client, deal, or investment decision in the style of their target employer. They build one connected **Role-Ready AI Work Sample Portfolio**. The learning loop, supported tools, validators, deadlines, 70% common rubric, and ownership tests remain common. What changes by stream is the work context, case pack, data, decision, stakeholder, prototype form, 30% judgment overlay, and recruiter-facing entry point.

The portfolio promise is:

> “Here is a consequential decision I investigated for the role I want; here is the evidence; here is the AI operating prototype; here is where it fails and how I changed it; and here is the recommendation I would make.”

This is consistent with current employer signals. NACE's career-readiness framework asks graduates to demonstrate critical thinking, communication, teamwork, professionalism, leadership, and technology, and its critical-thinking behaviors explicitly include gathering diverse evidence, interpreting data, anticipating needs, and explaining decisions. The World Economic Forum continues to place analytical thinking first among employer core skills while AI and big-data skills are growing fastest. The 2026 Microsoft Work Trend Index describes advanced AI professionals not by tool familiarity but by multi-step agent use, routine workflow redesign, repeatable practices, and judgment about which human–AI collaboration mode a task needs. Sources: [NACE career-readiness competencies](https://www.naceweb.org/career-readiness/competencies/career-readiness-defined), [NACE critical thinking behaviors](https://www.naceweb.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/2025/career-readiness/nace-career-readiness-competencies-critical-thinking.pdf), [WEF Future of Jobs 2025 skills outlook](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/), and [Microsoft 2026 Work Trend Index](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization).

## Research basis and limits

The “authentic work” below is anchored primarily in current occupational-task frameworks rather than course-market copy:

- O*NET 2026 task profiles for [management analysts](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-1111.00), [general and operations managers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-1021.00), [financial and investment analysts](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-2051.00), [HR managers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3121.00), [logisticians](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-1081.00), [sales managers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/11-2022.00), [marketing managers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/11-2021.00), and [data scientists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-2051.00);
- the UK Government Digital and Data profession's [product-manager capability framework](https://ddat-capability-framework.service.gov.uk/role/product-manager), which defines product management around balancing user and business needs, outcomes, prioritization, roadmaps, and strategy;
- the [CFA Institute research-analyst role](https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/careers/research-analyst), [practice-analysis process](https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/practice-analysis), and [diligence and reasonable-basis standard](https://www.cfainstitute.org/standards/professionals/code-ethics-standards/standards-of-practice-v-a);
- the research-backed [SHRM Body of Applied Skills and Knowledge](https://www.shrm.org/credentials/certification/exam-preparation/bask), which combines HR expertise with ethics, business acumen, consultation, communication, relationship management, and critical evaluation.

O*NET is a US occupational system, not an India-specific placement taxonomy. Its task descriptions are used here as a structured check against invented “AI use cases,” not as a claim that every post-MBA role has identical scope. Company examples and available evidence are India-forward. Exact role expectations should later be checked against the Masters' Union batch profile, target-employer job descriptions, and recruiter interviews.

## The common core: 70–80% of the course

All nine streams practice the same durable capabilities and submit the same evidence types.

| Common capability | Common student action | Common proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frame value | Translate a broad employer/role interest into a work context, decision owner, decision, metric, constraints, and non-goals | Work Sample Brief and acceptance criteria committed before building |
| Research and source judgment | Build a source hierarchy, extract public evidence, distinguish fact/inference/assumption, and let evidence change a thesis | Source ledger, raw snapshot, claim ledger, counter-evidence |
| Work with data | Define metrics, clean a dataset, detect quality problems, analyze alternatives, and communicate uncertainty | Data dictionary, reproducible analysis, chart notes, decision memo |
| Context and grounded AI | In a controlled lab, construct a role knowledge base and test retrieval, citation, conflict, injection, and abstention behavior | Held-out questions, retrieval traces, failure-and-fix log; optional prototype component |
| Codify judgment | Turn a recurring role task into the bounded method layer of an operating prototype | Trigger/non-trigger tests, input/output contract, clean-session peer run |
| Build an operating prototype | Select an authentic app, workflow, grounded assistant, dashboard/model, or data surface; map deterministic/model/human work, approvals, logs, and recovery | Live or replayable run, failure injection, three to five LMS-assigned task attempts, release decision |
| Direct role-authentic communication | Experience multimodal direction, then select the stakeholder format appropriate to the stream; film is a public option, not universal | Brief, source/asset ledger where relevant, factual/claim audit, disclosure |
| Evaluate and own | Predict outcomes, use test cases, diagnose by layer, make an unseen change, and defend a trade-off | Eval set, checkpoint history, live modification, 90-second defence |

This reflects the empirical direction of current AI use. Anthropic's March 2026 Economic Index reports management-related use including investment memos and customer responses, and sales/outreach use including lead research, enrichment, qualification, and drafting. Its June 2026 report also observes financial-model/dashboard work and structured extraction/tagging. These are useful opportunity signals, but the course must grade business judgment and verification, not raw automation volume. Sources: [Anthropic Economic Index, March 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report) and [June 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report).

### Three universal objects, different recruiter entry points

Every learner submits a decision evidence brief, one role-authentic AI operating prototype, and a recruiter case page/proof pack. Components such as RAG, skill, workflow, dashboard, app, data pipeline, image, voice, and video are selected because the work requires them, not collected as separate trophies.

| Stream | Decision evidence brief | Valid operating prototype examples | Role-authentic public communication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consulting | transformation recommendation | diagnostic/recommendation workflow, option model, grounded client evidence assistant | executive client readout |
| FOCOS | CEO operating review | weekly review system, decision cockpit, owner/escalation workflow | CEO brief and action register |
| Product | opportunity/experiment brief | feedback triage, PRD/experiment assistant, narrow product prototype | demo and release decision |
| Finance | deal/IC/variance/capital-allocation memo | comps or diligence workflow, forecast model, exception review | deal-team, IC, or CFO readout |
| HR | workforce/skills decision brief | policy-grounded manager aid or planning model with appeal/human review | manager guidance and implementation note |
| Operations | process/root-cause brief | exception workflow, control-tower/model, SOP assistant | operating update and runbook |
| Sales | account/segment opportunity brief | research/qualification/account-review workflow with approval | account plan or manager call brief |
| Marketing | audience/positioning/experiment brief | campaign planning, creative-testing, claim/brand QA system | image/video variants and experiment decision |
| Data | analytical decision note | reproducible data-quality pipeline, notebook/model, metric assistant, dashboard | decision dashboard and data/model card |

A public grounded identity experience is optional and requires consent, disclosure, and safety gates. Famous-person cloning is not a universal artifact.

## Company and problem selection gate

A favourite company alone is not a viable project. The LMS should approve a proposal only when it passes all six gates:

1. **Role fit:** the problem belongs to work a candidate in the selected stream might plausibly do or influence.
2. **Decision:** there is a named decision-maker and a decision, not merely “use AI at Company X.”
3. **Evidence:** at least two primary sources, one comparison source, and one structured dataset or instructor-provided data analogue are available.
4. **Testability:** the prototype can be exercised through three to five LMS-assigned qualified task attempts; external access is optional enrichment.
5. **Safety:** the build does not require confidential records, personal data, unauthorized identity cloning, regulated advice, or high-impact automated decisions.
6. **Scope:** one core task can be demonstrated in under three minutes and evaluated with held-out cases.

Listed firms are often easier because NSE's [corporate filings directory](https://www.nseindia.com/companies-listing/corporate-filings-directory) exposes official disclosures; US-listed firms can use the SEC's [EDGAR submissions and XBRL APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces). India-wide evidence can come from the [Open Government Data Platform](https://www.data.gov.in/) and [RBI Database on Indian Economy](https://dbieold.rbi.org.in/DBIE/). Private companies are allowed only when sufficient public evidence exists. Where company-level operational data do not exist, the course supplies a clearly labelled synthetic case dataset calibrated to public aggregates; students may not present synthetic records as leaked or actual company data.

## Detailed stream cards

### 1. Consulting

**Authentic post-MBA work.** Management analysts gather and organize information, interview stakeholders, analyze data, develop alternatives, document recommendations, redesign procedures, and help new systems function successfully. That makes the authentic unit of work a defensible change recommendation—not a pretty deck. See [O*NET: Management Analysts](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-1111.00).

| Dimension | Course design |
| --- | --- |
| Company × function × problem samples | **IndiGo × customer operations × disruption-support triage**; **Tata 1mg × service operations × prescription-query escalation**; **Asian Paints × dealer operations × field-service knowledge workflow**. Students act as an external transformation team using public evidence and a labelled case-data pack. |
| AI-use opportunities | Rapid source mapping; interview-guide and hypothesis-tree critique; extracting evidence from filings/policies; qualitative coding; scenario analysis; process mining from event logs; grounded client knowledge assistant; proposal-quality skill; human-in-loop transformation workflow. |
| Recruiter-judgeable signals | Sharp issue definition; MECE-enough logic without ritualized framework theatre; quantified baseline and benefit logic; evidence/counter-evidence; implementation sequence, owners, risks, adoption plan; working proof that one redesigned step survives an exception; concise partner-style defence. |
| Realistic evidence | Annual reports, investor presentations, regulator/industry data, company policies/help pages, public customer complaints, expert interviews with explicit consent, and an instructor-supplied process/event log. Company facts and simulated client records must be visibly separated. |
| Simulation moment | The student receives a partner brief, three conflicting stakeholder notes, an operating dataset, and a deadline. Midway, the CFO rejects an assumption or a source is invalidated. The student must revise the recommendation, value case, and workflow rather than merely rewrite slides. |
| Risks and gaming | Generic “AI transformation roadmap”; invented client interviews; fabricated citations; frameworks pasted before evidence; benefits with no baseline; recommendation with no implementation; deck delegated end-to-end; hiding contradictory evidence. Counter with source liveness checks, pre-analysis hypothesis commit, random claim defence, and one live change to the operating proof. |

**Recruiter proof question:** “Show me the claim in your recommendation most vulnerable to being wrong, the evidence you used, and what you would measure in a pilot.”

### 2. FOCOS — Founders' Office / General Management / Chief of Staff / Strategy

**Authentic post-MBA work.** General and operations managers review financial and activity data, coordinate production/pricing/sales/distribution, allocate budgets and resources, set objectives and policies, monitor suppliers, and implement process changes. The FOCOS skin should therefore test cross-functional synthesis, prioritization, follow-through, and escalation—not executive-email drafting. See [O*NET: General and Operations Managers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-1021.00).

| Dimension | Course design |
| --- | --- |
| Company × function × problem samples | **Eternal × general management × new-city/category operating cadence**; **Zepto × Founders' Office × reduce dark-store launch variance**; **Freshworks × Chief of Staff × improve cross-functional enterprise-AI adoption review**. |
| AI-use opportunities | Build a live company brief; synthesize meetings and metrics into decisions; detect unresolved commitments; create a weekly business-review skill; automate owner/reminder/escalation flows; model scenarios; build a decision cockpit; ground a briefing assistant in approved company evidence. |
| Recruiter-judgeable signals | Separates signal from executive noise; links strategy to metrics and owners; makes explicit trade-offs; writes a short decision memo; designs a cadence that closes loops; knows what not to automate; produces an audit trail of changed priorities. |
| Realistic evidence | Filings, founder interviews and official blogs, product/pricing pages, public market indicators, competitor disclosures, and a synthetic weekly operating pack with finance/growth/ops/talent tables and stakeholder messages. |
| Simulation moment | A CEO inbox arrives with 20 items from sales, product, finance, legal, and operations. Budget funds only two initiatives. A late customer escalation invalidates one plan. The learner must prioritize, request missing evidence, assign owners, trigger the workflow, and write the decision note. |
| Risks and gaming | “AI Chief of Staff” that only summarizes; dashboards with no decision rights; confident fabricated internal metrics; treating every request as equally urgent; auto-sending executive communications; judging polish rather than follow-through. Require a before/after decision log, explicit deprioritization, unresolved-risk register, and an unseen next-week packet. |

**Recruiter proof question:** “Which initiative did you deliberately not recommend, and what new evidence would change your mind?”

### 3. Product Management

**Authentic post-MBA work.** The UK Government capability framework defines a product manager as balancing user and business needs to achieve outcomes, creating and prioritizing actionable goals, using roadmaps, and discussing product strategy at all levels. The course should test problem ownership and release judgment, not PRD generation. See [Government Digital and Data Product Manager](https://ddat-capability-framework.service.gov.uk/role/product-manager).

| Dimension | Course design |
| --- | --- |
| Company × function × problem samples | **Swiggy × consumer product × post-order issue self-service**; **CRED × member experience × explain rewards/value transparently**; **Myntra × discovery × improve confidence for a high-consideration purchase**. |
| AI-use opportunities | Cluster public feedback; build a source-grounded voice-of-customer simulator; draft and critique problem statements/acceptance tests; prototype an AI interaction; generate synthetic edge cases; instrument events; analyze sessions; convert validated evidence into backlog changes; automate feedback triage with human review. |
| Recruiter-judgeable signals | Clear user/job and non-goals; metric tree and event taxonomy; prototype tied to a hypothesis; three to five assigned usability/task attempts; observed failure and evidence-led release; model fallback and safety behavior; roadmap trade-off. |
| Realistic evidence | Product/help pages, consented user interviews, app-store review samples collected under platform rules, public pricing/terms, competitor flows, plus a supplied anonymized/synthetic funnel and session-event dataset. Public reviews generate hypotheses; they do not substitute for user research. |
| Simulation moment | An executive requests a fashionable AI feature while user evidence points to a smaller trust problem. Mid-test, the model gives a plausible but unsafe response. The learner must decide what to ship, change the acceptance criteria/fallback, and defend the release. |
| Risks and gaming | UI polish as product evidence; feature-factory PRD; five friends opening a URL; synthetic “user quotes” presented as real; fabricated analytics; cherry-picked positive sessions; chatbot pasted onto any product. Require task completion telemetry, consented observation notes, negative cases, release decision, and live response to a changed constraint. |

**Recruiter proof question:** “What did users do that contradicted your original product belief, and what changed in the release?”

### 4. Finance — Investment Banking / Venture Capital / Corporate Finance

**Authentic post-MBA work.** O*NET includes quantitative analysis, valuation, monitoring company/industry/economic developments, financial models, transaction materials, and recommendations. CFA Institute describes research roles as collecting company data, building earnings models, conducting valuation, and communicating recommendations, and requires diligence and an adequate research basis. See [O*NET: Financial and Investment Analysts](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-2051.00), [CFA research analyst](https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/careers/research-analyst), and [CFA Standard V(A)](https://www.cfainstitute.org/standards/professionals/code-ethics-standards/standards-of-practice-v-a).

| Dimension | Course design |
| --- | --- |
| Company × function × problem samples | **IB:** Tata Consumer × transaction advisory × acquisition/financing scenario and buyer materials. **VC:** a publicly evidenced Indian SaaS category × investment team × screen one target and frame diligence. **Corporate Finance:** Bharti Airtel × FP&A/capital allocation × prioritize a network/customer investment under scenarios. |
| AI-use opportunities | Extract and reconcile filings; normalize financial tables; research market/peer signals; generate but audit model scaffolds; explain variance; run scenarios/sensitivities; create a source-grounded filings assistant; build a diligence checklist skill; route new disclosures to model-review workflow with approval. |
| Recruiter-judgeable signals | Every material number traceable; model logic and assumptions visible; valuation/scenario range rather than one magic number; downside and red flags; distinction between fact, estimate, and judgment; decision-quality memo; no claim that the tool gives regulated advice. |
| Realistic evidence | NSE filings and iXBRL, annual reports, official investor presentations, RBI DBIE macro series, SEBI disclosures, SEC XBRL for global firms, and a labelled simulated data room. The SEC APIs explicitly expose filer submissions and XBRL financial-statement data: [EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces). |
| Simulation moment | A late disclosure conflicts with the model or changes a covenant/working-capital assumption. Students must locate the broken link, revise the model and recommendation, and present what changed to an IC/CFO/deal-team role. |
| Risks and gaming | Copied valuation template; hallucinated comps or transaction terms; circular formulas and hard-coded outputs; false precision; cherry-picked multiples; investment conclusion before diligence; undisclosed AI-generated research; public “buy/sell” recommendation. Require source cells, formula checks, sensitivity, independent recomputation, red-team memo, and oral defence of a random assumption. |

For regulated finance contexts, evaluation and accountability are non-negotiable. SEBI's current research-analyst framework makes the research entity responsible for security, confidentiality, integrity, AI-assisted output, and legal compliance; the classroom portfolio must be framed as an educational, independent analysis, not client advice. See [SEBI Research Analysts Master Circular, February 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/web/?file=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.sebi.gov.in%2Fsebi_data%2Fattachdocs%2Ffeb-2026%2F1770375507051.pdf).

**Recruiter proof question:** “Pick any important number in your recommendation and trace it from primary source through transformation to decision.”

### 5. Human Resources

**Authentic post-MBA work.** HR managers handle employee/management questions, staffing, compensation, workforce forecasts, training, performance systems, employee relations, and analysis of personnel data. SHRM adds ethics, business acumen, consultation, relationship management, communication, and critical evaluation. See [O*NET: Human Resources Managers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3121.00) and [SHRM BASK](https://www.shrm.org/credentials/certification/exam-preparation/bask).

| Dimension | Course design |
| --- | --- |
| Company × function × problem samples | **Infosys × talent management × skills mobility and learning allocation**; **Tata Steel × workforce planning × role/skill transition planning**; **Marriott India × HR operations × improve frontline onboarding and policy access**. |
| AI-use opportunities | Analyze anonymized workforce patterns; draft and test skills taxonomies; ground a policy/onboarding assistant; create a structured job-description review skill; triage employee requests without making final decisions; recommend learning pathways; build a manager decision-support interface with human approval and audit. |
| Recruiter-judgeable signals | Frames a business and employee outcome; documents lawful/minimal data use; tests disparate impact and proxy leakage; preserves human review and appeal; communicates uncertainty; creates a useful employee/manager workflow without automating high-impact judgment. |
| Realistic evidence | Annual-report workforce disclosures, public job descriptions, policy documents, labor-market data from government sources, consented interviews, and a synthetic/de-identified employee dataset with known bias traps. Never upload real resumes, performance reviews, health, compensation, or grievance data to an unapproved model. |
| Simulation moment | A workforce model recommends a group for intervention, but the data contain a historical-bias proxy and an employee challenges the recommendation. The learner must diagnose the issue, decide whether the tool may be used, add human/appeal controls, and explain the decision to an HR leader and employee representative. |
| Risks and gaming | “AI recruiter” that scores candidates; scraping personal profiles; real PII in prompts; fairness theatre based on one aggregate; synthetic employee quotes presented as real; policy assistant answering beyond sources; efficiency metric that ignores dignity and trust. Require a data sheet, prohibited-use list, subgroup/error checks, abstention tests, human decision record, and appeal route. |

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified in November 2025 with a phased enforcement timeline. The LMS must therefore treat data minimization, notice/consent context, access control, and deletion as build requirements, not an optional ethics slide. See [MeitY DPDP Rules 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa) and [commencement notification](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf).

**Recruiter proof question:** “Who could be harmed if this recommendation is wrong, and where exactly does accountable human judgment remain?”

### 6. Supply Chain and Operations

**Authentic post-MBA work.** Logisticians allocate materials, redesign movement to reduce cost and improve value, review performance against service agreements, coordinate departments and subcontractors, manage projects, and communicate solutions. See [O*NET: Logisticians](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-1081.00).

| Dimension | Course design |
| --- | --- |
| Company × function × problem samples | **Delhivery × network operations × delayed-shipment exception prioritization**; **Zepto × dark-store operations × stockout/expiry intervention**; **Asian Paints × supply chain × dealer-demand and replenishment exception review**. |
| AI-use opportunities | Extract SOPs; analyze event logs; forecast/scenario-assist with explicit baselines; summarize exceptions; create root-cause and corrective-action skill; route low-confidence cases to a planner; build control-tower interface; generate frontline training simulations; monitor runs and costs. |
| Recruiter-judgeable signals | Process map and bottleneck evidence; metric definitions for service, cost, quality, and risk; scenario trade-offs; distinction between deterministic rules and model judgment; exception handling; idempotency/retry; human override; run logs and measurable pilot target. |
| Realistic evidence | Filings, supplier/service disclosures, public trade/transport/weather data, data.gov.in, and instructor-supplied order/inventory/shipment/event tables calibrated to public aggregates. The [Open Government Data Platform](https://www.data.gov.in/) provides ministry/department datasets under India's Government Open Data License. |
| Simulation moment | A weather/supplier disruption lands while inventory and vehicle capacity are constrained. A malformed event triggers a duplicate action. Students must allocate resources, protect a service threshold, repair the workflow, and communicate the compromise to customer and finance stakeholders. |
| Risks and gaming | Clean toy optimization with no missing/late events; minimizing cost while ignoring service or safety; LLM used for deterministic arithmetic; impossible access to company telemetry; happy-path workflow screenshot; no operator override. Require dirty events, a constraint ledger, competing KPIs, failure injection, replay logs, and frontline/operator peer test. |

**Recruiter proof question:** “Show the exception where your automation should stop and a planner should take over—and why.”

**Authentic post-MBA work.** Sales managers monitor preferences and operating records, project sales/profitability, set territories/goals, coach teams, advise customers, resolve complaints, determine pricing/discounts, and coordinate service. See [O*NET: Sales Managers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/11-2022.00).

| Dimension | Course design |
| --- | --- |
| Company × function × problem samples | **IndiaMART × B2B sales × qualify SME leads and recommend next best action**; **Freshworks × enterprise sales × prepare evidence-grounded account plans**; **Razorpay × solution sales × route product-fit questions and human escalation**. |
| AI-use opportunities | Public account and category research; signal extraction; call preparation; role-play buyer simulator; transcript coaching; qualification/account-plan skill; product/objection RAG; workflow from approved signal to CRM task; draft follow-up gated by seller approval; pipeline-risk pattern detection. |
| Recruiter-judgeable signals | Evidence-backed account thesis; thoughtful discovery questions; listening and objection handling; accurate product claims/citations; clear qualification and next action; approval/opt-out boundaries; CRM hygiene; learning from a lost or disqualified opportunity. |

**Recruiter proof question:** “What evidence justified your next action—and what evidence made you decide not to pursue an account?”

### 8. Marketing

**Authentic post-MBA work.** Marketing managers identify and evaluate strategy, pricing and demand; analyze research and trends; forecast sales; coordinate product, distribution, and promotion; and assess financial implications. See [O*NET: Marketing Managers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/11-2021.00).

| Dimension | Course design |
| --- | --- |
| Company × function × problem samples | **Nykaa × brand/growth × test a new-segment launch proposition**; **Tata Consumer × brand × reposition an energy/functional beverage for a bounded audience**; **Myntra × growth × improve confidence and conversion for a category moment**. |
| AI-use opportunities | Research category and audience evidence; extract/cluster feedback; construct a brand/claims RAG; create insight-to-brief and claim-check skills; direct images/voice/video from a storyboard; generate coherent variants; localize with review; build experiment landing page; automate asset QA and reporting. |
| Recruiter-judgeable signals | Traceable insight → proposition → brief → creative; brand and factual consistency; multimodal direction rather than one-click generation; meaningful variant hypothesis; target-user response or behavioral test; unit/economic logic; honest interpretation of results. |
| Realistic evidence | Annual reports, product/catalog and packaging pages, official ad libraries where available, public search/category trends, consented interviews, platform-compliant review samples, and supplied campaign-performance data. Use public assets for a clearly labelled speculative educational project; do not imply brand approval. |
| Simulation moment | Two creative variants arrive. One wins initial preference but contains an unsupported product claim or violates a locked brand constraint. The learner must reject/repair it, predict a new variant, and choose an experiment metric that reflects the campaign objective. |
| Risks and gaming | Generic “AI slop”; copied reference campaign; unauthorized celebrity/voice clone; fabricated trend or performance data; likes as business impact; testing friends outside target; changing multiple variables; post-hoc hypothesis. Require asset/model ledger, claim citations, locked brief, pre-registered variant, qualified audience, disclosure, and brand audit. |

**Recruiter proof question:** “Which customer insight caused this creative choice, and what result would falsify your proposition?”

### 9. Data

**Authentic post-MBA work.** Data scientists identify business questions, clean and manipulate data, choose methods, compare and validate models, identify trends, visualize results, recommend solutions, and communicate to decision-makers. See [O*NET: Data Scientists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-2051.00).

| Dimension | Course design |
| --- | --- |
| Company × function × problem samples | **Bharti Airtel × customer analytics × diagnose ARPU/churn drivers and prioritize a retention test**; **PB Fintech × growth analytics × diagnose funnel anomalies**; **Delhivery × network analytics × detect service-level deterioration and likely drivers**. |
| AI-use opportunities | Create data dictionaries and profiling plans; generate/review transformation code; semantic search across data documentation; detect quality anomalies; assist exploratory analysis; propose but not select causal claims; write tests; build metric assistant; automate ingestion/quality alerts; ship a decision dashboard or constrained analytical app. |
| Recruiter-judgeable signals | Business question and metric contract; raw-to-clean lineage; reproducible code/workbook; tests and missingness handling; appropriate baseline; no target leakage; uncertainty and subgroup/error analysis; model/analysis choice justified; decision implication communicated plainly. |
| Realistic evidence | Company filings, RBI DBIE, TRAI/sector statistics, data.gov.in, SEC XBRL for global firms, and supplied row-level synthetic data matched to published aggregates. Raw, derived, and synthetic sources must be labelled in the repository. |
| Simulation moment | A dashboard spike appears. One stakeholder changes the metric definition while a hidden data leak makes a model look excellent. Students must identify the issue, revise the pipeline/model card, and tell the business whether a decision can safely be made. |
| Risks and gaming | Copied notebook; generated code not understood; target leakage; p-hacking; benchmark overfit; causal language from correlation; polished dashboard without decision; unversioned data; hidden manual edits. Require unit/data tests, data lineage, baseline comparison, held-out slice, random code/logic defence, and reproduction in a clean environment. |

**Recruiter proof question:** “What would make this analysis invalid, and which test in your repository detects it?”

## The common simulator engine with nine skins

The LMS should not implement nine unrelated simulators. Build one reusable **Business Decision Simulation Engine** with stream-specific content packs.

### Shared state machine

1. **Role and objective:** assign the learner a role, decision, stakeholder, metric, time limit, and boundaries.
2. **Initial evidence:** release a messy brief, primary sources, a structured dataset, and stakeholder messages.
3. **Prediction gate:** require a provisional hypothesis, confidence, missing evidence, and delegation plan before tools open.
4. **Build/action:** allow research, data analysis, skill/workflow/app execution, and a draft decision.
5. **Injected change:** reveal a source conflict, malformed record, stakeholder objection, policy constraint, service outage, or changed target.
6. **Repair:** learner diagnoses the layer, changes the build or recommendation, and records why.
7. **Defence:** system selects a random claim, number, branch, or decision for a short explanation.
8. **Transfer:** learner handles a parallel unseen case with less scaffolding.

### What the skin changes

| Simulator field | Common contract | Examples by skin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learner role | Named accountable operator | consultant, CoS, PM, analyst, HRBP, planner, account executive, brand manager, data analyst |
| Business outcome | Metric plus guardrail | value capture, priority, activation, IRR/risk, retention/fairness, service/cost, qualified progression, conversion/brand, forecast/error |
| Evidence pack | Primary source + structured data + stakeholder messages | filings, user feedback, data room, workforce data, events, CRM, campaign results |
| Failure injection | Plausible work failure | invalid citation, changed priority, unsafe answer, formula break, bias proxy, duplicate event, unsupported claim, brand violation, leakage |
| Human boundary | Approval/escalation/appeal | partner, CEO, release owner, IC/CFO, HR committee, planner, seller, brand/legal, data owner |
| Scoring | Common dimensions with role-specific anchors | framing, evidence, analysis, build reliability, business judgment, risk, communication, ownership |

### Scaling to 480 students

- Maintain one rubric and event schema; vary names, numbers, sources, and injected failures within each stream pack.
- Use deterministic checks for file presence, citations, formulas, schema, secrets, event completion, test results, and run logs.
- Use rubric-bounded model feedback for explanation/coaching, never as the only high-stakes score.
- Randomly sample oral defences and route suspiciously similar work for review.
- Compare students within common capability dimensions, not by whether one stream's output looks more visually impressive.
- Let students replay a parallel scenario after feedback; retain first-attempt and revision evidence.

## Evidence and dataset policy

Use a four-level evidence ladder:

1. **Primary public:** regulatory filings, official statistics, company investor documents, official product/policy pages.
2. **Independent public:** reputable research, market/industry sources, platform-compliant public feedback.
3. **First-hand collected:** consented interviews/usability sessions with method and sample limits disclosed.
4. **Simulated:** instructor-provided records needed to rehearse internal work; prominently labelled and calibrated to public facts.

Every published claim must be labelled as fact, calculation, observed user evidence, or assumption. Every dataset must include source, timestamp, collection method, fields, transformations, limitations, and license/usage note. Students may scrape only sources permitted by law, terms, and the course's allow-list; “the page was public” is not sufficient permission.

## Cross-stream anti-gaming controls

| Gaming path | Common control | Stream-specific anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Favourite-company fan fiction | selection gate and evidence feasibility score | reject problems requiring invented internal access |
| AI-generated polished artifact | grade checkpoints, failures, and unseen changes | recruiter proof question selected at random |
| Template laundering | templates allowed; adaptation is assessed | changed requirement/data schema after build |
| Fabricated company data | source/synthetic labels and primary-source checks | numbers must trace to source or course case pack |
| Fake users/interviews | consented evidence, qualified-task definition, telemetry | PM/marketing tests need target fit; sales calls are labelled simulations |
| Hidden teammate/outsourcing | individual prediction, commits, clean-room run, defence | individual transfer case even if build work is paired |
| Unsafe automation | explicit human authority, approval, escalation, logs | HR, finance, sales, and ops have mandatory stop conditions |
| Tool-choice theatre | common capability rubric | supported golden path; advanced tools earn no bonus by themselves |
| Brand/recruiter misrepresentation | mandatory disclaimer | “Independent speculative educational project; not affiliated with or endorsed by Company X.” |

## Assessment implication

The nine streams should not receive different grading standards. Use one capability rubric with role-specific performance anchors:

1. **Problem and business value** — clear decision, metric, guardrails, and realistic scope.
2. **Evidence and data** — source quality, provenance, analysis, uncertainty, and counter-evidence.
3. **AI direction and system design** — context, task allocation, skill/workflow design, and tool restraint.
4. **Reliability and risk** — tests, failures, recovery, permissions, privacy, and human control.
5. **User/stakeholder evidence** — qualified use, observed behavior, response to feedback.
6. **Communication** — role-appropriate recommendation and disclosure.
7. **Ownership** — prediction, decision trail, unseen modification, and defence.

The public portfolio can emphasize the stream-relevant lead artifact, but the assessor opens the same proof view: original brief, sources/data, build, tests, failure-and-fix, user evidence, decision, repository, and defence.

## Decisions to freeze before lesson planning

1. Approve **one common core + nine skins** as the delivery model.
2. Confirm whether stream choice is known before Course 1 begins; otherwise collect it in the diagnostic.
3. Define the company/problem approval gate and duplication caps.
4. Choose one golden-path example per stream and build the full evidence pack before accepting open company choice.
5. Decide which row-level datasets Praxy will synthesize/provide, because public company evidence alone cannot support authentic internal workflow practice.
6. Freeze the common portfolio proof view and disclaimer.
7. Freeze the shared simulation state machine and telemetry before designing individual simulation content.
8. Recruit at least one practitioner/recruiter per stream to review the golden sample and rubric anchors; this is validation, not nine-course co-design.

2. Fully prototype three high-contrast packs first: **Finance** (regulated/quantitative), **HR** (people/privacy), and **Marketing** (customer/creative). If one engine works credibly for these, the other six are less likely to expose a structural mismatch.

5. Only then map the ten lessons and their stream-specific inputs/outputs.
