# Course 1 Concept — AI for Business: The Role-Ready Operator

**Audience:** Masters' Union PGP  
**Format:** 10 × 120-minute live sessions; 480 students; eight sections  
**Portfolio home:** Praxy  
**Status:** Concept v2 for review

## The one-line pitch

Choose a role and employer context you care about; investigate one consequential decision, build one useful AI operating prototype, break and repair it, and publish a work sample a recruiter can inspect.

## Why this course exists

Most AI-in-business courses produce vocabulary, tool familiarity, and confident opinions. This course should produce evidence that a management student can:

- find a valuable and bounded AI opportunity;
- work with sources and data without outsourcing truth to a model;
- encode a method and connect it to a controlled operating system;
- choose an appropriate prototype form rather than defaulting to a chatbot/app;
- use AI heavily while retaining accountability;
- learn from observed failure and transfer the method to a new context;
- communicate the work in the language of a target role.

## Student story

> “I did not build a generic AI project. I performed a representative piece of work for the role I want. Here is the evidence, the operating prototype, the failure that changed it, the limit I would disclose, and the next decision I would recommend.”

The selected company creates motivation and recruiter relevance. It does not grant access to internal facts and does not have to be where the work occurs.

- **Operator mode:** an internal or customer decision in a target employer.
- **Advisor/investor mode:** a client, deal, or investment decision in the style of a target Consulting, IB, or VC employer.

## Nine career pathways

The course covers Consulting, FOCOS, Product Management, Finance, HR, Supply Chain and Operations, Sales, Marketing, and Data. Finance includes IB, VC, and Corporate Finance examples.

Seventy to eighty percent is a common operating core. Each pathway changes the role, decision, data, stakeholder, failure, prototype form, and recruiter entry point—not the learning standard.

| Stream | Representative work sample |
| --- | --- |
| Consulting | AI operating-model recommendation and diagnostic workflow for a client |
| FOCOS | CEO operating review and decision/owner/escalation system |
| Product | opportunity brief and narrow, instrumented prototype or experiment assistant |
| Finance — IB | transaction/buyer/comps decision and traceable diligence workflow |
| Finance — VC | investment memo and curated-data-room diligence assistant |
| Finance — Corporate Finance | variance/capital/forecast decision and controlled model/review workflow |

## What everyone leaves with

Three universal assessed objects:

1. **Decision evidence brief** — role-authentic, data-bearing, sourced, concise, and decision-ready.
2. **AI operating prototype** — one app, workflow, assistant, dashboard/model, data pipeline, or supported combination selected for the work.
3. **Recruiter case page and proof pack** — a fast public story backed by deeper evidence and a reproducible repository.

The pre/post performance mission measures growth and transfer but is not portfolio content.

Students still experience RAG, safe voice/identity, skills, automation, application building, images, and video. They do not collect all of them as seven mandatory trophies. Skill and workflow become layers of the operating prototype; apps and films are role-specific choices; a famous-person clone is never a universal public requirement.

## What happens in class

Every session uses the same grammar:

1. see a professionally surprising result;
2. predict before assistance;
3. open one mechanism;
4. build on supplied scaffolds;
5. face a plausible failure or changed requirement;
6. diagnose and repair;
7. transfer to an unseen variant;
8. ship inspectable evidence.

Cases such as Chegg, Klarna/customer support, Delphi/digital identities, Air Canada accountability, and Coca-Cola AI media create the tension for a build. They occupy minutes, not lectures. See the [anchor case bank](../research/2026-07-17-anchor-case-bank.md).

## The LMS concept

The LMS is an **AI Transformation Mission Room**, not a video library.

It provides:

- Work Sample Brief approval and curated fallbacks;
- staged role missions with persistent decisions and evidence;
- stream-specific sources, data, stakeholder personas, and hidden variants;
- prediction gates before tools open;
- structured research/data, grounding, method, workflow, prototype, communication and proof workspaces;
- injected failures and replay;
- a cross-section task-attempt exchange with role/input cards;
- deterministic validators, rubric-bound coaching, peer attack, trained-human primary scoring, moderation and double scoring;
- facilitator heat maps, exception queues, incident controls and section moderation;
- private proof and safe public portfolio views;
- pre/post performance missions, capability transcript and confidence calibration;
- printable/offline forms of every assessed decision.

Praxel's Market Research Simulator supplies useful design DNA: coherent stages, AI personas, persistent context, structured work, immediate feedback, a bias checkpoint, analysis lab, progress and executive defence. Course 1 adds explicit ownership/transfer, deterministic gates, stream overlays, safe publication, appeals and outage recovery.

## Assessment

| Component | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Decision evidence brief | 20% |
| AI operating prototype | 30% |
| Individual unseen transfer sprint | 25% |
| Recruiter case page and proof pack | 15% |
| Structured participation and peer attack | 10% |

Major artifacts use a 70% common capability rubric and a 30% career-stream overlay. One hundred percent of submissions receive mechanical checks and a trained-human primary score on bounded judgment evidence; every student also completes the individual transfer task. Human moderation covers every safety/borderline/appeal case, and at least 10% of every major component is double-scored across sections.

## Workload and infrastructure assumption

The complete concept assumes 20 live hours plus 8–12 structured asynchronous hours, including a separate low-stakes 25–35 minute post performance mission parallel to the pre measure. Most pre-work is capped at 30 minutes; testing and final proof receive longer windows. If 20 hours means total learner effort, the required portfolio reduces to the evidence brief and one prototype plus the in-class transfer test, and no performance-growth claim is made unless parallel pre/post time is retained.

Praxel/MU must provision one supported golden path for research/coding, Git, workflow, deployment/analytics, extraction, and media. Students do not purchase tools and advanced stacks earn no grade bonus. Starter repos, data, credentials, hidden tests and fallback outputs are infrastructure, not student prerequisites.

## Non-negotiable safeguards

- public, licensed, course-supplied synthetic, or explicitly consented evidence as labelled; no confidential company data;
- no confidential records or unauthorized scraping;
- no autonomous high-impact HR, finance, operations, sales or marketing action;
- explicit human approval, escalation or safe stop;
- no unauthorized living-person voice/likeness imitation;
- source, asset, model, template and AI-assistance provenance;
- public/private evidence split and prominent non-affiliation disclaimer;
- no grading advantage from social access, paid tools, coding background, visual polish or popularity.

## What changed from the COT v1.2 reference

Retained: 20 live hours, build-first rhythm, pre-work, cases, challenges/failures, portfolio, capability sprint, and final defence.

Changed: Rambo/rotating brands/live SME/40-day handover becomes one role-ready work sample; the real-owner reaction becomes assigned task evidence; the universal app/ad/clone set becomes one role-authentic prototype plus optional communication components; universal live viva becomes transfer plus stratified audit.
