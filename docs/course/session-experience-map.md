# Course 1 Session Experience Map

**Experience north star:** every class feels like entering a live business control room, not attending a tool workshop. The learner has a role, incomplete information, a consequential decision, a mechanism they can control, and evidence that persists into one recruiter-facing case.

## The ten-episode story

| Session | Cold open / “I need to know” | Student agency | Designed aha moment | Proof that leaves the room | Next-episode tension |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Find the leverage | A gorgeous “AI CEO copilot” collapses under four basic questions; a narrow system completes one defensible job | choose the role, decision, metric, boundary, evidence route, and thin system | impressive AI is often a feature with no owner; leverage begins with a consequential decision and acceptance test | approved/repaired Work Sample Brief, opportunity thesis, acceptance tests, authority boundary, transfer receipt | “What evidence would make this decision real rather than a story?” |
| 2 — Build the evidence engine | two confident executive claims compete; one survives a source trace and calculation, the other disintegrates | choose sources, construct a small dataset, reproduce a number, label fact/inference, and decide what may be claimed | deep research is not more pages; it is a claim system where a skeptical person can replay the path | decision brief v1, data dictionary, calculation trace, claim ledger, counter-hypothesis | “Can a system use this evidence without pretending to know what is absent?” |
| 3 — Make AI know—not pretend | base model, naïve RAG, and evaluated grounding answer the same questions; the fluent system is compromised by one trusted-looking document | curate the corpus, set trust/freshness rules, write answer/abstention contract, inspect traces, and repair retrieval/generation failure | RAG is a governed evidence interface, not a truth button; knowing when not to answer is a product behavior | corpus manifest, grounded component/config, evaluation set/results, injection repair, disclosure | “How do I make this judgment repeatable by someone other than me?” |
| 4 — Package judgment | a heroic mega-prompt wins once and fails fresh; a bounded method survives literal use by a peer | define trigger/non-trigger, input/output schemas, steps, checks, stop conditions, and run tests | a prompt that works for its author is not an operating method; interfaces and tests turn tacit judgment into a reusable skill | method/skill package, four execution traces, peer clean-session result, hidden-change repair | “What real event should trigger this method, and who approves what happens next?” |
| 5 — Make the system act | a fast automation duplicates a consequential action while the “slower” controlled workflow stops safely | design event schema, states, branches, idempotency, approvals, logs, retry/recovery, and escalation | automation value comes from controlled exceptions, not the happy-path demo; safe stops are successful outcomes | workflow/control specification or working run, event/decision log, duplicate/malformed/policy failure repair | “What is the smallest form in which a real user can operate this system?” |
| 6 — Ship the right prototype | the class chooses among a chatbot, form, dashboard, workflow, and replay for the same job—and the flashiest form loses | select the role-authentic form, assemble the evidence/method/execution layers, deploy or create a deterministic replay, instrument one task | “build an app” is not the objective; the interface is an adapter to a decision and its evidence | prototype v1, task run, telemetry/replay receipt, human boundary, fresh assigned attempt | “What do users actually do when the author is not there?” |
| 7 — Earn evidence from task attempts | a prototype with five-star opinions shows abandonment at approval and one unsafe completion in its logs | perform assigned task/attack, classify observed failures, select one change and one deliberate non-change, repair and rerun | compliments are weak evidence; observed behavior and failure severity change release decisions | qualified attempt pack, failure taxonomy, before/after rerun, prototype v2, release rationale | “How should this evidence be communicated to the stakeholder who must act?” |
| 8 — Communicate for the role | the same evidence becomes a CFO note, frontline runbook, and short campaign film; then an AI-generated version invents a claim | lock evidence, choose stakeholder/action/format, create, absorb a changed constraint, run a blind stakeholder scan, repair | communication format is part of the operating system; creativity cannot outrun the claim lock | role-authentic readout, claim/asset/rights ledger, changed-constraint revision, stakeholder-fit receipt | “What does a recruiter need to understand in 90 seconds—and what proof must stay behind it?” |
| 9 — Make the work recruiter-legible | a recruiter scans two versions of identical work for 20 seconds; the tool museum loses to the proof story | decide public/private/excluded evidence, build scan and proof views, run secrets/rights/link checks, survive a blind scan and random proof request | a portfolio is a retrieval interface for judgment; a smaller verifiable claim beats a larger unsupported one | public case page, private proof pack, frozen manifest/repo, 90-second defence receipt | “Can I still operate when the requirement changes and my familiar workspace disappears?” |
| 10 — Operate under pressure | a sealed mission changes a material requirement; no project chat, memorized prompt, or collaborator can carry the learner | interpret the change, predict affected layers, inspect evidence, modify the minimum responsible layer, rerun tests, recommend and defend | expertise is not artifact memory; it is diagnosis, bounded change, verification, and honest escalation under transfer | unseen clean-session transfer pack, final recommendation, proof defence, capability transcript/next practice plan | course closes with evidence of transfer, not a showcase-only finale |

## Repeated experience grammar without repetition fatigue

The learning loop repeats because prediction, failure, repair, and proof are the ownership rail. The emotional form changes:

- Sessions 1–2 use **exposure**: a polished claim or project is revealed as empty.
- Sessions 3–5 use **system betrayal**: a plausible AI system fails through evidence, method, or control.
- Sessions 6–7 use **contact with reality**: form choice and user behavior overturn builder assumptions.
- Sessions 8–9 use **audience pressure**: stakeholders and recruiters cannot see what the author believes is obvious.
- Session 10 uses **transfer pressure**: the familiar context is removed and durable operating judgment must remain.

## Engagement is not gamification

The Mission Room may visualize stages, unlocks, personas, heat maps, and receipts. It rewards only consequential behavior:

- a prediction made before help;
- a weak claim removed;
- an abstention or escalation used correctly;
- a failed test diagnosed at the right layer;
- a repair that improves a rerun;
- counter-evidence that changes a recommendation;
- a public claim made smaller and more defensible;
- a transfer completed without copying the original artifact.

No XP is awarded for logins, word count, number of prompts, model choice, visual polish, or unverified external engagement.

## What students should say afterward

Not: “We covered prompting, RAG, agents, no-code, and AI video.”

But:

> “I learned how to choose a decision worth augmenting, build an evidence trail, encode and control the work, ship the right interface, learn from failure, and prove the judgment. I can show you the system, the mistake that changed it, and the boundary I would not automate.”
