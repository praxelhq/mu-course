# Course 1 Architecture v2 — AI for Business: The Role-Ready Operator

**Programme:** Masters' Union PGP — Applied AI for Business, Course 1  
**Scale:** 20 live hours, 10 sessions, 480 students, eight sections  
**Status:** Working course contract for review  
**Last updated:** 17 July 2026

## North-star transformation

Students enter as capable AI users who can ask for outputs. They leave as credible AI operators who can frame an ambiguous business problem, ground AI in evidence, encode judgment, connect and deploy a system, test it with people, diagnose failure, and defend the business and technical choices.

The course is not certified by tool familiarity. It is certified by inspectable work and transfer under an unseen change.

## Course promise

> Choose a role and employer context you genuinely care about. Investigate one consequential decision, build one useful AI operating prototype, and publish a work sample that a recruiter can open, test, and question.

Every student project is defined as:

> **Target role × Representative work context × Consequential decision × Inspectable evidence**

The selected company is the motivating context, not always the organization where the work occurs.

- **Operator mode:** target employer × target function × internal/customer decision. Used for FOCOS, Product, Corporate Finance, HR, Operations, Sales, Marketing, and Data.
- **Advisor/investor mode:** target employer × target role × client/deal/investment decision. Used for Consulting, Investment Banking, and Venture Capital.

“I am building something for Nike” is not a work sample. “As a category manager at Nike India, I am testing whether a grounded discovery prototype can reduce choice overload for first-time runners” is. A consulting candidate might instead advise a retail client; an IB candidate might evaluate a buyer set; a VC candidate might decide whether an opportunity advances to investment committee.

## Primary learner and starting point

- Management students across nine placement streams with uneven technical confidence.
- Comfortable with documents, presentations, spreadsheets, and common consumer software.
- No coding prerequisite; advanced students receive harder failure and integration variants rather than a different grade ceiling.
- Students may use AI heavily. The assessment asks whether they can specify, inspect, challenge, adapt, and own AI-assisted work.

## Required career streams

1. Consulting
2. FOCOS — Founders' Office, General Management, Chief of Staff, Strategy
3. Product Management
4. Finance — Investment Banking, Venture Capital, Corporate Finance
5. Human Resources
6. Supply Chain and Operations
7. Sales
8. Marketing
9. Data

This is one common-core course, not nine mini-courses. Each stream receives equivalent problem cards, datasets, simulation variants, portfolio examples, and recruiter proof. The capability, time, scaffolding, and evidence bar remain common.

## Scope

Students learn to:

- frame AI opportunities around decisions, jobs, risk, and measurable value;
- research and extract public evidence, work with messy data, and separate fact from inference;
- prompt with explicit context, constraints, schemas, and quality bars;
- build and evaluate a grounded knowledge or stakeholder interface;
- encode a recurring method as a reusable skill;
- turn the method into a triggered workflow with branches, approval, logging, and recovery;
- direct an AI coding agent to ship a narrow deployed application with telemetry;
- observe qualified users, diagnose failure, and make an evidence-led release;
- direct image, voice, and video systems from a business brief;
- use Git, provenance, tests, and a decision log to make work reproducible;
- present a concise recommendation and defend choices under an unseen change.

## Explicit non-goals

- Surveying every major AI tool.
- Teaching production software engineering or model training.
- Producing implementation-ready advice for the selected company.
- Requiring confidential company access, internal data, or employee interviews.
- Grading students on aesthetic taste, follower counts, company prestige, or paid tools.
- Creating unauthorized clones of living people or publishing deceptive synthetic media.
- Running nine separate curricula.

## Portfolio promise

The final deliverable is one **Role-Ready AI Work Sample Portfolio**, hosted on Praxy with a public recruiter view and a deeper proof view.

### Public recruiter view

In under two minutes a recruiter can see:

1. the desired role, work context, decision owner, and consequential decision;
2. the evidence-led opportunity thesis;
3. a working system or recorded live run;
4. one meaningful failure-and-fix story;
5. qualified task-attempt or evaluator evidence;
6. the student's individual contribution and next recommendation;
7. links to the proof view and repository.

Every page carries: **Independent educational project; not commissioned by or affiliated with the company. Public, licensed, course-supplied synthetic, or consented evidence as labelled; no confidential company data.**

### Three universal assessed objects

| Object | Student output | Required ownership proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision evidence brief | role-authentic 1,000–1,500 word brief or six-screen equivalent; evidence map, dataset, original calculation, claim ledger, counter-hypothesis, recommendation | reproduce one number, defend one vulnerable claim, and explain what changed the thesis |
| AI operating prototype | one deployed or replayable app, workflow, grounded assistant, dashboard/model, data pipeline, or supported combination | live run, human–AI allocation, failure injection, assigned task attempts, evidence-led revision, unseen clean-session modification |
| Recruiter case page and proof pack | 60–90 second public scan backed by private/public repository evidence | failure-and-fix story, provenance, disclosure, live links, evaluator-selected claim/component defence |

The pre/post performance mission is assessed but is not a portfolio object.

### Components students experience but do not all publish

- **Research, scraping, data, and article writing** merge into the decision evidence brief.
- **Skill and workflow** merge into the method and execution layers of the operating prototype.
- **An application** is one prototype form, not a universal UI requirement.
- **Grounded RAG and safe voice/identity work** are a common controlled studio and an optional prototype component. Famous-person cloning is never a universal public artifact.
- **Image/video work** is a common studio experience and a role-authentic public option: core for Marketing, useful for Sales/Product/FOCOS, and practice or an explainer option elsewhere.
- **Git and the proof pack** remain a continuous evidence rail.
- **Three to five LMS-assigned task attempts** replace mandatory external users. Real target users are optional enrichment, not a grading advantage.

## Project-selection contract

Students submit a one-page Work Sample Brief before Session 1. The LMS approves it only if all gates pass.

| Gate | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Career legibility | names one of nine streams, a plausible target role, operator/advisor/investor mode, and hiring audience |
| Work authenticity | names a role-authentic context, consequential decision, decision owner, output, and boundary of authority |
| Evidence feasibility | sufficient legal public evidence or a course-provided synthetic data pack |
| Build feasibility | a useful thin system can be prototyped without company credentials or privileged access |
| Test feasibility | the course task-attempt exchange can issue three to five qualified role-card attempts; external access is not required |
| Safety | no high-stakes autonomous action, sensitive personal data, deception, or unauthorized likeness |
| Differentiation | if a company/problem is duplicated, the role, user, thesis, or mechanism differs materially |

Students who miss the gate choose from curated challenge cards. No one loses a week searching for a project.

## The course grammar

Every session follows the same learning loop:

1. **See the impossible:** a short professional-quality result creates desire.
2. **Commit a prediction:** the student records what should happen before assistance.
3. **Open one mechanism:** the instructor makes one controllable idea visible.
4. **Build with scaffolds:** starter assets remove account and setup waste.
5. **Break it deliberately:** a realistic source, input, policy, API, or user failure appears.
6. **Repair and explain:** students diagnose the layer rather than reprompt blindly.
7. **Transfer:** a changed requirement or unseen case removes imitation as a strategy.
8. **Ship evidence:** artifact, test, decision, and provenance enter Git/Praxy.

Cases occupy 10–15 minutes and create a consequential decision. They do not replace the build.

## Headline ten-session arc

The stream coverage matrix supplies nine variants for each common mechanism.

| Session | Promise | See | Do | Leave with | Prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Find the leverage | turn role interest into a defensible AI opportunity | an apparently attractive AI idea fails when the real work and decision are exposed | run a disruption/opportunity simulation and lock the Work Sample Brief | opportunity thesis v0 and baseline evidence | reject a wrong brief and frame an unseen one |
| 2. Build the evidence engine | make AI research auditable and data-bearing | a polished deep-research answer collapses against a source/number audit | source, extract, structure, analyse, and revise a thesis | dossier v1, dataset, claim ledger | reproduce a number and resolve a planted contradiction |
| 3. Make AI know—not pretend | ground an interface in evidence and safe boundaries | base model, RAG system, and manipulated corpus answer differently | run a controlled corpus lab; add citations, abstention, and disclosure; optionally test safe voice | evaluated grounded component that may enter the prototype | pass held-out, contradictory-update, injection, and disclosure tests |
| 4. Package judgment | turn a good method into a reusable prototype layer | the same task succeeds in a clean session without its author | specify, build, and red-team a stream method | prototype method layer and test report | trigger, non-trigger, messy input, unseen change |
| 5. Make the system act | turn the method into a controlled execution layer | a trigger completes work, then safely stops on an exception | map event/state/action, add approval, logging, and recovery | prototype execution layer and run log | survive failure injection and explain the human boundary |
| 6. Ship the right prototype | choose the form that best represents the target role | the same narrow job appears as app, workflow, dashboard/model, assistant, or data surface | write acceptance tests, build on a supported shell, deploy or make replayable, instrument | operating prototype v1 | a fresh assigned tester completes the core task and the system fails safely |
| 7. Earn evidence from task attempts | learn more from observed failure than friendly praise | behavior and task evidence contradict self-reported feedback | complete the LMS-assigned task exchange, attack peers' systems, classify failures, prioritize one release | three to five qualified attempts, issue trail, prototype v2 | justify what changed and what was deliberately not changed |
| 8. Communicate for the role | turn evidence into a stakeholder narrative, not AI spectacle | one evidence base becomes different role-authentic outputs | direct images/voice/video in a controlled studio, then produce the most appropriate communication format | role-authentic communication layer; film where relevant | preserve facts, claims, disclosure, and stakeholder fit under a changed constraint |
| 9. Make the work recruiter-legible | convert a pile of work into a concise proof story | the same portfolio is scanned by different hiring lenses | publish public/proof views, harden repo, rehearse challenge questions | live portfolio and recruiter walkthrough | pass privacy/provenance checks and a random claim defence |
| 10. Operate under pressure | transfer the method to an unseen change and defend the system | a late source, policy, schema, or user incident threatens the case | diagnose, modify, rerun tests, present the transformation case | final portfolio, operating snapshot, maturity post-mission | unseen sprint plus three-minute stack defence |

## Studio and rail structure

- **Intelligence Studio — Sessions 1–3:** frame and make AI know something.
- **Systems Studio — Sessions 4–5:** make judgment reusable and make AI act.
- **Operating Prototype Studio — Sessions 6–8:** make one role-authentic system people can use, test, and understand.
- **Evidence Rail — Sessions 1–10:** Git, provenance, evals, decisions, failures, privacy, and clean-room checks.
- **Growth Rail — pre-course, studio gates, Session 10:** parallel performance missions and confidence calibration.

## Assessment doctrine

Artifact success and student ownership are scored separately. Polished output cannot compensate for inability to diagnose or change it.

| Component | Weight | Evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Decision evidence brief | 20% | role-specific research/data judgment, original calculation, counter-case, recommendation |
| AI operating prototype | 30% | working/replayable system, human boundary, reliability, task attempts, revision |
| Individual unseen transfer sprint | 25% | clean-session adaptation and explanation on a parallel case |
| Recruiter case page and proof pack | 15% | role legibility, provenance, failure-and-fix story, disclosure, live proof |
| Structured participation and peer attack | 10% | completed reviews and evidence-specific feedback, not popularity or airtime |

### Common rubric dimensions

1. Problem framing and business value
2. Evidence and verification discipline
3. System design and tool/model choice
4. Execution, reliability, and human boundary
5. User evidence and iteration
6. Communication and defence
7. Provenance, safety, and ownership

Popularity, leaderboard rank, raw number of prompts, commit count, and visual polish are not grading dimensions.

### Maturity assessment

The pre/post instrument has three layers:

- a short scenario-based literacy and judgment bank;
- parallel messy-business performance missions scored on C1, C2, C8, and C9;
- confidence recorded beside key decisions to measure calibration.

The baseline is low-stakes and not released with its solutions. The post-course mission changes domain and surface details. Growth is reported separately from the course grade.

## Participation and decorum

Participation means observable operating contribution, not airtime.

Expected behaviors:

- arrive with pre-work and accounts/files ready;
- respect build windows and do not interrupt live demos with setup issues handled in the support lane;
- give evidence-specific peer feedback and test classmates' work in good faith;
- disclose AI assistance and sources; do not fabricate users, citations, logs, or company affiliation;
- use only course-authorized identity assets and public, licensed, course-supplied synthetic, or explicitly consented evidence as labelled;
- never paste confidential, personal, credential, or proprietary data into course tools;
- challenge ideas and systems, not people; consent before recording or publishing user evidence;
- maintain a fallback export of important work.

## Delivery and workload contract

The 20 live hours protect at least 11 hours for student building, testing, and review. The complete portfolio also requires structured work outside class.

**Planning assumption:** 20 live hours plus 8–12 hours of asynchronous work across the course. Pre-work is capped at 30 minutes for most sessions; task-attempt testing and portfolio finishing receive longer windows. This assumption must be confirmed with Masters' Union. If 20 hours is the total student effort, the required portfolio reduces to the evidence brief and one prototype plus an in-class transfer test; recruiter-page polishing becomes optional.

## LMS experience requirements

The LMS must:

- run the Work Sample Brief gate, including operator/advisor/investor mode, and allocate fallback challenge cards;
- issue stream-specific cases, datasets, personas, and hidden variants;
- collect predictions before assistance and preserve immutable checkpoints;
- launch staged simulations with persistent consequences and replay after feedback;
- attach repository, deployment, workflow, and telemetry evidence;
- run deterministic validators for structure, links, citations, tests, secrets, PII, and required events;
- use rubric-bound AI feedback for coaching, never as the sole high-stakes judge;
- run a cross-section task-attempt exchange with role cards, consent, supplied inputs, observable task events, and assigned failure prompts;
- support calibrated peer attacks, trained-human primary scoring of all judgment-bearing graded evidence, moderation of all flags/borderlines/appeals, and cross-section double scoring of at least 10% of every major component;
- show facilitator heat maps and exception queues across eight sections;
- publish only after provenance, consent, privacy, and independent-project gates pass;
- display capability growth separately from XP, completion, and grades;
- retain a low-tech classroom fallback for every assessed simulation.

## Tool policy

- One supported golden path per capability; optional advanced lane.
- Tool choices are dated delivery configuration, not curriculum outcomes.
- No paid account unless centrally provisioned.
- Starter repos, corpora, synthetic data, credentials, deployment, analytics, and validators are course infrastructure.
- Exact tool stack freezes shortly before delivery and has tested fallback assets.

## Instructor and facilitator consistency

Each session package must include a 120-minute plan, demo script, student brief, nine stream cards, starter/solution assets, simulator, quiz, rubric link, failure injection, setup fallback, advanced lane, telemetry, and a facilitator calibration note.

Before release, a second reviewer runs the learning-asset validator. Blockers and majors are fixed; minors are logged. Each section submits a 24-hour pulse covering timing, failure modes, confusing instructions, tool reliability, and outcome evidence so the next section can receive a controlled revision.
