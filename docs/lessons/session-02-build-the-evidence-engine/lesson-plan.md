# Session 2 Lesson Plan — Build the Evidence Engine

**Duration:** 120 minutes  
**Studio:** Intelligence Studio  
**Primary capability:** C2 — build a reproducible evidence chain from sources to structured data to claims  
**Supporting capabilities:** C8 — diagnose and repair under contradiction; C9 — communicate a defensible recommendation  
**Canonical outcome IDs:** L02-O1–L02-O6  
**Shared mechanism:** **The Claim Chain: question → source → extraction → structured data → calculation → claim → decision**  
**Portfolio increment:** evidence dossier v1 + dataset/data dictionary + claim ledger + revised opportunity thesis

## Planning assumptions

- Session 1's Work Sample Brief is approved, or the student receives an equivalent curated stream case.
- Students completed a three-source plan but are not assumed to know deep research, scraping, spreadsheets, code, or citation managers.
- The course provides an allow-listed, cached source pack and a structured extraction shell for every stream; open-web work is an optional lane, not a dependency.
- Exact browser, research model, extractor, spreadsheet/notebook, and repository tools remain replaceable capability slots.
- The common output is a small auditable evidence chain, not a finished 1,000–1,500 word decision evidence brief.

## Session promise

> By the end of this session, you will produce one decision-relevant claim that another person can reproduce from a live or cached source through a structured dataset and original calculation—and you will revise it when a planted contradiction appears.

## Prerequisites

- approved Work Sample Brief v1/v1.1 with decision, metric, guardrail, and evidence plan;
- two primary-source candidates and one independent comparison source, or assigned course pack;
- LMS, repository, and evidence-ledger access;
- spreadsheet literacy at basic sort/filter/formula level is helpful but not required;
- acknowledgement of source, scraping, copyright, personal-data, and synthetic-data rules.

## Materials

### Student-facing

- Source Triage Card: provenance, date, primary/independent/lead-only, directness, limitations, permission/terms;
- Claim Chain worksheet and structured evidence ledger;
- cached source pack with stable page/section locators;
- extraction table template with required raw-value and normalized-value fields;
- small stream dataset containing at least one missing value and one benign formatting inconsistency;
- calculation template in spreadsheet form and optional notebook equivalent;
- claim ledger template: claim ID, exact wording, claim type, source locator, transformation, caveat, status;
- contradiction envelope issued after checkpoint;
- peer number-reproduction checklist and unseen transfer card.

### Instructor-facing

- a polished deep-research memo containing a real-looking but unsupported citation, a denominator error, and a stale number;
- source pages and calculation sheet that expose the failures;
- one complete golden Claim Chain;
- three annotated student anchors: supported, weak/overstated, unsupported;
- section heat map, source allow-list, licensing/terms notes, and incident guide.

### Capability slots

- source discovery/browser and cached-source reader;
- source extraction/scraping capability restricted to allow-listed content;
- structured spreadsheet and optional notebook analysis;
- claim/evidence ledger;
- citation/link/number validators;
- repository checkpoint and version comparison.

## Minute-by-minute run of show (120 minutes)

**Protected student build/review/transfer time: 75 minutes** (minutes 29–68 and 75–111).

| Time | Mode | Activity | Outcome/evidence |

| 118–120 | Exit | State: “I believed __; evidence __ changed/did not change it because __; uncertainty remains __.” Record confidence. | C9.2 + calibration |

## Instructor demo script

**Demo goal:** collapse confidence in polished research without creating cynicism about AI-assisted research.

1. Display a six-sentence executive memo with citations and one chart. Ask: “Would you forward this?” Students commit yes/no and confidence.
2. Open the first cited page at the exact locator. Highlight that it supports market growth, not the memo's causal claim.
3. Open the spreadsheet behind “conversion improved 25%.” Reveal values 20% and 25%: the memo reports a 25 percentage-point increase or a 25% relative increase incorrectly. Recompute the correct 5 percentage points / 25% relative lift and name both.
4. Reveal that the chart combines calendar-year and financial-year data without disclosure.
5. Repair only one claim live:
   - restate the question;
   - choose primary source;
   - extract raw values with units/period;
   - calculate visibly;
   - write a narrower claim;
   - add one caveat and decision implication.
6. Ask the same “Would you forward this?” question. The repaired output looks less sweeping but is auditable.
7. Close: “Deep research finds leads. The Claim Chain earns the right to make a decision.”

## Common student brief

### Your role

Continue as the role-holder from Session 1. Your stakeholder wants an evidence-backed first view before authorizing any build.

### Your task

Build one complete Claim Chain that could alter your consequential decision:

1. express the decision as three answerable research questions;
2. triage at least three sources, including one primary and one independent/contrast source;
3. choose the smallest 5–12-row/field dataset needed for one decision-relevant claim;
4. preserve a raw snapshot and create a data dictionary;
5. calculate one original metric/comparison/scenario with visible formula;
6. write the claim, its type, exact support, caveat, and decision implication;
7. state one counter-hypothesis;
8. resolve the injected contradiction and record what changed;
9. revise opportunity thesis v0 to v1.

### Definition of done

A peer can reproduce the number from the supplied sources without asking you what you did, and the claim remains true after the contradiction is handled.

## Checkpoints and validators

| Gate | When | Deterministic checks | Judgment check | Retry rule |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Source plan | 43 | ≥3 sources; source type, owner, date, locator, permission note | directness and decision relevance | one coached retry |
| Dataset | 57 | 5–12 rows/fields; raw/normalized/unit/period/source/transformation present | fields can answer the question | revise before calculation |
| Calculation | 68 | formula visible; no hard-coded result; units/denominator/rounding present | calculation is decision relevant | one repair retry |
| Pre-failure freeze | 75 | version and checkpoint captured | claim wording calibrated | no edits to frozen version |
| Contradiction repair | 99 | both sources retained; conflict class and resolution recorded | revision is justified | unresolved is acceptable if honest and decision adjusted |
| Peer reproduction | 106 | reviewer answer and variance recorded | unsupported wording flagged | author may make one correction |
| Transfer | 111–118 | individual, timed, complete | correct broken link and calibrated claim | feedback only in class |

Validators verify structure and arithmetic properties, not strategic truth. A trained human scores high-stakes interpretation for every frozen evidence brief; moderation and double scoring protect reliability.

## Injected failure

One contradiction is assigned per student from an equivalence blueprint:

- a later official source corrects the earlier number;
- sources use different denominators, geographies, cohorts, or time periods;
- a secondary summary overstates what the primary source says;
- units are thousands versus millions or percentage versus percentage points;
- a synthetic row is duplicated/transposed;
- a source is real but stale for the decision.

The learner must classify the conflict as **date, definition, scope, provenance, or arithmetic**, update all downstream links, and preserve the losing evidence with status. An honest “unresolved; do not make the decision yet” can outperform false reconciliation.

## Ownership and transfer test

The LMS gives each learner an unseen two-source pack and one assertive claim. In seven minutes the learner must:

1. identify the exact unsupported or contradictory link;
2. quote/paraphrase the source no more strongly than it supports;
3. specify the correct denominator/period/transformation for one calculation;
4. rewrite the claim and decision implication;
5. record confidence and one missing evidence item.

No research model is available in the transfer window. Source-reader and basic calculation capability remain available.

## Likely misconceptions and responses

| Misconception | Evidence in work | Facilitator response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Citation presence equals support | URL exists but locator/wording mismatch | require exact support and claim-type label |
| A search snippet is a source | snippet cited without opening page | mark lead-only; open primary source or remove claim |
| More sources means stronger research | long bibliography, weak chain | ask for minimum evidence that could change the decision |
| AI extraction is the data | no raw snapshot or lineage | preserve raw; compare sample rows; record transformation |
| Correlation proves the proposed intervention | causal language from observational comparison | add counter-hypothesis and narrow claim |
| Percentage points and percent are interchangeable | ambiguous lift wording | make baseline, denominator, and both units explicit |
| Contradiction should be averaged away | sources combined without definition check | classify conflict before reconciling |
| Synthetic data proves a company fact | simulated row-level result stated as actual | label simulation and limit claim to scenario learning |

## Differentiation and accessibility

### Scaffold lane

- pre-highlighted locators, 8-row extraction shell, formula menu, and claim sentence frames;
- spreadsheet-first path with no code;
- source pack uses accessible HTML/text plus tagged PDF excerpts;
- facilitator checks question–field alignment before calculation.

### Advanced lane

- programmatic extraction of an allow-listed source with reproducible script;
- second independent calculation or sensitivity;
- automated citation-support check evaluated against manual ground truth;
- harder contradiction involving restatement or definition drift; same grade ceiling.

### Accessibility requirements

- every scanned/PDF source has OCR text and page/section locator; charts have alt text and underlying table;
- spreadsheet templates are screen-reader structured with named ranges and no color-only status;
- demo is captioned with a step transcript and downloadable calculations;
- keyboard-only source-to-ledger workflow is supported;
- students may submit calculation narration as text, audio, or captioned video alongside the formula;
- approved time accommodations apply to transfer; source complexity, not reading speed, is the construct;
- cached low-resolution/plain-text source pack supports low bandwidth.

## Rubric link and session application

Use the [common rubric dimensions](../../course/course-1-architecture-v2.md#common-rubric-dimensions) and [stream anchors](../../course/stream-coverage-matrix.md#detailed-stream-cards). The session mastery score follows the accepted 70/30 common-plus-stream rule.

- **70% common:** source judgment/provenance 15; dataset lineage/dictionary 15; original calculation/reproducibility 20; calibrated claim/counter-case 10; contradiction diagnosis and transfer 10.
- **30% stream anchor:** the evidence question, metric/calculation, caveat, and decision implication are authentic for the selected role/sub-track.
- Fabricated sources, concealed synthetic data, or a material number that cannot be traced blocks progression until repaired.

- run extraction and formulas on pass, missing-value, unit-error, and contradiction cases;
- confirm each stream and IB/VC/Corporate Finance sub-track has equivalent calculation difficulty;

- calibrate supported/overstated/unsupported claim anchors;
- preload the demo and cached pages; never depend on a live external site;
- inspect Session 1 evidence plans and assign scaffold/advanced lane without changing standards;
- prepare a short data/source ethics reminder and escalation path for questionable scraping.

### Ten minutes before

- hide contradiction files and transfer packs;
- confirm formulas calculate in offline spreadsheet mode;
- open demo memo and exact source locators;
- check dashboard for students on curated cases and accessibility alternatives;
- write the session question: **“Can another person reproduce the claim that drives your decision?”**

## Failure recovery, no-network path, and setup fallback

| Failure | Immediate recovery | Evidence parity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student sources are weak/broken | switch to the same-stream cached source pack; retain own question if feasible | same Claim Chain and rubric |
| Scraper/extractor unavailable | manual copy into extraction shell with source locator and screenshot/text snapshot | extraction method is not graded |
| Research model unavailable | use keyword/source map and cached pages; AI discovery is optional | judgment and evidence unchanged |
| Spreadsheet/notebook issue | use provided offline workbook or printed calculation grid; calculator allowed | formula/note and peer reproduction retained |
| LMS unavailable | local ledger/workbook plus numbered contradiction envelope | timestamps recorded by facilitator; bulk import later |
| Repository unavailable | export a zipped/printed evidence bundle with manifest and timestamp | attach checkpoint after restoration |
| Whole-room network loss | run cached demo, local source packs, offline spreadsheets, printed transfer cards | all core outcomes remain assessable |
| External source changes mid-class | use immutable cached source version and log the live change as an instructor incident | no student penalty |

Infrastructure failures are not misclassified as missing evidence discipline.

## Async follow-up and Session 3 bridge

**Required, target 25–30 minutes:**

1. repair flagged evidence and freeze dossier v1.1;
2. expand the claim ledger to three consequential claims: one fact/calculation, one inference, and one assumption to test;
3. choose 3–6 short, legally usable documents/excerpts that a grounded interface could use in Session 3;
4. write five candidate questions: two answerable, one absent, one time-sensitive/conflicting, and one unsafe/out-of-authority;
5. complete delayed retrieval items on source support, denominator, and synthetic-data claims.

Students unable to provide a safe corpus use the stream-specific course corpus. Session 3 never requires scraping or uploading confidential material.
