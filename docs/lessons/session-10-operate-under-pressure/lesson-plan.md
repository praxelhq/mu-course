# Session 10 Lesson Plan — Operate Under Pressure

**Duration:** 120 minutes live  
**Course position:** Final Growth Rail and Executive Defence Arena  
**Primary capabilities:** C8, C9; transfer of C1–C6  
**Canonical outcome IDs:** L10-O1–L10-O5  
**Assessment contribution:** Individual unseen transfer sprint, 25% of course grade; final portfolio freeze  
**Shared mechanism:** **Detect → diagnose → decide → modify → verify → defend**  
**Protected individual work time:** approximately 82 minutes  
**Assessment posture:** Assistance is reduced, surface context changes, and every student works individually in a clean session. AI use remains permitted and declared; access to the original long chat/project context and classmates is not permitted.

## Session promise

> Your portfolio proves what you built with scaffolds. This session proves what you can do when the company, evidence, and failure change. By the end, you will diagnose an unseen business-system problem, make the smallest defensible modification, rerun the critical test, and defend your decision with evidence and honest limits.

## Observable lesson outcome

Given a parallel but unseen stream case containing a changed company/context and seeded defect, the student will independently frame the decision, identify the failing layer, modify a supplied or frozen role-authentic system in a clean workspace, verify the result, state the human boundary, and defend the recommendation using cited evidence.

## What “clean session” means

- a new LMS mission/workspace with no prior chat memory, hidden prompt, or personalized agent context;
- student may use the frozen Session 9 proof bundle, approved tools, supplied reference material, and their own notes;
- student may not open the original build conversation, ask classmates, use another person's artifact, or contact an external helper;
- AI assistance is expected but must begin from the new brief and be declared;
- the case uses a different company or surface context and an equivalent stream decision;
- the student must make one substantive change, not merely write an explanation;
- accessibility tools and approved accommodations remain available and are not treated as assistance violations.

## Complete output contract

Every student submits one immutable **Unseen Transfer Packet**:

1. **Case identity:** variant ID, stream/subvariant, new company/context, decision owner, decision, time, supplied evidence/data versions, and allowed resources.
2. **Pre-assistance commitment:** provisional diagnosis, expected system behavior, decision quality bar, safety/human boundary, missing evidence, planned delegation between human/model/deterministic logic, and confidence.
3. **Evidence and diagnosis:** exact source, row, formula, log, policy, requirement, user event, or output that indicates failure; failure layer; business/user consequence; alternative explanation considered.
4. **Substantive modification:** versioned patch to one or more of the method, context, source handling, schema, formula, branch, approval, fallback, test, interface, or recommendation. The student marks supplied, AI-generated, adapted, and personally decided elements.
5. **Verification:** before/after or fail/fix/retest evidence on the hidden/equivalent case; result classified pass, partial, fail, or correct safe escalation; regression/guardrail check where supplied.
6. **Decision note:** role-authentic recommendation, action/owner, expected value, uncertainty, remaining risk, and next evidence needed. No claim exceeds the supplied evidence.
7. **Defence card:** a 90-second accessible response structure—decision, proof opened, modification, result, human boundary, limitation, next step—plus links to the exact evidence.
8. **Calibration:** confidence after verification and one sentence explaining whether confidence moved for a valid reason.
9. **Integrity and provenance:** new workspace ID, assistance declaration, frozen output/checkpoint, validator version, and incident/accommodation record if applicable.

**Minimum pass:** identifies a material problem, makes a substantive relevant change, verifies against an equivalent test, maintains a safe human boundary, and gives an evidence-linked recommendation. Correct refusal or safe escalation can pass when action would exceed authority or evidence.

- Complete access check for the clean workspace and fallback files.

### LMS/program team

- Preload accessible/offline equivalents and incident relief rules.
- Lock collaboration only for the individual sprint window; do not block approved assistive technologies.
- Assign every frozen packet to a calibrated primary scorer and prepare a stratified 10% blind-double-score seed before scores are visible; add all flags/borderlines to moderation.

## Instructor neutral demo script: “One mechanism, no answer leakage”

**Demo length:** five minutes. Use a toy inventory CSV unrelated to all assessed cases.

1. Show a workflow that confidently recommends reordering every duplicated row.
2. Ask students to predict whether a better prompt or evidence inspection should come first.
3. Open the event IDs and expose duplicate records created by a schema mismatch.
4. Name the layer: data/schema plus deterministic idempotency, not “hallucination.”
5. Add a duplicate-ID check, rerun, and show one remaining partial failure.
6. Lower confidence and escalate the remaining ambiguous record instead of claiming success.
7. Model the defence sentence: evidence, change, result, boundary, limitation.

Do not demonstrate any stream variant, data, hidden defect, or answer form used in the assessment.

## Student task brief

You are operating in a new context. Your job is not to recreate your portfolio or produce the most polished answer. Your job is to demonstrate control.

1. Read the role, decision, authority, evidence, and quality bar.
2. Predict before asking AI or running the system.
3. Inspect the evidence and locate the failing layer.
4. Make the smallest substantive change that addresses the consequence.
5. Rerun the critical test and one guardrail where available.
6. Recommend, refuse, or escalate within your authority.
7. State what remains uncertain.
8. Be ready to open the exact evidence under challenge.

AI can draft or modify. You remain accountable for the evidence selected, change accepted, test interpreted, and decision defended.

## Misconceptions and facilitator responses

| Misconception | Response |
| --- | --- |
| “Clean session means no AI.” | AI is allowed. Clean means no hidden prior context and reduced scaffolding; control and provenance are assessed. |
| “I should rebuild my original project for the new company.” | Transfer the mechanism needed for the decision; do not recreate the entire portfolio. |
| “The validator failed, so my business judgment is wrong.” | Mechanical checks and judgment are separate. Inspect the criterion and incident class. |
| “I should repair every issue.” | Prioritize the defect that changes the consequential decision or safety boundary. |
| “High confidence signals competence.” | Confidence should track evidence. A justified decrease can improve calibration. |
| “Safe refusal looks weaker than a completed output.” | Correct refusal/escalation is strong ownership when action exceeds evidence or authority. |
| “A polished defence can rescue a failed test.” | Defence explains evidence; it cannot substitute for artifact success or transfer. |

## Assistance, integrity, and fairness rules

- AI assistance is allowed through the supported clean workspace and is declared automatically/manually.
- Similar outputs are not automatic misconduct because cases and templates are shared.
- Integrity signals create a human review queue; final decisions use provenance, test performance, modification evidence, and defence.
- Accessibility support, extra time, text-to-speech, speech-to-text, screen readers, and approved alternatives are permitted resources.
- Platform/model failure is classified separately from learner error and may trigger reset, equivalent variant, deadline relief, or score correction.
- No style detector or “AI detector” determines authorship or grade.

## Differentiation

The final sprint uses equivalent difficulty, not different grade ceilings.

### Supported core

- structured brief, supplied data/evidence, one supported system shell, explicit output schema, known test plus hidden-equivalent test.
- no hints after prediction except tool/incident support.

### Advanced variant

- denser distractor, second regression check, or more subtle business trade-off—not extra infrastructure.
- advanced technical implementation earns no bonus unless it improves the same evidence criteria.

## Accessibility and inclusion

- Cases, datasets, logs, validators, and defence prompts are keyboard/screen-reader accessible and downloadable.
- Text, audio, or captioned-video defence formats are equivalent; live speech speed and accent are not scored.
- Charts include data tables; images include text alternatives; no color-only failure signals.
- Approved timing accommodations receive equivalent variants and secure pause/resume.
- Public posting is not required for the sprint; transfer evidence stays private by default.
- Offline paper/spreadsheet path tests the same diagnosis, decision, patch specification, and verification logic.
- Accommodation data never appears in performance comparison or audit sampling criteria.

## Transfer rubric: 70% common + 30% stream judgment

This rubric determines the 25% individual unseen transfer component.

Canonical references: [course common rubric](../../course/course-1-architecture-v2.md#common-rubric-dimensions) and [stream judgment anchors](../../course/stream-coverage-matrix.md#detailed-stream-cards).

| Common criterion | Points | Demonstrated evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Reframing and prediction | 10 | decision owner, quality bar, guardrail, delegation plan, and calibrated prediction fit new context |
| Evidence and diagnosis | 15 | exact evidence identifies material failure layer and consequence; plausible alternative considered |
| Substantive adaptation | 20 | relevant method/system/recommendation change is personally selected and versioned |
| Verification and human boundary | 15 | before/after test, regression/guardrail, correct approval/escalation, honest remaining risk |
| Defence, provenance, and calibration | 10 | opens proof, explains trade-off and assistance, states limits, confidence tracks evidence |
| Stream judgment overlay | 30 | role-specific anchor in the variant table |
| **Total** | **100** |  |

Essential rules:

- no substantive change or no verification: cannot exceed Developing;
- unsafe high-impact action, fabricated evidence, or hidden sensitive data: score held for review and publication quarantined;
- correct safe refusal/escalation with strong evidence is eligible for full credit;
- documented platform incident triggers equivalent retry/relief, never silent zero;
- speaking style, visual polish, speed beyond the declared time window, and tool complexity are not scored.

## Feasible human-scoring and defence model for 480 students

### What happens to every student

- 100% complete the individual unseen sprint and structured defence card.
- 100% receive deterministic checks on required evidence, test outputs, versions, safety, and provenance.
- 100% complete a peer executive rehearsal; peers do not assign grades.
- 100% receive trained-human primary scoring on the bounded transfer packet.

### Human scoring and moderation

- A trained scorer reviews every packet using the 70/30 transfer rubric and cites prediction/diagnosis/patch/test/defence evidence.
- Review all safety/integrity flags, score borderlines, contested feedback and appeals in moderation.
- Use the bounded packet and structured defence response; the assessment operations baseline budgets approximately eight minutes per first pass.
- Blind-double-score at least 10% of all transfer packets—48 course-wide—stratified across section, stream/subvariant, starting band and score band.
- Live defence can be used for sampled/flagged cases, but a universal three-minute viva is prohibited: 60 students would require 180 minutes before transitions.
- Where a live change is requested, issue one equivalent micro-change and record result; do not reveal hidden case keys to later sections.

AI may generate questions or summarize evidence but cannot be the sole high-stakes judge. Students can contest criterion-level feedback through the frozen evidence and appeal route.

## Failure recovery and fallback

| Failure class | Immediate response | Assessment treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LMS/model outage before case opens | issue sealed offline pack and local workspace | same clock after recovery; incident recorded |
| Outage during run | preserve checkpoint; use precomputed execution log or equivalent deterministic rerun | objective preserved; no platform penalty |
| Validator defect | freeze submission, override false negative, rerun against corrected version | score reproducible from frozen evidence |
| Case/data defect | pause affected variant and issue calibrated alternate | original attempt excluded from grade |
| Hosting failure | use supplied replay/shell; assess modification/test, not vendor uptime | no penalty |
| Unsafe input/output | stop, quarantine, issue sanitized alternate if exposure was platform-caused | safety decision retained; wellbeing protocol followed |
| Network/power loss | section-wide fallback within five minutes; adjust clock | incident-based relief |

## Facilitator preparation and calibration

Before Session 10:

- run every represented stream/subvariant and confirm equivalent information volume, repair demand, time, and scoring;
- verify the new company/context differs from students' portfolios and original chat is unavailable;
- validate cases against pass, partial, distractor-following, unsafe, accessibility, outage, and safe-stop paths;
- rehearse only the neutral toy demo;
- freeze validator, rubric, case, data, and sample seeds;
- calibrate with common and stream anchors, including all three Finance variants;
- confirm clean-workspace access, offline packs, assistive technology, incident relief, and secure submissions;
- assign a trained primary scorer to every frozen sprint packet and pre-assign moderation plus a stratified 10% blind double-score sample;
- establish hidden-test containment for later sections;
- brief reviewers on 90-second defence and live micro-change rules; and
- publish appeals, score-release, public-portfolio update, and deletion/unpublish procedures.

## After-class follow-up

### Required

- No strategic revision to the frozen sprint unless an approved incident creates an equivalent reassessment.
- Resolve mechanical, safety, access, or publication issues through the documented repair path.
- Respond to a moderator/defence request if flagged or sampled for double scoring; accessible written, recorded or live modes are equivalent where speaking is not the assessed outcome.
- Review capability transcript and calibration feedback when released.
- Update the public recruiter view only after the transfer result is audited and the claim remains accurate; the private proof view retains the full record.

### Program follow-up

- Within 48–72 hours, administer the separate low-stakes Part A post-literacy form and 30-minute Part B performance mission on the learner's counterbalanced parallel assignment. These are maturity evidence, not part of the 25% sprint grade; Session 10 confidence/results do not substitute for them.
- Complete all primary scoring, moderation, double scoring, incident correction, and appeals before final grade release.
- Compare transfer gaps across section, stream, starting band, and accessibility path without exposing accommodation status.
- Retire broken variants, record model/tool drift, and publish a 24-hour delivery note for any remaining section.
- Invite optional portfolio publication; do not make public identity or external engagement a grading condition.

## Course completion statement

The student completes Course 1 only when the record distinguishes:

- **completed:** artifacts submitted;
- **demonstrated:** known contracts passed;
- **verified:** failure/hidden tests and ownership evidence passed; and
- **transferable:** equivalent unseen work completed with reduced scaffolding.

The portfolio shows professional evidence. Session 10 establishes whether the operator can carry the method into the next business context.
