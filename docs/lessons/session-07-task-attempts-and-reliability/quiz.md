## Entry / in-lesson diagnostic

### S07-D-01A — Which attempt counts?

- **Version/staleness:** v0.1; review 17 October 2026; durable.
- **Outcome:** C6; task-attempt integrity.
- **Purpose/demand:** formative diagnostic; diagnose.
- **Stream/context:** Product Management; family `S07-D-01`.
- **Difficulty/time:** easy; 45 seconds.
- **Prompt:** Which record is a qualified task attempt for a post-order support prototype?
- **Options:**
  - A. Twenty classmates opened the link.
  - B. A friend said the colors looked professional.
  - C. An assigned user-role tester used a supplied damaged-order case, attempted the defined resolution task, and the system recorded completion/fallback evidence.
  - D. The author replayed the demo video five times.
- **Correct answer:** C.
- **Rationale:** A qualified attempt binds a target role, supplied task/input, frozen prototype, observable state, and evidence.
- **Distractors:** A is traffic, B is preference, and D is author activity; none proves an independent core task.
- **Feedback:** “Count task attempts by role, input, behavior, and result—not clicks, praise, or author activity.”
- **Accessibility:** no visual interpretation; “fallback” means the safe alternate path.

### S07-D-01B — Parallel retake

- **Version/staleness:** v0.1; review 17 October 2026; durable.
- **Outcome:** C6; task-attempt integrity.
- **Purpose/demand:** equivalent diagnostic retake; diagnose.
- **Stream/context:** Human Resources; family `S07-D-01`.
- **Difficulty/time:** easy; 45 seconds.
- **Prompt:** Which record is a qualified attempt for a policy/onboarding assistant?
- **Options:**
  - A. An assigned employee-role tester uses a supplied policy question, the assistant cites/abstains, and the human-review or appeal state is recorded.
  - B. The assistant receives fifty greetings.
  - C. The author reports that colleagues “loved it.”
  - D. The page receives ten likes.
- **Correct answer:** A.
- **Rationale:** A observes the intended job and its safety boundary using a defined role and input.
- **Distractors:** B–D measure activity or opinion without task, evidence, or boundary behavior.
- **Feedback:** “A valid attempt tests the declared work and human boundary—not engagement volume.”
- **Accessibility:** plain text; no personal employee data implied.

## Delayed-retrieval Form A

### S07-R-01A — Evidence, not validation theatre

- **Version/staleness:** v0.1; review 17 October 2026; durable.
- **Outcome:** C6.
- **Purpose/demand:** delayed retrieval; apply.
- **Stream/context:** Product Management; family `S07-R-01`.
- **Difficulty/time:** medium; 60 seconds.
- **Prompt:** Four assigned testers use a checkout-support prototype. Two complete, one abandons at the approval screen, and one safely escalates an unsupported case. Which conclusion is best?
- **Options:**
  - A. The market has validated the product because four people used it.
  - B. The attempts show formative evidence: inspect approval friction, preserve correct escalation, and avoid demand claims.
  - C. The safe escalation should be removed because it lowers completion rate.
  - D. Average the four satisfaction ratings and ignore task states.
- **Correct answer:** B.
- **Rationale:** Small assigned attempts reveal task/reliability issues, not market demand; correct safe escalation is success for an out-of-scope case.
- **Distractors:** A overclaims validation. C optimizes completion over safety. D substitutes opinion for behavior.
- **Feedback:** “Use small task attempts to choose the next repair. Do not infer adoption or remove a correct safety boundary.”
- **Accessibility:** completion states are textual; no statistics required.

### S07-R-02A — Prioritize by consequence, not complaint count

- **Version/staleness:** v0.1; review 17 October 2026; durable.
- **Outcome:** C6, C8.
- **Purpose/demand:** delayed retrieval; analyze.
- **Stream/context:** Consulting; family `S07-R-02`.
- **Difficulty/time:** hard; 90 seconds.
- **Prompt:** Five client-role attempts produce: three comments that the report font is small; one run where an invalid source changes the benefit estimate; one request for an extra export format. What should the consultant prioritize first?
- **Options:**
  - A. Add the export because it is a new feature request.
  - B. Fix and rerun the source/benefit logic because it can change the recommendation; also log the readability issue for accessibility repair.
  - C. Fix only the font because it appears three times.
  - D. Make no change because five attempts are too few for statistical significance.
- **Correct answer:** B.
- **Rationale:** Decision consequence and reliability outrank raw frequency; readability still matters and should not be discarded.
- **Distractors:** A follows novelty. C uses frequency alone. D confuses “not market validation” with “no useful iteration evidence.”
- **Feedback:** “Prioritize with consequence, risk, recurrence, and effort. Small samples can still expose a decisive failure.”
- **Accessibility:** explicitly acknowledges the small-font accessibility issue; no visual judgment needed.

### S07-R-03A — Safe response to a harmful incident

- **Version/staleness:** v0.1; review 17 October 2026; durable; policy wording should be reviewed with institutional guidance.
- **Outcome:** C8; reliability/risk.
- **Purpose/demand:** delayed retrieval; evaluate.
- **Stream/context:** Human Resources; family `S07-R-03`.
- **Difficulty/time:** hard; 85 seconds.
- **Prompt:** During a tester attempt, an HR prototype uses a location field as a proxy and recommends lower-priority training for one group. What is the best immediate response?
- **Options:**
  - A. Hide the output from the tester and continue collecting attempts.
  - B. Stop the affected decision path, preserve restricted incident evidence, remove/review the proxy and data need, add human/appeal controls, then rerun subgroup tests before use.
  - C. Ask the model to make the language more neutral.
  - D. Keep the system because a human can ignore bad recommendations later.
- **Correct answer:** B.
- **Rationale:** The issue is potential biased decision logic and data use, not tone; the safe path stops harm, preserves evidence, repairs controls, and retests.
- **Distractors:** A conceals risk. C edits presentation rather than mechanism. D treats vague human oversight as a substitute for designed accountability.
- **Feedback:** “Stop the harmful path, diagnose data and decision logic, preserve accountable review/appeal, and retest before release.”
- **Accessibility:** no sensitive real-person data; direct language; “proxy” means a field standing in for a protected or sensitive characteristic.

## Parallel Form B / retake

### S07-R-01B — Evidence, not validation theatre

- **Version/staleness:** v0.1; review 17 October 2026; durable.
- **Outcome:** C6.
- **Purpose/demand:** parallel retake; apply.
- **Stream/context:** Human Resources; family `S07-R-01`.
- **Difficulty/time:** medium; 60 seconds.
- **Prompt:** Three assigned manager-role testers use a workforce-policy aid. One gets a cited answer, one triggers correct abstention and HR review, and one cannot find the appeal path. Which conclusion is best?
- **Options:**
  - A. The aid is validated for all employees.
  - B. Keep the correct abstention; repair appeal-path findability and rerun; report only three formative attempts.
  - C. Remove human review to increase completion.
  - D. Publish the tester names as proof.
- **Correct answer:** B.
- **Rationale:** B uses observed evidence precisely, preserves the safety boundary, and targets the actual friction without overclaiming.
- **Distractors:** A overgeneralizes. C weakens accountability. D violates privacy and adds no task evidence.
- **Feedback:** “Report the small evidence honestly and improve the observed task failure without erasing correct safeguards.”
- **Accessibility:** no identity required; appeal path is explained by context.

### S07-R-02B — Prioritize by consequence, not request volume

- **Version/staleness:** v0.1; review 17 October 2026; durable.
- **Outcome:** C6, C8.
- **Purpose/demand:** parallel retake; analyze.
- **Stream/context:** Sales; family `S07-R-02`.
- **Difficulty/time:** hard; 90 seconds.
- **Prompt:** Four seller-role attempts yield two requests for a different layout, one unsupported security claim in a drafted follow-up, and one request for more account news. What is the first release priority?
- **Options:**
  - A. Repair the claim-verification/approval path and rerun the risky follow-up case.
  - B. Add more news because data volume looks impressive.
  - C. Redesign the layout because two people mentioned it.
  - D. Auto-send more messages to collect a larger sample.
- **Correct answer:** A.
- **Rationale:** An unsupported external claim creates high relationship and compliance consequence; it outranks cosmetic frequency.
- **Distractors:** B adds input without fixing truth. C uses count alone. D scales the unsafe behavior.
- **Feedback:** “Fix the highest-consequence mechanism first, then rerun the exact risky case.”
- **Accessibility:** no sales jargon needed; external claim risk is explicit.

### S07-R-03B — Safe response to an operating incident

- **Version/staleness:** v0.1; review 17 October 2026; durable.
- **Outcome:** C8.
- **Purpose/demand:** parallel retake; evaluate.
- **Stream/context:** Supply Chain and Operations; family `S07-R-03`.
- **Difficulty/time:** hard; 85 seconds.
- **Prompt:** A delayed-shipment event is delivered twice while the supplier service times out. The workflow creates two intervention tasks. What is the best response?
- **Options:**
  - A. Add a longer model prompt explaining duplicates.
  - B. Pause the external action, classify orchestration/idempotency and service failure, add duplicate control plus timeout fallback, then replay with the same event ID.
  - C. Delete one task manually and call the run successful.
  - D. Ignore it because duplicate work is reversible.
- **Correct answer:** B.
- **Rationale:** The failure belongs to orchestration/external service control; idempotency and fallback must be repaired and proven by replay.
- **Distractors:** A assigns a deterministic control to a model. C removes the symptom without fixing the system. D understates operational consequences.
- **Feedback:** “Repair by layer: control duplicate events deterministically, handle timeout, and prove recovery in the log.”
- **Accessibility:** “idempotency” is explained in the feedback as preventing repeat action for the same event.

## Scoring, threshold, and retake

- Entry diagnostic is unscored and drives a five-minute reteach if fewer than 80% choose the qualified attempt.
- Delayed Form A: three items, one point each, no partial credit.
- **3/3:** ready. **2/3:** Developing; assign repair on evidence versus preference or priority. **0–1/3:** prerequisite repair and private facilitator alert.
- Students below 3/3 receive item feedback, complete the targeted repair card, then take Form B once.
- Store first and retake separately; latest informs readiness. Neither can compensate for missing task attempts, unsafe behavior, or failed rerun.
- Option order may randomize; family pair and scenario facts may not be mixed.
- Retrieval mastery is 90% across delayed checks, not one perfect quiz.

## Item-quality checks

- Two reviewers independently answer all items; any disagreement triggers rewrite.
- Verify A/B equivalence for evidence volume, consequence visibility, reading load, and cognitive demand.
- Confirm Product, HR, Consulting, Sales, and Operations are represented as substantive professional work.
- Review the HR incident item with privacy/fairness guidance before delivery; date-stamp any policy-specific change.
- Check distractors remain clearly inferior without depending on hidden tool knowledge.
- Accessibility review: plain text, no color-only/spatial cues, defined specialist terms, compatible with screen readers and extra time.
- Pilot analysis: difficulty, time, omission, discrimination, distractor function, and differential section/stream performance where sample size permits.
- Version and retire; never silently alter a live scored item.
