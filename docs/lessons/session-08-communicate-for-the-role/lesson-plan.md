# Session 8 Lesson Plan — Communicate for the Role

**Duration:** 120 minutes live  
**Course position:** Final Operating Prototype Studio session; follows evidence-led release in Session 7  
**Primary capabilities:** C7, C9; supporting C2, C6, C8  
**Canonical outcome IDs:** L08-O1–L08-O5  
**Assessment contribution:** Communication layer inside the AI operating prototype and recruiter proof  
**Shared mechanism:** **Evidence → audience → action → format → proof**  
**Protected student-work time:** approximately 86 minutes  
**Important boundary:** Every student experiences controlled multimodal direction. A video is public graded evidence only when it is role-authentic; it is never the universal deliverable.

## Session promise

> The same evidence should not become the same deck, memo, dashboard, demo, or film for every job. By the end of class, you will turn your verified release evidence into the communication artifact your target stakeholder actually needs—without weakening the facts, hiding uncertainty, or mistaking spectacle for persuasion.

## Observable lesson outcome

Given a verified release result, a named stakeholder, and a changed communication constraint, the student will select and produce a role-authentic format that preserves consequential claims, makes the requested decision/action clear, meets disclosure/accessibility requirements, and survives a blind stakeholder-fit review.

## Complete output contract

The Session 8 submission is a **Role Communication Pack** with:

1. **Communication contract:** target stakeholder, decision/action required, context they already know, likely objection, time/attention limit, channel, accessibility need, confidentiality level, and explicit non-goal.
2. **Format decision:** selected role-authentic artifact and a 50–100 word rationale. Students must state why an image, voice, or video layer is or is not useful.
3. **Evidence lock:** three consequential claims or numbers linked to exact evidence, transformation/calculation where relevant, permitted language, uncertainty, and claim owner.
4. **Artifact v1:** produced in the stream format below. It may be a memo, six-screen readout, demo, dashboard narrative, runbook, account plan, IC/CFO note, manager guide, or film/creative system.
5. **Constraint card and revision:** the LMS reveals one changed audience, time, brand, legal, risk, data, or accessibility constraint. The student records the predicted consequence, revises the artifact, and names what must remain unchanged.
6. **Multimodal evidence where used:** storyboard/layout, prompt/direction notes, asset/model ledger, rights/consent status, synthetic-media disclosure, captions/transcript, and alt text. Generic voice or consent-cleared assets only; no unauthorized identity cloning.
7. **Stakeholder-fit test:** one blind peer receives only the role card and artifact. They identify the decision/action, strongest evidence, material limitation, and unanswered question. The author may not coach them.
8. **Final artifact and changelog:** role-authentic v2, 100-word decision note, public/private status, repository/checkpoint ID, and AI assistance declaration.

**Minimum pass:** stakeholder/action is unambiguous; consequential claims remain traceable; changed constraint is handled; required disclosure/accessibility is present; the format is plausible for the role. Visual polish cannot compensate for an unsupported claim or wrong stakeholder action.

## Instructor demo script: “One evidence base, three decisions”

**Demo length:** eight minutes.

1. Start with a frozen evidence card: two out of four assigned attempts failed at an approval boundary; the fix reduced unsafe completions from two to zero in the rerun; the sample is formative.
2. Show three stakeholder prompts:
   - CFO: approve a limited pilot with risk control.
   - Frontline operator: know when to stop and escalate.
   - Marketing audience: understand a new service promise without an unsupported performance claim.
3. Render the same facts as:
   - a six-screen approval readout;
   - a one-page runbook with stop condition; and
   - a 20-second storyboard.
4. Run a one-click video prompt that invents “100% accurate.” Pause before generation and ask students to predict the failure.
5. Repair by locking permitted claims, writing the intended audience action, adding disclosure, and using generic/consent-cleared voice.
6. Change the constraint to “no audio; accessible on a noisy shop floor.” Convert the film to caption-led stills without changing the evidence.
7. Close: “Format can change. Evidence, uncertainty, authority, and disclosure cannot.”

## Student task brief

You are not making content “about your project.” You are producing the work artifact that moves one real role decision forward.

Before using a creation tool, decide:

- Who is accountable for the next decision?
- What should they decide, do, approve, reject, remember, or test?
- What evidence must they be able to inspect?
- Which limitation must remain visible?
- Which format fits their time, workflow, and risk?
- What should remain private?

Use multimodal tools only when they improve comprehension, recall, demonstration, training, or persuasion for this stakeholder. Do not add video simply because the model can generate it.

## Misconceptions and facilitator responses

| Misconception | Response |
| --- | --- |
| “Everyone should make a video because AI video is impressive.” | The target role and stakeholder action choose the format. Film is one option, not the learning outcome. |
| “Short content does not need citations.” | Any consequential claim remains traceable even when the public asset uses concise language. |
| “More visuals mean better communication.” | Grade decision usefulness, evidence fidelity, and stakeholder fit—not decoration. |
| “The generated voice sounds like a leader, so it is persuasive.” | Unauthorized imitation is prohibited. Use generic or consent-cleared voice and disclose synthetic media. |
| “If a stakeholder remembers the message, persuasion succeeded.” | Recall without truthful evidence or appropriate action is not success. |
| “Accessibility is final-stage formatting.” | Channel and asset choices must account for captions, transcript, alt text, contrast, and non-audio use from the brief. |

## Differentiation

### Supported path

- Use the stream template, locked claim table, and supplied layout/storyboard components.
- Produce a static or recorded artifact; no custom media stack is required.
- Use pre-generated licensed assets and add direction, sequence, captions, and evidence.

### Advanced lane

- Produce two controlled variants that change only one strategic variable.
- Run a small blind response test against a pre-registered question.
- Automate claim/brand checks or generate an accessible alternate form.

Technical or aesthetic complexity does not raise the grade ceiling.

## Accessibility and inclusion

- Every video/voice artifact requires captions and transcript; an equivalent non-audio path is mandatory.
- Images and charts require alt text or an equivalent data/table description.
- Students may submit text, static screens, audio, or captioned video where the role permits; live performance is not required.
- Public display name and publication choice follow institutional consent policy.
- Do not grade accent, idiom, acting ability, design background, or production budget.
- Templates use semantic headings, readable contrast, no color-only meaning, and reduced-motion alternatives.
- Blind reviewers assess action/evidence/limitation, not taste.

## Session rubric: 70% common + 30% stream judgment

Canonical references: [course common rubric](../../course/course-1-architecture-v2.md#common-rubric-dimensions) and [stream judgment anchors](../../course/stream-coverage-matrix.md#detailed-stream-cards).

| Common criterion | Points | Demonstrated evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Stakeholder and action fit | 15 | clear decision owner, requested action, objection, and suitable channel |
| Evidence fidelity | 20 | consequential claims remain supported, qualified, and traceable across format |
| Changed-constraint control | 15 | revision handles the injected constraint without hiding risk or altering truth |
| Communication execution and accessibility | 10 | artifact is usable in the role context, concise, accessible, and not judged on taste |
| Provenance, rights, disclosure, public/private boundary | 10 | complete ledger and safe publication state |
| Stream judgment overlay | 30 | role-specific anchor in the variant table |
| **Total** | **100** |  |

Essential caps:

- unsupported consequential claim: maximum Developing until corrected;
- unauthorized identity/voice or missing consent: unsafe; publication blocked;
- missing accessible equivalent: incomplete, with repair opportunity;
- visually polished but wrong stakeholder action: does not pass;
- external media outage: no penalty if the static/storyboard evidence meets the same capability.

## Scalable review model

- **100% deterministic checks:** required sections, source links, claim IDs, disclosure, rights/consent fields, captions/transcript, alt text, duration/file/URL health.
- **100% blind peer scan:** reviewer records what they understood; they do not score aesthetics or assign grades.
- **Rubric-bound coaching:** AI may flag possible claim drift or audience mismatch with cited evidence; students may contest it.
- **Human formative review:** section facilitators resolve unsafe claims, disputes and blockers. Session 8 does not create a separate summative lesson score.
- **Summative route:** if this communication is included in the frozen recruiter proof, it is scored once by the Session 9 primary human scorer under the gradebook map; at least 10% of final recruiter packets are blind-double-scored.

The review packet leads with the communication contract, three claim links, changed constraint, artifact and peer scan. Video is not reviewed at full length when the assessed segment is identified and a transcript is provided.

## Failure recovery and fallback

| Failure | Immediate path | Learning objective preserved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Image/video/voice model unavailable | use pre-generated licensed assets, storyboard, static frames, transcript, or asset-card pitch | direction, evidence fidelity, constraint handling |
| Generation queue exceeds build window | freeze brief and sequence; use low-fidelity composite | role communication and proof |
| Claim validator disputed | open exact source and route to human review; never silently fail | evidence judgment |
| Endpoint or LMS down | work from downloadable stream template; capture local version and later attach incident ID | artifact and decision trail |
| Unsafe generated content | stop, quarantine, replace asset, document failed control | safety ownership |
| Accessibility tool fails | use supplied caption/transcript/alt-text templates and static equivalent | equal capability evidence |

## Facilitator preparation and calibration

Before class:

- rehearse the three-format demo and unsupported-claim repair;
- preflight centrally provisioned media credits, queues, file limits, and the no-media fallback;
- confirm all student claim locks draw from Session 7 evidence, not invented results;
- load stream templates and changed-constraint cards, including all Finance subvariants;
- calibrate against one evidence-rich plain artifact and one polished unsupported artifact;
- review identity, brand, rights, synthetic-media, public-data, and accessibility rules;
- verify private/public classification and publication quarantine;
- ensure same-stream or adjacent-stream reviewer routing; and
- reserve formative exception review; final scoring/double-score assignments occur on the frozen Session 9 packet.

## After-class follow-up

### Required, normally 20–30 minutes

- Resolve any failed claim, rights, disclosure, or accessibility check.
- Freeze the final Role Communication Pack and choose its public recruiter excerpt.
- Complete a three-item delayed retrieval check on format choice, claim drift, and synthetic-media disclosure.
- Write the Session 9 lead sentence: target role, consequential decision, system/result, and proof link.

### Optional extension

- Run one additional qualified stakeholder response using the LMS role card.
- Produce a controlled alternate format for a second stakeholder without adding new facts.

## Session 9 bridge

Session 9 separates presentation from proof. Students will decide what a recruiter can scan publicly, what an evaluator may inspect privately, and what must be excluded. The communication artifact becomes one entry point—not the portfolio itself.
