# Session 1 Lesson Plan — Find the Leverage

**Duration:** 120 minutes  
**Studio:** Intelligence Studio  
**Primary capability:** C1 — frame an AI opportunity with a role, decision owner, decision, metric, constraints, and acceptance tests  
**Supporting capabilities:** C8 — adapt under a changed condition; C9 — communicate and defend a recommendation  
**Canonical outcome IDs:** L01-O1–L01-O6  
**Shared mechanism:** **Decision-first opportunity framing**  
**Portfolio increment:** Work Sample Brief v1 + opportunity thesis v0 + first immutable decision-log checkpoint

## Planning assumptions

- A section has approximately 60 students with a lead instructor and a setup/support lane; facilitator count is still an external decision.
- Students completed the technology check, maturity baseline, stream selection, and a draft Work Sample Brief before class.
- The LMS has approved, returned, or replaced that draft with a curated stream challenge card. No student spends class searching for a project.
- The 20 hours are live contact time, with short asynchronous work between sessions. If Masters' Union later defines 20 hours as total effort, the async refinement below becomes optional.
- Exact model, document, repository, and LMS products are delivery configuration. This plan names capability slots only.

## Session promise

> By the end of this session, you will turn a broad role/company interest into one role-authentic, evidence-feasible AI opportunity that names a consequential decision, measurable value, a safe human boundary, and a thin build that a recruiter can inspect.

## Prerequisites

Students must arrive with:

- completed pre-course performance baseline and confidence items;
- selected career stream and target role;
- a one-page draft Work Sample Brief or an assigned fallback challenge card;
- access to the LMS workspace and course repository;
- the independent-project disclaimer acknowledged;
- no confidential, personal, credential, or proprietary data in the workspace.

No coding, AI terminology, or paid account is required.

## Instructor demo script

**Demo goal:** make the difference between an exciting AI output and a defensible opportunity visible in under seven minutes.

1. Open the generic brief: “Build an AI CEO copilot for Company X that reads everything and improves decisions.”
2. Ask the system for a weekly recommendation. Display the polished memo.
3. Ask the room to predict whether a recruiter could verify the claimed value. Collect a show of cards; do not debate yet.
4. Reveal four missing foundations one at a time:
   - no named decision or owner;
   - requires confidential “everything” access;
   - no baseline or outcome metric;
   - no authority boundary or failure behavior.
5. Switch to the qualified example: “As an operations analyst, reduce time to classify delayed-shipment exceptions for a planner while preserving manual approval for rerouting.”
6. Run one normal and one unsafe/ambiguous input. Show classification, cited evidence, confidence, and escalation.
7. Display the opportunity stack behind the run and the two acceptance tests.
8. Close with: “The second system is smaller. It is also the one a recruiter can test, challenge, and discuss.”

Do not explain prompt frameworks or tool features in this demo.

## Common student brief

### Your role

You are preparing a speculative, independent work sample for a role you could plausibly pursue. You do not work for the named company and do not possess internal access.

### Your task

Produce a one-page Work Sample Brief that completes this sentence:

> “As a **[target role]** operating in **[operator/advisor/investor mode and context]**, I will investigate/build **[thin system]** to help **[decision owner]** decide/do **[consequential decision or task]**, improving **[outcome metric]** while protecting **[guardrail]**. I can test it using **[public, licensed, course-supplied synthetic, or consented evidence as labelled, plus assigned task attempts]**. A human remains accountable for **[authority boundary]**.”

Include:

1. hiring audience and role;
2. operator/advisor/investor mode;
3. representative work context;
4. decision owner and consequential decision;
5. current friction and why AI may have leverage;
6. outcome metric and guardrail;
7. legal public, licensed, course-supplied synthetic, or explicitly consented evidence as labelled;
8. thin system with inputs and outputs;
9. human–AI allocation and stop/escalation rule;
10. two observable acceptance tests;
11. non-goals and known assumptions;
12. independent-project/public-or-synthetic-data disclosure.

### Definition of done

The brief passes all project gates, the thin task is testable in under three minutes, and a fresh reader can state what decision improves without seeing a demo.

## Injected failure

The LMS assigns one of four equivalent failures after the pre-failure brief freezes:

1. **Access loss:** the planned internal dataset or company credential is unavailable.
2. **Authority violation:** the system would make a regulated, employment, pricing, capital, or customer action autonomously.
3. **Test collapse:** the proposed user or success measure cannot be observed during the course.
4. **Metric corruption:** the metric rewards activity/vanity while ignoring the consequential outcome or guardrail.

Students first classify the failing layer—evidence, authority, testability, or value—then revise. Merely adding “human in the loop” without naming the human, decision, and trigger does not pass.

## Ownership and transfer test

Each student receives one unseen brief from a different industry but the same stream. In seven minutes they must:

1. identify the two most consequential defects;
2. reject, narrow, or reframe it;
3. write role, owner, decision, metric, guardrail, thin system, and one acceptance test;
4. state confidence and the missing evidence that could change the decision.

The response is individual, time-bounded, and cannot reuse the student's company text. This is C1 ownership evidence, not a leaderboard challenge.

## Likely misconceptions and responses

| Misconception | Evidence in student work | Facilitator response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The company is the user.” | no named person/role or decision | ask who acts differently on Monday |
| “Build a chatbot” is a problem | output/form appears before work/decision | remove the tool noun; restate the job and decision |
| Faster output is automatically business value | time saved with no use or consequence | ask what decision/outcome changes and what could worsen |
| Public information means permission to scrape/use anything | no source/terms/privacy boundary | require allow-listed source or course pack; distinguish visibility from permission |
| “Human in the loop” is a sufficient safeguard | no named authority, trigger, or action | require who reviews what, when, and with what power |
| A famous company makes a strong portfolio | broad prestigious brand, infeasible access | score role authenticity and inspectability, never prestige |
| The final app must be chosen now | premature UI/tool commitment | choose the job and evidence; prototype form is decided later |

## Differentiation and accessibility

### Scaffold lane

- sentence stems, role-task examples, metric/guardrail menu, and one worked card;
- curated fallback brief with complete public, licensed, or course-supplied synthetic evidence;
- facilitator asks one question at a time rather than rewriting the brief;
- students may submit the defence as audio, text, or captioned video.

### Advanced lane

- define a baseline and value equation with explicit assumptions;
- map two alternative human–AI allocations and reject one;
- design one adversarial acceptance test;
- receive a harder cross-stream transfer card, with no additional grade ceiling.

### Accessibility requirements

- all demo speech has a transcript and captions; all diagrams have a linear text equivalent;
- brief form is keyboard and screen-reader navigable with persistent labels;
- no meaning depends on color, drag-and-drop, or rapid spoken participation;
- timers are announced in text; approved accommodations can extend transfer time without changing the construct;
- low-bandwidth/printable packet accepts typed or handwritten evidence later uploaded by staff;
- examples avoid requiring knowledge of one consumer platform or English idiom; plain-language glossary is available.

## Failure recovery, no-network path, and setup fallback

| Failure | Immediate recovery | Evidence parity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student brief unapproved/missing | LMS or facilitator assigns a curated card in the student's stream | same canvas, failure, transfer, and rubric |
| Student device/account issue | use printed/editable local form; pair for viewing only, but each submits an individual brief and transfer | staff uploads timestamped scan/export |
| Conversational model unavailable | use pre-generated critique cards; model use is not assessed | decision and revision evidence unchanged |
| LMS unavailable | use numbered paper/local forms and sealed failure cards; facilitator logs reveal time | bulk import after restoration |
| Repository unavailable | export evidence bundle with student ID and timestamp; attach checkpoint next session | no grade penalty for infrastructure failure |
| Whole-room network loss | play cached demo; run canvas, peer attack, failure envelopes, and transfer entirely offline | deterministic checks run later; frozen paper versions retained |
| Demo fails live | switch to cached two-minute recording and screenshots of normal/unsafe runs | debrief follows same prediction prompts |

Facilitators tag infrastructure incidents separately from learner errors and may extend only the affected gate.

## Async follow-up and Session 2 bridge

**Required, target 25–30 minutes:**

1. respond to validator/facilitator feedback and submit Work Sample Brief v1.1;
2. locate two allowed primary sources and one independent comparison source that could support or challenge the opportunity thesis;
3. add each to the empty evidence ledger with title, owner, date, URL/file, source type, and expected claim—not a summary generated by AI;
4. complete a four-item delayed retrieval check.

**Optional enrichment:** interview one consented proxy practitioner/user only to refine questions. Do not represent it as company research or upload personal data.

Session 2 unlocks when the brief passes the project gate and the three-source plan is present. Students who do not unlock use the curated evidence pack for their stream and can return to their own case after repair.
