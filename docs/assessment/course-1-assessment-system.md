# Course 1 Assessment System

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 17 July 2026  
**Applies to:** 480 students, eight sections, nine career streams

## Assessment claim

The grade certifies that a student can investigate a consequential business decision, direct and inspect an AI operating prototype, repair it under a changed condition, and communicate the evidence for a target role. It does not certify unaided production or familiarity with a fixed tool list.

Artifact success and student ownership are distinct claims. A polished submission that cannot survive an unseen change cannot earn the top mastery band.

## Components and weights

| Component | Weight | Primary evidence | Ownership evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Decision evidence brief | 20% | role-authentic brief/six-screen equivalent, data, original calculation, claim ledger, counter-case | reproduce one number; defend an evaluator-selected claim |
| AI operating prototype | 30% | deployed/replayable system, input/output contract, human boundary, logs, failure handling, task-attempt revision | supervised checkpoint; hidden failure; clean-session change |
| Individual unseen transfer sprint | 25% | parallel work context, structured decisions, corrected output | completed individually in a controlled window with reduced scaffolding |
| Recruiter case page and proof pack | 15% | 60–90 second public scan, private/public proof, disclosure and live links | random proof link/claim defence; provenance and publish-gate pass |
| Structured participation and peer attack | 10% | preparation gates, three assigned attacks, evidence-specific feedback, response to feedback | system-recorded completion and quality checks; never popularity or airtime |

Pre/post maturity growth is reported separately and is not awarded points for “improvement.”

The component evidence, aggregation, scorers, freezes, and retry rules are canonical in the [Course 1 Gradebook Map](course-1-gradebook-map.md). Lesson rubrics are feedback/checkpoint instruments unless that map explicitly names the frozen evidence as summative.

## Mastery bands

| Band | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Emerging | restates the brief, accepts AI output, or cannot produce replayable evidence |
| Developing | completes a scaffolded happy path but misses important evidence, reliability, or role-judgment requirements |
| Demonstrated | produces a useful role-authentic object, passes known checks, and explains the main mechanism and limits |
| Verified | passes a hidden failure or unseen change, repairs by layer, and supplies inspectable ownership evidence |
| Transferable | applies the capability to a different work context with reduced assistance and defends the trade-off |

Portfolio components can earn up to Verified. Transferable is awarded through the individual sprint and reflected on the capability transcript.

## Assessment gates

1. **Work Sample Brief:** role, mode, context, decision, owner, work product, evidence tier, prototype form, success criterion, safety boundary.
2. **Evidence sufficiency:** primary/authoritative sources where available, raw snapshot, data dictionary, pre-data thesis, counter-hypothesis, metric.
3. **Supervised ownership checkpoint:** prediction, one substantive mechanism change, replayable output, automatic timestamp, supplied/generated/adapted/authored labels.
4. **Publication safety:** secrets, PII, identity, brand, licensing, employment/finance/outbound-action risks.
5. **Reliability:** pathway-specific injected failure and safe stop/escalation.
6. **Task-attempt exchange:** three to five assigned attempts, one observed friction, one evidence-led change, one rerun.
7. **Individual transfer:** unseen context/data/policy changed in a clean session.
8. **Recruiter publication:** liveness, disclosure, licenses, consent, private/public split, independent-project label.

Failure at a publication gate blocks public release; it does not erase private learning evidence. Serious fabrication or unsafe conduct follows Masters' Union academic-integrity policy.

## Evaluation division of labour

| Layer | Coverage | Appropriate judgments |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Deterministic validators | 100% | files/fields, schema, URL liveness, citations, formula/test results, required logs/events, secrets/PII, disclosure labels |
| Trained human primary scorer | 100% of judgment-bearing evidence in all four major components and the individual transfer sprint | 70% common capability judgment, 30% stream overlay, ownership defence, criterion notes and final criterion score |
| Individual transfer task | 100% | equivalent common capability under an unseen change; deterministic evidence plus trained-human rubric scoring |
| Assigned peer attack | 100% at selected gates | observable task behavior and failure evidence; peer does not assign the artifact grade |
| Model-assisted review | 100% evidence routing/coaching; no final criterion score | criterion-level observations citing submitted evidence and confidence |
| Human moderation | all flags, borderlines, safety cases, appeals and detected drift | adjudication, ownership defence, disputed feedback and incident relief |
| Cross-section double score | >=10% stratified sample of every major graded component | reliability, equivalence and drift detection |

Before grading, every scorer completes calibration on five anchors spanning performance bands and the relevant stream overlays. Material disagreement is reconciled. Section distributions and criterion-level drift are reviewed without forcing identical curves. A score is not released without a named human scorer, frozen evidence packet, rubric version and criterion note.

## Integrity and anti-gaming controls

| Gaming path | Control |
| --- | --- |
| One-prompt delegation | pre-tool decision, structured intermediate choices, supervised checkpoint, failure diagnosis |
| Template laundering | templates allowed; grade changed contract, hidden adaptation, clean-session transfer |
| Copied/outsourced prototype | individual checkpoint, unseen change, random component defence, similarity flag for human review |
| Fabricated company data | source/synthetic labels, source liveness, instructor-controlled assessed data |
| Fake users | assigned task-attempt exchange with role/input cards and observable events |
| Cosmetic Git history | automatic checkpoint IDs; commit count never scored |
| Known-test overfit | parallel hidden variants and changed requirement |
| Invented reflection | reflection must point to an immutable execution, test, issue, or evidence item |
| AI grader agreeing with AI output | mechanical checks, calibrated peer attack, trained-human primary score, moderation and appeal route |
| Unsafe publication | hard publish gate for secrets, PII, consent, identity, licenses, claims, and affiliation |

## Participation

The 10% participation score is composed of observable actions:

- 2% prerequisite/entry gates completed on time;
- 4% three assigned peer attacks completed with observable evidence and a specific friction/boundary note; verified safe decline, author-endpoint failure or platform incident receives a replacement or incident-equivalent receipt without penalty;
- 2% documented response to received evidence;
- 2% professional operating conduct, including safe data/tool use and honest disclosure.

Attendance, speaking frequency, leaderboard position, charisma, accent, English flourish, visual taste, and speed do not earn points by themselves.

## Appeals and incident relief

- Students may challenge model feedback and identify the criterion/evidence in dispute.
- Platform incidents are logged separately from learner failures; verified incidents trigger retry or equivalent evidence routes.
- Accessibility accommodations preserve the outcome while changing timing or modality where live speaking/voice is not the assessed capability.
- A facilitator cannot silently change a score without a criterion note and evidence reference.
